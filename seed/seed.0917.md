# Scenario seeds for erl-entrance (2026-09-17, revised 2026-09-24)

Two hundred customer-service scenario definitions to be used as seeds for erlbench environment synthesis. Each entry is one company (or public body) with a real, high-volume support desk in the contemporary United States, chosen so that a single contact can change something material for the customer — money owed or refunded, housing, health care, income, coverage, mobility or legal standing — and so that the business process is genuinely multi-step (eligibility gates, deadlines, fees and prorations, claims, reversals, cross-product interactions).

## How to use

- The identifier at the start of an entry is the one-line scenario definition expected by `skills/erl-entrance` (industry + company form, which is also the directory name under `env/`). Run, from the repository root:

  ```
  /erl-entrance <identifier> N=<number of difficulties> M=<tasks per difficulty>
  ```

  The sentence after the identifier states the intent (company form, who calls, which tiered contracts they hold, which events the desk books). It may be pasted along with the identifier; the blueprint skill still performs its own ontology-fit check and industry enumeration, and nothing in the sentence fixes tier names, numbers or policies.
- Entries already synthesized under `env/` keep their numbers and their original wording (001-006, 013, 015). Every other number was reassigned in the 2026-09-24 revision and the free numbers are filled in priority order: the earliest free numbers carry the desks with the largest product of national contact volume and money at stake, the later numbers carry business-to-business desks and lower-frequency but still high-stakes consumer desks. Taking the free entries in index order therefore takes the most consequential desks first. The sector label on each line is bookkeeping only.
- `env_0000_online_travel_agency` already exists in the repository and is not in this list. Identifiers are unique, snake_case, at most 45 characters after the `env_NNNN_` prefix that carries the entry number, and none repeats a scenario present under `env/`.

## Selection criteria

Every seed had to pass four tests, and the list is weighted by the first two rather than spread evenly across sectors:

1. **High stakes per contact.** What the desk decides in one call moves real money or a real entitlement for the caller: a mortgage or rent balance, a claim or coverage decision, a benefit payment, a disconnection, a repossession, a tuition hold, a medical bill. Desks whose typical contact is worth a few dollars (streaming, fast food, movie tickets, newspapers, satellite radio, meal kits) were removed even though their call volume is high.
2. **High frequency in American life.** The company form runs a national or state-scale contact center whose volume shows up in the public record: CFPB complaint categories (credit reporting, debt collection, cards, mortgages, student and auto loans, money transfers), FCC and state utility commission complaints (wireless, broadband, energy), hospital and pharmacy billing volumes, airline and hotel loyalty desks, marketplace and big-box retail, and the largest public desks (motor vehicles, tax, retirement and disability benefits, unemployment, food and medical assistance, veterans, public housing). Sectors were sized to that record: finance, insurance, health care, utilities and telecom, housing and vehicles carry most of the list; recreation, personal retail and specialty B2B carry little.
3. **Real.** The company form exists at scale in the United States today under exactly this description, the tiering is the industry's own tiering (plan tiers, coverage levels, rate classes, fare brands, care levels, program eligibility) and the events are what the desk actually books; nothing was invented to satisfy the ontology. Niche or affluent-recreation desks (marinas, country clubs, campgrounds, ski-club style memberships) and thin daily services dressed up with tiered plans (dry cleaning, house cleaning, office coffee, water softeners) were removed for this reason.
4. **Ontology fit.** Customers hold tiered contracts (six or more variants per product line, seven or more product lines) that continuously produce booked events (charges, payments, usage, claims, renewals, transitions, reversals), as `skills/erl-scenario-blueprint` requires.

Business-to-business desks are kept only where the business's own money is at stake on every contact and the desk is high volume (payments, payroll, freight, distribution, leasing, small-business software and lending). Roughly a third of the list serves both consumers and businesses from one desk.

Excluded on purpose: firearms, gambling and lotteries, adult content, tobacco, cannabis and alcohol-centric businesses, payday lending and cryptocurrency, and anything whose demand would have to be fabricated.
