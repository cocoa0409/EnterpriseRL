# Scenario seeds for erl-entrance (2026-09-17)

Two hundred customer-service scenario definitions to be used as seeds for erlbench environment synthesis. Each entry is one company (or public body) with a real, high-volume support desk in the contemporary United States, chosen so that the money at stake per contact is material and the business process is genuinely multi-step (eligibility gates, deadlines, fees and prorations, claims, reversals, cross-product interactions).

## How to use

- The identifier at the start of an entry is the one-line scenario definition expected by `skills/erl-entrance` (industry + company form, which is also the directory name under `env/`). Run, from the repository root:

  ```
  /erl-entrance <identifier> N=<number of difficulties> M=<tasks per difficulty>
  ```

  The sentence after the identifier states the intent (company form, who calls, which tiered contracts they hold, which events the desk books). It may be pasted along with the identifier; the blueprint skill still performs its own ontology-fit check and industry enumeration, and nothing in the sentence fixes tier names, numbers or policies.
- The list is uniformly shuffled with `random.Random(20260917)` (Python). Taking entries 001..k in order is a uniform random sample of size k, and any contiguous block is a random sample. The sector label on each line is bookkeeping only.
- Identifiers are unique, snake_case, at most 45 characters, and none repeats a scenario already synthesized in this repository (regional_retail_bank, online_travel_agency, cloud_platform_provider, commercial_insurance_group, global_container_shipping_line, home_appliance_warranty_provider, regional_pet_insurer, metro_gym_chain).

## Selection criteria

Every seed was checked against the ontology the blueprint skill enforces: customers hold tiered contracts (the industry's own tiering, wide enough for six or more variants per product line and seven or more product lines) and those contracts continuously produce booked events (charges, payments, usage, claims, renewals, transitions, reversals). Beyond that, each seed had to satisfy all of: a mainstream, present-day American need (household or business); high contact frequency at the desk; material economic value per customer; and process complexity that comes from real policy rather than invented mechanisms. B2C and B2B are both represented; roughly two fifths of the list sits in the daily-life spending core (housing, utilities, home services, vehicles, travel, food, apparel) and the rest spreads evenly across finance, insurance, healthcare, education, work, logistics, technology, recreation, personal care, agriculture and public services.

Excluded on purpose: firearms, gambling and lotteries, adult content, tobacco, cannabis and alcohol-centric businesses, payday lending and cryptocurrency, and anything whose demand would have to be fabricated.
