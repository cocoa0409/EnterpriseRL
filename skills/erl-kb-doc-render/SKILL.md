name: erl-kb-doc-render
description: Render a product charter losslessly into a genre-rich internal support KB, enriching each document with same-source equivalent restatements and provable logical derivations while keeping normative semantics in strict two-way correspondence with the structured source, and adding no calculation, condition or procedure from outside it. What the solver is meant to work out — the dependencies between problems (a prerequisite: another problem that must be resolved before this one can be handled) and the course of a handling procedure (a workflow) — is never written as an instruction, an entry condition, a stated order or a runbook: it is carried implicitly, as what follows from definitions and principles written in paraphrased natural prose that copies no charter sentence and repeats no phrasing across documents, under titles that name general subjects rather than any task's situation, and it is never aggregated into a dependency table or a handling route. Two kinds of input: (1) env/{scenario}/tasks/{difficulty}/{product}.extended.yaml plus the task_tools.py beside it (an instantiated category, rendering base+extended+tools+workflow documents); (2) env/{scenario}/blueprint/products/{product}.yaml (an unclaimed category, rendering base only). Output env/{scenario}/docs/{product}/{topic}_{uuid8}.json. Use whenever a yaml charter is to be split into retrievable policy documents, a KB corpus generated, or documents re-rendered after a yaml change. Run once per product, from the enterprise_rl repository root.

# erl-kb-doc-render (once per product)

You are a policy-manual editor. The input is one product's charter yaml (the type is determined automatically: if `env/<scenario>/tasks/*/<product>.extended.yaml` exists it is the **extended type**, read together with the `task_tools.py` beside it; otherwise `env/<scenario>/blueprint/products/<product>.yaml` is taken as the **base type**). The output is a set of KB documents landing in `env/<scenario>/docs/<product>/`.

**The yaml is the only authoring source of policy truth, and the published docs are the only policy surface the agent can see.** This skill is the rendering step from "structured truth -> retrievable corpus": after any change to the yaml, the whole product must be re-rendered, pass the gates in staging, and replace the official directory wholesale; never hand-patch the official directory incrementally — a patch inevitably drifts the basis. There is neither a floor nor a ceiling on the number of documents: split only along the semantic boundaries that genuinely exist in the source. If the structured source is right but the published text leads a careful agent to a different answer, or the published text makes the agent carry an obligation the source does not have, the rendering has failed; yaml/compute the agent cannot see is never a defence for an ambiguity or an added rule in a document. The rendering has equally failed if the published text states outright — as an instruction, an entry condition, a precedence, an ordered procedure or a warning against a wrong reading — a dependency, an order or a way of resolving a situation that the task design leaves for the solver to work out: the KB is the context the solver reasons from, never the solution it follows. Correctness and implicitness are held together: the corpus must entail exactly what the source entails, and state none of what the solver is meant to derive.

### The correctness boundary (never to be crossed)

This skill is responsible only for rendering the structured policy losslessly into documents the agent can see. Correctness is verified by three relationships: structured policy <-> GT, structured policy -> complete coverage in the published documents, and published documents -> reverse traceability with nothing added. The render gates can check a document's structure, coverage, references and the appearance of numbers, but they may not erect business rules, calculate on the agent's behalf, or judge eligibility, and they may not treat tool-call/refusal behaviour, retrieval ranking, top-k hits or a surface-request smoke test as a correctness backstop. A tool description states only the calling contract `task_tools.py` can prove; the policy's decisions about eligibility, objects, timing and calculation come only from the yaml, and are made by the solving agent itself. GT, a task's `user_scenario`, `design_rationale`, audit conclusions and existing docs are never a source of fact for back-filling policy.

## Values (whenever a step conflicts with them, the values win)

### 0. Strict semantic bijection, carried implicitly: semantics do not proliferate, expression may be rich, and nothing the solver must work out is pre-solved (highest priority)
The KB and the structured policy form a strict bijection at the level of **normative semantic atoms**; this does not require a literal one-to-one match between source sentences and natural-language sentences. One policy may be split across several documents along real semantic boundaries; every source semantic atom still has exactly one **bearing document**, but within that document it may form a `presentation bundle`: one formal clause as the normative baseline, then tables, Q&A, cases or diagrams restating it equivalently or displaying its necessary consequences. Several presentation units are still the same policy semantics, not a new policy, so long as they change no executable condition, action, parameter, ordering or result:

- **Forward completeness**: every piece of semantics that can change a judgement or an action — in the variants parameters, in base rule/eligibility/temporal basis, in extended trigger/subject/rule/compute/cross-category/**prerequisite**, and in workflow scenario/steps/owner/order rationale/**prerequisite** — must appear losslessly and be assigned to exactly one bearing document; a source atom may never be dropped on the grounds that the expression is already rich. `prerequisite` is load-bearing semantics, not metadata: it holds that "that must be settled before this can be handled", and a corpus that does not entail it leaves the solver unable ever to discover that it must be done — one of the most serious forms of omission there is. Carrying it losslessly means the corpus entails it at the same strength; it never means stating it (see the implicit-carriage bullets below). The same holds for a workflow's order and for the reasons it rests on.
- **Reverse: nothing added**: every calculation, eligibility, authorisation, evidence requirement, step, ordering, wait, review, notification, exception handling and business effect in the prose — every "must / must not / only if ... then / first ... then / should ask or confirm / what to do on failure" — must have an explicit source locator. If the source does not have it, it may not be written; common sense, real company practice, the writer feeling it is safer, and existing documents already saying so are none of them sources.
- **Strength and boundaries unchanged**: never turn "may" into "must", never turn a tool parameter into a pre-action eligibility, and never add customer confirmations, delivery dates, read-backs, post-action reviews, duplicate guards, corrections, approvals, waits or handoff steps — unless the selected yaml or `task_tools.py` contract contains them verbatim or equivalently. Nor may you delete an actor, an object correlation, an evidentiary authority, a point in time, an equality boundary, a calculation base or an ordering relation that the source has.
- **One-to-many expression is allowed within a document**: the same atom may be written first as a formal clause, then as a table, then reworded as a general question the rule answers (never a particular customer's circumstances or request), or split into "condition - judgement - result". These expressions may repeat the numbers and conclusions, each time in fresh wording, but must point at the same source locator, and must not be split into another document to form a second home. Across documents, replicating a quantitative basis or a complete rule remains forbidden.
- **Only closed logical derivation is allowed**: from the source atoms already assigned to this document you may deterministically generate numeric worked examples, boundary cases that just do and just do not qualify, truth tables of condition combinations, positive and negative cases, input-judgement-output diagrams and FAQs. A derived conclusion must be a logical consequence of the source rule; it may not call on a policy fragment outside this document to complete the conclusion, and the author's chosen example inputs may not be passed off as company facts or new thresholds. A derivation illustrates this document's own rule only: it never walks across problems, never demonstrates a dependency or an order between problems, and never contrasts a wrong reading with a correct one or names a mistake to avoid.
- **Derivation does not assume a closed world**: where the source says only "when A holds, B follows", you may not unilaterally write "if A does not hold, B definitely does not follow", turn a sufficient condition into a necessary one, or treat the listed branches as an exhaustive set. Only where the source explicitly gives a complete boundary ("only / otherwise / if any is unmet / if and only if") may you fill in the corresponding counter-case, the remaining rows of a truth table, or a negative FAQ answer; combinations the source does not cover must be omitted, never completed into "refuse, leave unchanged, or hand off".
- **Derived examples are isolated from tasks**: a hypothetical case is constructed only from the source schema/enums and this document's rules; it must never read or restate a task's `user_scenario`, initial state, evaluation criteria, GT, actual tool-run results or internal UUIDs. Examples exist to explain general policy, and must not hint at any task's target object, complete input or execution chain.
- **Editorial content must be decision-neutral**: titles, introductions, section notes, table headers, captions and connective sentences are allowed; after deleting them, the set of permitted actions, forbidden actions, parameters, ordering and results the agent derives for any business fact must be completely unchanged. Never pad with corporate culture, service philosophy, industry common sense, invented "frequently asked" questions or synonymous filler that carries no retrieval value.
- **Expression may expand, the normative may not**: never invent a new condition, action or promise under the cover of a "scope of application, handling note, exception, customer communication, risk warning, frequently asked question" section. Richness comes from presenting existing semantics in several forms and from provable inference, never from policy outside the source.
- **Tools and procedures have separate sources**: an invocation document's capability, parameters, owner, successful return and explicit errors come only from `task_tools.py`; a workflow document's situation, parts, owners and reasons come only from the yaml workflow. That a tool accepts a parameter does not mean policy requires asking the customer for it or confirming it; that a tool records something under the current date does not mean the customer must supply a service date.
- **The KB is context to reason from, never a solution to follow (co-equal with the bijection)**: the dependencies between problems, the order in which actions across problems must happen, the course of a workflow, and the way a situation is resolved are what the task design leaves for the solver to work out. They are carried only by entailment — as what necessarily follows when a careful reader applies the definitions and principles the documents state to the facts the records show — and never as a statement a reader could follow without reasoning: no entry condition, no gating clause that makes one problem wait on another, no precedence label, no "before / first / only after / until / not yet" directive about another problem's action, no ordered step list or step number, no sequence of tool names, no checklist, no contrast between a wrong reading and a correct one, no named mistake, no reminder. A sentence, heading, table or example from which a dependency, an order or a procedure can be read off directly is a rendering failure even when it is semantically correct. Implicit carriage changes only the form in which these atoms are borne, never whether they are borne.
- **How a dependency is carried**: the blocked problem's own documents state, in abstract and principled terms, what its governed result is and what it may and may not rest on — what an amount includes and excludes, what must be true of an object or of the inputs for an effect or a figure to be correct, which kinds of item the result must never carry. The depended-on problem's own documents state, independently and in their own terms, when that problem's action applies and what it changes. Neither side mentions the other, names the other's action or tool, or states an order; the dependency exists only as the consequence a reader draws by applying both to the facts. The strength is preserved exactly: a hard gate in the source must follow as a hard gate (the governed result cannot be correct while the condition is unmet), a sufficient condition must not become a necessary one, and a principle must never be so vague that it no longer entails the gate.
- **Findable from the facts, never from the dependent's phrasing**: the depended-on documents must be reachable by a solver who has looked at the records — they use the plain business words by which the records describe the relevant items (the kinds of entries, states and events the solver sees), so that reasoning about what the records show leads to a search that finds them. They never borrow the blocked side's phrasing as a bridge, and no document points to another.
- **Paraphrase; never copy, never template**: every sentence is the author's own wording. No sentence or long run of words is copied from the charter (rules, conditions, eligibility, rationales, scenario text, effects, descriptions), and the same idea is never phrased the same way in two documents: openings, invocation sentences, section scaffolds and recurring explanations are each worded afresh, with synonyms and varied syntax, so that locating a policy takes understanding rather than string matching. Paraphrase changes wording, never semantics: every restatement must stay exactly equivalent to its source atom, with the same actor, object, base, boundary, strength and order of calculation. Identifiers the solver must reproduce exactly — tool names, their parameter names and enum values, any fixed phrase a tool accepts, and the product's official display name — are never paraphrased; they keep their exact form where they are homed, written in backticks when they sit inside prose.
- **General titles and openings**: a title names the product and a general subject at the charter's own level of generality; it never restates a customer's request, never describes the particular combination of circumstances some task presents, never announces a procedure for a specific situation, and never contains words that signal a dependency, a sequence or a remedy. An opening makes the product and the general subject plain and never opens with a scenario. Each document must read as a standing page of the company's knowledge base, not as a reference written to match one case.
- **Precise without pre-solving**: implicitness never licenses ambiguity. Every rule must still admit exactly one natural reading — its bases, groupings, caps, rounding points, time boundaries and authority boundaries pinned down — but that precision comes from exact definitions of what a quantity or a state is, never from telling the reader what to do first or warning them away from a wrong reading.

Every `semantic_obligation` in the authoring ledger must record a precise `source_locator`, a unique `target_doc` and a `canonical_section`; and every presentation unit must record its type (`canonical | equivalent | derived | editorial`), its section and its source. `canonical` and `equivalent` record one or more precise source locators; `derived` records the source locators it depends on, the author's chosen hypothetical inputs and a re-computable derivation; `editorial` must be declared non-normative. A normative sentence with no source or no proof of derivation is deleted, never retro-fitted into the yaml afterwards to accommodate the document.

### 1. The register of a real support knowledge base: one topic per document, varied genres, length from same-source expansion
The readers are front-line support agents, and the artifact is **a real company's internal support knowledge base**, which may carry policy clauses, quick references, Q&A, cases, decision tables, tool references and descriptive accounts of how its procedures hang together all at once. Each document answers only the bases of judgement, handling actions, procedures or tool contracts the structured source has already defined for its topic; where the source defines nothing, it answers nothing. On wording and form:
- **Formal register**: complete clause-style sentences and formal terms of address ("the customer", "the company", "that number"), with no colloquialism. Formal does not mean adding cross-document navigation: a hub, a factor and a meta_rule may none of them write "for details see / see also / continue reading"; each document states only business rules the source already has. Write a scope, a condition of judgement, an exception or a handling note only where the source genuinely supplies that semantics; section completeness never overrides Value 0.
- **Wording carries the scenario's own flavour**: the corpus must read as this industry's knowledge base, not a generic template with the nouns swapped. Draw the terms of address, the names of business objects, roles, channels, units, statuses and documents from the scenario's own charter (display names, workflow owners, enum wording) — its vocabulary, never its sentences — and frame worked examples and FAQ questions around general situations this business plausibly meets, never around the particular circumstances of a task. Flavour lives in vocabulary and framing only: never import an industry fact, trade custom, regulator or brand the source does not carry, and where a colourful scenario term would blur a boundary the source pins down, the source's precision wins.
- **Genre follows the shape of the rule**: the whole corpus must not collapse into one "scope - basis - exception" template. A single-value or per-tier parameter is best as a quick-reference card, a Q&A or a comparison table; eligibility conditions as a checklist or decision table; if/else as branching Q&A or a truth table; thresholds and time windows as boundary cases; amounts and rates as a calculation guide; priority as an exact statement of which rule governs; object binding and deduplication as an input-judgement-output diagram; a workflow as a descriptive account of its purpose, its parties and what each part relies on — never a runbook; a tool contract as an operations reference. Genre changes how it is read, never the policy topology.
- **Establish the normative baseline first, then expand vertically**: the prose must contain one formal clause that can independently restore the source semantics; then choose the modules suited to this document from "equivalent restatement" and "logical derivation". So long as the shape of the source supports it, use at least one equivalent restatement and one logical derivation; a complex branch, calculation or time rule must never be published as a single compressed clause. Where the source genuinely does not support a kind of derivation, omit it; never manufacture material to complete a set of sections.
- **Equivalent restatement modules**: tabulate the same conditions and results, break a long sentence into condition/judgement/action, re-explain it as the question a customer might ask, or restate the same conclusion at a different granularity. A restatement must preserve the actor, the object, the authoritative source, the time boundary, the acting base, the priority and the strength.
- **Logical derivation modules**: publish numeric worked examples, boundary cases on both sides, condition truth tables, positive/negative cases, input-judgement-output diagrams and FAQs, each about this document's own rule only. The title or prose of an example must make "hypothetical/example" explicit, so an example value never looks like a new fixed rate, term, status or customer fact; an FAQ's question may be editorially written, but its answer may only restate or deduce from this document's source atoms. Without ticket data or statistics to prove "frequent", call the section "basis Q&A / points of judgement", and never claim that a question or misunderstanding is common.
- **Expand length deliberately, with no mechanical word-count gate**: a simple quick reference usually runs about 250-500 characters, a conditional or branching policy about 450-900, a calculation/time rule about 700-1400, a tool reference about 700-1200, and a workflow SOP about 1000-2200; these are editorial targets, not permission to add content. Below the range, first check whether a suitable equivalent or derived module was omitted; above it, check for repeated filler or overreach. Never pad by copying definitions across documents or by inventing exception handling, review actions or customer-communication steps.
- **Layout serves the genre**: use `##`/`###`, numbered clauses, tables, block quotes, FAQ questions, or input-judgement-output blocks within a single rule as the content warrants; one document need not use every form. Per-tier values and condition matrices suit tables, and the structure of a single calculation may be numbered; the order between problems and the course of a procedure are never numbered, tabulated or listed, and no layout contrasts a wrong reading with a correct one.
- **Every calculation relationship is stated in words**: multiplied by, divided by, less, total, the higher of, prorated by the remaining proportion — and **arithmetic symbols must never appear** (=, x, /, + and the like); writing it as an equation is an engineering view, not policy language.
- **Putting it in words must not compress the relationships**: the object acted on, the original/intermediate base, the ordering and grouping, the point at which a cap/floor applies and the point of rounding — everything the source rule pins down must be preserved in the factor/meta_rule that bears it. In particular, never compress "the discount rates are combined first and act once on the original base fee, the two never acting successively" into "the discounts are applied in turn", and never write successive action as merely "then discounted further" while omitting that the second acts on the amount after the first. This skill never re-chooses the algorithm; it translates the source rule losslessly.
- Internal codes **must never appear** — table names, field names, event types, parameter keys, policy/workflow ids, the calculation DSL, row ids. Translate them all into business language (the call balance, the billing period, months in service, registering an intake, awarding points). Never write implementation-facing phrasing such as "writes to / lands in the table / field"; write only the business effect. The one code form allowed is **a tool name, and that tool's own parameter names and enum values** (only inside its invocation document — see Value 4).

### 2. Scatter in an orderly way: policy entry points and independent fragments
Scattering is not shredding; it is **layering by semantic factor**, so the agent must discover and assemble the calculation policy across the corpus itself; no document may serve as a solution index or supply a "you have now read everything" stopping condition:

- **Each complex calculation policy (an extended policy) -> 1 hub + several independent policy fragments.** The hub carries only the completeness entry point that the policy exists: the applicable business situation the source has defined, the result it governs, the handling action or observable effect, and the calling instructions for the corresponding tool (when that tool is exclusive). The hub **must contain no factor name, factor count, component inventory, calculation decomposition, composition order, cross-category hint, satellite/meta-rule navigation, or any hint about "which other documents must still be read"**; and it must set no stopping signal such as "once you have read what this document lists, the assessment is complete". It tells the agent "such a policy, or such an entry point, exists", and never tells them what the policy is made of. Its applicable situation is stated at a general level, never as the combination of circumstances a task presents, and it carries nothing about another problem except, where it is a prerequisite's principle home, the abstract principle Value 0 allows.
- **Each factor document covers exactly one independent policy fragment.** It states only the applicable product, business situation, basis of judgement, mode of effect, quantitative rule and non-application boundary the source has defined; it never calls itself "the Nth factor", never mentions the hub, the other factors, the total factor count or what remains to be read. The agent can find it only from the customer's situation, DB facts and natural search terms; having found one factor, they still cannot know how many remain undiscovered.
- **Vertical expansion does not change horizontal boundaries**: a factor, meta_rule, facet, policy, tool and workflow document may each use several genres inside their own bearing document, but a case, FAQ, truth table or diagram may use only the source atoms already assigned to that document. Never use a "complete case" to aggregate other fragments, list the whole policy's components, or reassemble discrete policies into a solution index.
- **A composition meta-rule stands as its own document**: take the highest / do not stack / multiply rather than add / cap at the lower / priority overrides — each such "how several components combine" rule is its own document, never merged into any factor, and never navigated to from a hub. Missing a meta-rule means adding all the components together and getting it wrong, which is the sharpest source of discreteness there is.
- **Each cross-category condition is its own document**: for a reduction/uplift/waiver arising from holding another product, state "what is held, in what state, and what effect it has on which value of this product".
- **Split the variants tiers by facet**: one document covers one facet (pricing, data allowance, contract and early termination, points entitlements, entry thresholds, suspension and resumption ...), and within it a table covers that facet's parameter across **every tier** (the table is self-sufficient and leaves no tier gap). Never write "one big document per tier".
- **Base policies get one document each** (or are split further by basis); lifecycle states and account meanings are woven into the relevant documents in business language rather than getting their own; binding/schema/events/measures are internal implementation and never become documents.

### 3. Complete carriage: split into many x complete multi-factor coverage x complete span coverage
Scattering must never drop anything. **Every block** of the input yaml must be carried by the corpus, and missing one is a rendering failure:

- **Complete span coverage**: every parameter key of variants, every base policy, every extended policy (a hub with no index + independent factor/meta_rule), every task tool, every workflow — each has a document carrying it, with none left unrendered. Splitting into many means "breaking one block into several documents", not "dropping a few blocks along the way".
- **Complete carriage of eligibility boundaries**: every `eligibility` of every base policy must be written out in that policy's document with its original meaning, including the applicable event, the pre-action condition, the authority boundary between system records and customer statements, and the business-object correlation. A time gate must make explicit the business event the clock starts from, the verifiable record, the day/month counting algorithm for `anchor + duration`, whether the comparison is not-earlier-than or not-later-than, whether equality at the boundary satisfies it, and the source's explicit handling when it is unmet; "the customer gives notice of this cancellation" may not be generalised into "the contract has existed for some time", and calendar days may not be rewritten as full 24-hour periods. This content may be translated into business language, but it may not be split away, re-anchored, or have handling added to it.
- **Complete multi-factor coverage, with the proof of completeness hidden from the agent**: neither a hub's prose nor its meta declares the factor list. At authoring time, a factor/meta_rule declares in its runtime-ignored meta, via `meta.covers`, which source policy compute bindings it carries; the gate then requires, back from the yaml, that every binding (including `result`) is claimed by **exactly one** independent fragment, with none missing, none duplicated, and none claiming a binding that does not exist. A meta_rule must claim at least one composite binding; a factor carrying a constant basis, an eligibility condition or a cross-category branch that has no binding of its own may keep `covers` empty, but may never fabricate a binding. The number of factors is set only by the real policy fragments that can stand alone semantically in the source; there is no floor such as "at least three documents" divorced from source semantics, and rules may never be duplicated, diluted or invented to satisfy a topology or a document count. Each `meta.factor` is unique within one policy.
- **Quantitative completeness with a single documentary home**: the agent can reconstruct every calculation from docs + DB alone — every quantitative decision value of a policy (a fee, a rate, a threshold, a cap, a number of days, a multiplier) must belong to exactly one bearing document, and no other document may restate it or navigate to it. Within that bearing document the value may recur in the formal clause, a table, an example and an FAQ; those occurrences are all governed by the same `carries` and source locator. Amounts, dates or boolean combinations an example chooses for itself are explicitly labelled hypothetical inputs, are not policy quantitative keys, and may never be fixed across documents or disguised as business parameters.
- **Prerequisites are carried one hop at a time by entailment — never stated, never aggregated**: the semantics of every `prerequisite` in the source must be entailed, at the same strength, by the corpus — by the principle the blocked problem's own bearing document states about its governed result (Value 0), completed by the depended-on problem's own rules and by the facts — while the condition itself is never written: not verbatim, not paraphrased as a condition, and not as a general clause of the form "before this is handled, that must already hold; where it does not, it is not handled". **Each principle serves only its own hop**: never, in any document, aggregate the whole A->B->C chain, or all of one problem's upstream prerequisites, into a dependency table, a prerequisite checklist, a handling-order overview or a "complete handling route"; nor create an index document, a navigation document, or any document that strings several problems into a procedure. The chain is what the solver should work out hop by hop; a stated hop, and still more an aggregated table, demotes it to following a checklist. Each document says only what its own subject is and what it may rest on, leaving the reader to discover what else must therefore be settled and how.
- **A prerequisite is never written as a fact of this task**: a document states only general principles, never concrete world state such as "this customer currently owes X and therefore cannot be handled" — that is a DB fact, for the solver to look up. Writing some task's opening state into a policy document both leaks the task and adds policy.
- **The action that resolves a prerequisite is never written into the blocked problem**: `resolved_by` points at **another problem's** handling action, whose description belongs only to that problem's own documents. The blocked problem's documents never mention that other problem, its action or its tool, never append guidance such as "if unmet, first handle Y", and never state an order — that would weld two independent problems into one procedure and hand the solver the dependency. Likewise the source's `effect` ("once resolved, this gate holds") is carried only as what the definitions imply, never as an operating instruction.
- **Semantically independent, with the product category named in the prose**: each document makes plain early on **which product category** it covers (naming it with the official display_name from the charter) and its general subject, without depending on the reader having read another document and without an opening template shared with other documents. The whole scenario's corpus merges into one retrieval pool, where several product categories each have their own late fees, penalties and discounts — a document whose prose never names its product is a source of ambiguous reference in that merged pool; the category prefix in the document id does not count, and **the prose is the policy body** (the gate hard-checks that each document's content contains the official product name).

### 4. Writing tools and workflow documents
- **Each task tool has exactly one invocation document**, containing at least the following four blocks and **expanded parameter by parameter**: (1) **situation of use and capability** — only the business effect the function implementation or docstring can prove, described at the level of the capability, never expanded into policy eligibility for the correct handling action, never framed as the circumstances of a task or the moment in a procedure at which it is used, and never mentioning upstream or downstream procedure; (2) **how it is invoked** — the tool name verbatim, and, in this document's own words (never a template sentence shared across tool documents), that an agent-side tool is unlocked and then called, or that a customer's own tool is unlocked and handed over to be run by the customer themselves — the word "unlock" must appear, and "confirm" appears only where the source explicitly requires it; (3) **parameters** — list each parameter name, the business meaning the source can prove, the format or precision basis, and the complete enum set; the existence of a parameter proves only that the value is needed at call time, and never automatically creates a step in which "the agent must ask for, read back or obtain the customer's confirmation of" it; (4) **return** — only the successful receipt and the explicit error contract the source can prove. Beyond the four blocks you may add a parameter quick-reference table, equivalent Q&A and a hypothetical input-return illustration, but they still carry only the same tool contract. Where the source defines no error, review, correction or duplicate-guard behaviour, none may be added. A parameter's meaning states only "what it is and in what format", and **never the logic by which the value is calculated**.
- **Where a calculating tool's invocation document lands is decided by its reference count, not by the author** (one tool may be referenced as `target_tool` by several extended policies, which is very common in real products — the same "register an assessment" action serving several kinds of accounting):
  - **Exclusive** (exactly one policy names it as `target_tool`): **merge** — the invocation document IS that policy's hub document, the hub sets `meta.tool`, and it is expanded parameter by parameter; it explains only what each parameter is, its format and its complete enum set, gives no hint about what value to pass, lists no factors, and navigates to no policy fragment.
  - **Shared** (>=2 policies name it as `target_tool`): **separate** — that tool gets **its own standalone `role=tool` invocation document**, and every hub sharing it **does not set `meta.tool`**, lists no parameters, does not name the tool document, and describes only its own policy's handling action in business language; the agent must retrieve the tool description independently.
  The reason is a hard constraint rather than a matter of style: the gate requires each task tool to have exactly one `meta.tool` document (a single home, or the parameter basis splits across two places and inevitably drifts), and the snake_case whitelist opens only for the document that sets `meta.tool`. If a shared tool's invocation were forced into one policy's hub, every other policy's hub would be arbitrarily demoted to a stub that cannot name its parameters, and which policy got the detail would be pure accident; giving it its own document makes the hubs symmetric, at the cost of one extra hop for the agent.
- **Each workflow has exactly one workflow document (role=sop), and it is never a runbook**: it carries the workflow's semantics — the class of situation it governs, stated generally; what each part achieves, in business language; who may perform each kind of part (an `owner=user` part is something the customer does themselves, which does not automatically mean the customer confirms anything); what each part relies on from another part's outcome; and the reasons in `order_rationale` — as an account of purpose and reliance written in principle form, so that the order of the parts is entailed by those reasons and never stated. It contains no numbered or ordered steps, no step or role-by-step table, no process arrows, no sequence of actions, no tool names (tool names live only in their own invocation documents), no "before running this procedure" entry condition, no counter-example of reordering, and no description of the particular combination of circumstances a task presents. The workflow's own `prerequisite` is carried like any other prerequisite (Value 3). Preparation, confirmation, waiting, review, wrap-up or exception branches the structured workflow does not contain may never be filled in.
- **Share tools** are always in the tool list and are called directly by name; never write "unlock / hand over" for them.
- A document never states when to hand off to a human — a handoff is the agent's own fallback when facing a request no policy covers, and policy documents do not mention it.

### 5. meta is a self-declaration hidden from the agent and checkable by the gate
Each document carries meta (ignored at runtime, never transcribed into title/content, for the gate only): `source` (the relative path of the source yaml), `category`, `profile` (base|extended), `role` (hub|factor|meta_rule|facet|policy|tool|sop), then per role `policy_id`/`workflow`/`tool`/`factor` (where `tool` is written only on that tool's invocation document — its hub when the tool is exclusive, or the standalone `role=tool` document when shared by >=2 policies; the hubs of a shared tool always omit `tool`, per the split in Value 4), and `carries` — the list of quantitative basis keys this document **carries** (an extended policy's constant keys, a base policy's literal constant keys, or tier parameter keys; a reference of the form "=parameter" in a base policy's params is excluded — the home of that number is already the tier parameter key it cites). A factor/meta_rule additionally declares in `covers` the compute binding names it implements, used only for the completeness gate; a factor may have an empty list, a meta_rule may not, and every compute binding (including `result`) has exactly one home across the whole corpus. A hub's meta **forbids `factors` and `covers`**, and its `carries` must be empty; no agent-visible index may form between it and the independent fragments. `carries`/`covers` constrain the uniqueness of the bearing document; they do not limit how many times an equivalent may recur within one document's prose. The provenance and derivation of presentation units stay in the private authoring ledger and never extend the published JSON schema. The gate checks against meta: complete tool coverage, one workflow document per workflow, no quantitative content in a hub, complete compute-binding coverage, and each quantitative key declared by exactly one document; the number of fragments is set by the real semantic boundaries the source locators represent.

`prerequisite` does not go into meta; it is registered in the private authoring ledger only, as `source_locator = <policy_id>.prerequisite[<i>]`, together with its carriage: the `principle_home` (the document and section of **the problem it blocks** — its hub or one of its fragments — that states the principle from which this hop follows), the `completing_docs` (the depended-on problem's own documents whose rules complete the entailment), the `anchors` (the plain business words by which the records describe the relevant items, present in the completing documents), and a short private derivation showing that principle, completing rules and facts together entail the condition at the source's strength. The condition sentence itself has no home in any document, and the depended-on problem's documents never state the blocked side's principle. The ledger also records that edge's `problem` (the depended-on side) so that aggregation across problems can be detected; none of this ever enters any prose. A workflow's order is registered the same way: each ordering reason, the documents whose principles entail it, and the derivation of the order.

## The global execution order within a product (once per product; ad-hoc document-by-document rendering is forbidden)

One doc render is a global transaction over one product. Freeze every source asset, the document topology and each document's presentation plan before writing any prose; any change to the yaml or the tools invalidates the whole plan, and it must restart from Step 0. Never "see a policy and immediately write a document" — otherwise the single documentary home for quantities, the uniqueness of tool invocations, complete compute coverage, genre variety and a hub that leaks no index can no longer be reliably guaranteed.

### Step 0: resolve the single input and freeze this render's snapshot

Read `skills/erl-kb-doc-render/references/kb_doc.json` from the repository root. Look for `<product>.extended.yaml` in the target scenario: with exactly one, choose the extended type and also read the `task_tools.py` beside it; with none, choose the blueprint's base product yaml; if several extended files are found, stop and remove the ambiguity of source first, never just taking the first. Record the source path, the profile and the official product name, and compute a content fingerprint for the chosen yaml and task_tools (if any); these fingerprints are persisted with the private plan in Step 2. If any fingerprint has changed before publication, this whole round is void and must be re-planned.

This step is read-only: it creates no document and deletes no existing docs.

### Step 1: build the product-level source asset ledger

Inventory the complete product in one pass, rather than hunting per document as you write:

- every variant, every `parameter_schema` key and the actual value at each tier;
- every base policy, and each of its literal constants and references to tier parameters;
- every eligibility of every base policy: the condition sentence, the applicable subject, the evidentiary authority and the object correlation; for a time gate also copy the temporal_basis's anchor event/field, the `anchor + duration` counting rule, the direction of comparison, the source of the duration and the equality boundary;
- every extended policy: its `rule`, its ordered compute bindings (including `result`), its params, its cross-category conditions and its target tool;
- every workflow: the applicable situation, the steps' tool/owner/order, the dataflow and the order rationale;
- every tool in `task_tools.py` with its owner, parameters, enums and return contract, and how many extended policies reference each tool;
- share tools serve only as a source for the prose code whitelist, and generate no invocation document.

Break these assets into normative semantic atoms and record a precise locator for each (at least a policy/workflow/tool id + a field path); GT, personas, design rationale, existing docs and industry common sense may never be mixed into the ledger. The ledger is the sole input for the later coverage and reverse-traceability judgements. Still no prose at this point.

### Step 2: design the corpus-wide document topology, the unique homes and the presentation plan first

Build a rendering ledger for **every** document this product will produce, freezing in advance each one's `role`, `source`, `policy_id`/`workflow`/`tool`, hidden `factor`/`covers`, `carries`, topic, filename, id, principal genre and intended presentation modules, and saving Step 0's source content fingerprints at the head of the plan. Keep writing the ledger into `env/<scenario>/.doc_render_staging/<product>/render_plan.yaml`; it is private authoring state, never published and never part of the KB. The uuid8 is generated once at planning time and frozen, so later prose edits never change an id.

The ledger also holds the unpublished semantic bijection and the surface one-to-many mapping:

- `semantic_obligations`: for each source semantic atom, its `source_locator`, an equivalence note, its unique `target_doc` and its `canonical_section`. For a base policy, record per rule/eligibility the condition, the authority boundary, the object correlation and the time anchor; for an extended policy, record per trigger/subject/rule/compute/cross-category the eligibility, the acting base, the ordering/grouping, the cap/floor and the rounding relations; for workflows and tools, take only the contracts that exist in their own source.
- `normative_claims`: pre-list, per document, the formal clauses and every normative claim in all equivalent expressions; record for each its one or more `source_locator`s, its section and its expression type. One obligation may correspond to several equivalent claims within its unique bearing document, but must not appear in a second document; and every claim must be fully covered by its locator.
- `presentation_units`: record per module its `type`, section, inputs and proof. `canonical` records the formal clause and its obligation; `equivalent` records the transformation used and the locator; `derived` records the premise locators, the explicit hypothetical inputs, the step-by-step derivation and the expected conclusion; `editorial` is recorded as non-normative. Every row of a table, every FAQ answer and every judgement in an example must fall into one of these; a single blanket source for a whole document is not enough.

The mapping must close at the semantic level: every obligation has exactly one bearing document and at least one canonical claim; every equivalent claim is equivalent to its obligation; every derived unit is re-computable from this document's obligations and its explicit hypothetical inputs alone; and every normative conclusion in the prose is either canonical, equivalent, or a proved derivation. `covers` can prove only that no binding was lost; it cannot prove that the wording did not change meaning, that a derivation is valid, or that nothing was added — those ledgers must close too.

Allocate in this order:

1. first assign every tier parameter by facet, so each `variants.<key>` has exactly one `carries` home;
2. then assign each base policy its policy document, and the homes of its literal constants and all of its eligibility/temporal obligations;
3. for each extended policy, assign every compute binding (including `result`) to the `covers` of exactly one factor or meta_rule, then assign its params and the tier quantitative keys it reads; each fragment carries one coherent business rule, and unrelated bindings may never be crammed together to save documents; at the same time register each of that policy's `prerequisite` edges with its carriage (Value 5) — a principle home that may only be this policy's own hub or one of its fragments, completing documents that are the depended-on problem's own documents, and the anchors — never a newly opened "prerequisite overview", and never a document that states the condition;
4. assign each extended policy exactly one hub, but the hub registers no `factors`/`covers`/quantitative `carries`, and the fragment information in the plan never enters the hub's prose;
5. count the target_tool references and freeze the invocation homes: an exclusive calculating tool merges into its hub, while a shared calculating tool and any non-calculating tool each get their own tool document; every tool ends with exactly one `meta.tool`;
6. assign each workflow exactly one workflow document, and register its ordering reasons and the documents whose principles entail them.

Once the unique homes are allocated, choose each document's principal genre and presentation modules by the shape of its rule: place the formal clause first; then choose the equivalent modules that genuinely aid understanding; then choose the derived modules that cover boundaries, branches or calculation ambiguities. Neighbouring documents should avoid mechanically repeating the identical set of sections, but a genre unsuited to the shape of the source may never be chosen merely to "look different". This ledger is for authoring and the gate only, and may never generate an agent-retrievable index, overview, table of contents or "suggested further reading" document.

### Step 3: audit the global plan before any prose

Before writing prose, complete one static closure check: every source asset has a home; every quantitative key belongs to exactly one document; every compute binding has exactly one `covers`; every rule/eligibility/temporal/calculation/procedure/tool obligation has exactly one bearing document and canonical position; every planned equivalent claim has a reverse source locator; every derived unit's premises, hypothetical inputs and derivation are closed; every extended policy has exactly one index-free hub plus the independent fragments its real semantic boundaries produce; every task tool has exactly one invocation home, with an exclusive target_tool in its hub and a shared or non-target tool in a standalone tool document; every workflow has exactly one workflow document; and ids and filenames do not collide.

Then audit for leakage and additions: the hub plan contains no factor name, count, binding, cross-category list, composition step, fragment title or completion condition; there is no index-style cross-linking among factors/meta_rules; no derived example reads across documents; **nothing the solver must work out is planned as a statement** — check document by document that no planned sentence, heading, table or example states a dependency between problems, an order across problems, the course of a procedure or a remedy; that every prerequisite has a principle home, completing documents, anchors and a closed private derivation at the source's strength; that no principle serves more than its own hop or lists an upstream chain; that no workflow document plans a step list, a step order or a tool name; that no blocked problem's document names the tool of a resolving action; that no document writes some task's world state in as a policy fact; that every planned title and opening is general; and that the wording plan copies no charter sentence and shares no phrasing across documents; and every confirmation, question, wait, read-back, review, notification, exception handling or duplicate-guard action the plan contains can be pointed back to an explicit requirement in the source. When the plan fails, change only the ledger, the derivation inputs, or delete the unsourced claim; an equivalent repetition can only enrich an existing obligation, never mask a missing one, and modifying the structured source to rationalise KB text already written is forbidden.

### Step 4: render the base corpus first

Write the facets first, then the base policies. A facet document covers one facet and lists every tier in its table; it may add Q&A around that facet, notes distinguishing the tiers, and hypothetical selection cases, but may never summarise another facet. A base policy document carries one complete policy or one indivisible basis; add an eligibility checklist, a branch table, boundary cases, positive/negative cases or a local FAQ as its logical shape warrants. As each document is written, immediately fill in `normative_claims` and `presentation_units` item by item, and check the official product name, the business language, the markdown structure, `carries`, whether the identity of formal numbers versus example numbers is clear, and whether any implementation detail leaked; delete any unsourced "handling note" or "exception" outright. A green light on one document does not mean the product is done.

When writing a base policy, first check whether any of its clauses has been retired by extended: extended and base are **successive versions** of the same product law, extended wins where they conflict, and every conflict is declared sentence by sentence by some extended policy's `overwrite` (`base_policy` + `superseded_clause` + `replaced_by`). A clause declared retired is **still carried by this base document** — an override never breaks the bijection, and the corpus may never contain a hole where the source has something and the KB has nothing — but it must be written as **retired**: register the clause verbatim in `meta.superseded_clauses`, and mark it in the prose, in controlled wording, as no longer applicable and superseded by the current basis. Three disciplines may not be broken: only the named sentence is retired, and the policy's other clauses are still rendered as clauses in force; **what is retired is the handling basis, not the parameter value**, so base's params values remain valid and are still carried by this document's `carries` (the single-home logic for quantities does not change by one line); and the retiring document **does not restate the new basis's numbers**, since the new basis is uniquely carried in that extended policy's fragment, or the same quantitative key would acquire a second home. The reverse is equally a red light: the KB may never retire a clause the source layer never declared retired — when the renderer judges that base and extended conflict, the correct action is to report the conflict back to the structured policy's authoring side so it can add the `overwrite` declaration, not to quietly hide one side at the document layer.

Base is the business foundation of the extended fragments; freezing it first prevents extended documents from copying across tier values and basic bases that already have a unique home. After each base policy document, check it against each eligibility: does the prose still clearly separate "what the customer said" from "what the system must show", and how the evidence correlates to this order/application/charge. A time gate should take one hypothetical date that just satisfies it and one that just fails as boundary cases in the same document; where the dates would introduce holiday, time-zone or calendar assumptions the source does not define, use abstract "on the boundary day / the day before" cases instead. Before publication, recompute from the case prose and confirm it reaches the same judgement as the source temporal basis.

### Step 5: render the independent factors and meta_rules, policy by policy

Work through the extended policies in order, but write the independent fragments of each before its hub:

- a factor states only its own piece of real business policy, never calls itself a "factor", "satellite" or "the Nth item", and never says what other fragments exist;
- a meta_rule independently states the combination, take-the-higher, cap, priority or ordering basis, lists no full policy index and navigates to no factor;
- title/content contain no `meta.factor`, `meta.covers`, binding name or policy id;
- each document carries only the policy quantities assigned to it; those values may recur in the same document's clauses, tables, FAQ and examples. Any other example value must be explicitly marked a hypothetical input and registered in `presentation_units`;
- the document that is a prerequisite's principle home states, in abstract terms and in its own wording, what its governed result is and what it may never rest on, so that the hop follows for a reader who applies it to the facts; it never writes the condition, never writes a handling condition of the form "before this is handled, that must hold", never names or points at the other problem, its resolution or its tool, never arranges a handling order, never tabulates or quizzes the dependency, and never writes any specific customer's current state;
- a factor is best expanded with a local input-judgement-output, a condition table, positive/negative cases or basis Q&A; a meta_rule with an exact statement of how the components combine, a truth table or boundary cases — never a contrast between a wrong and a correct reading. No module may reference a factor not assigned to this document;
- after finishing one extended policy's fragments, check forward in the authoring ledger that the compute bindings and quantitative keys are complete and not duplicated across documents, then check backward that every sentence of eligibility, action, ordering and handling can be located in a source field or strictly derived from this document's source fields. The coverage check itself never enters the prose, but proved local cases, Q&A and points of judgement should be published as presentation modules.

For calculation relationships, use a **semantic round trip** rather than keyword ticking: first construct, from the source rule/compute, a nonzero witness input that would make the candidate interpretations give different results, then recompute reading only the prose about to be published. Where combined versus successive rates, the original versus the discounted base, capping before versus after, or rounding stepwise versus finally would give different results, sharpen the formal clause's definition of the quantity until only the source result follows from it; a hypothetical worked example computed the source's way may accompany it, but the prose never names, presents or warns against the wrong candidate. Never declare it done merely because the prose contains one of the words "first", "then" or "combined"; and never manufacture an extra clause for an ordinary calculation that has no such semantic choice.

This step deliberately gives the agent no reading order: fragments are named by natural business topic, and whether they are relevant can only be inferred from the customer's situation, DB facts and policy semantics.

### Step 6: write the hub and the tool invocations only after the fragments are frozen

Only once Step 5's hidden coverage is closed may the hub be written. A hub may state, with grounds drawn only from the source policy's trigger, subject, rule and target tool, the applicable situation, the business result it governs, the priority boundaries the source has set, the handling behaviour and the observable effect; it may expand this local information with Q&A, a business-effect card or a tool parameter table, but must never bring the fragment ledger of Steps 2/5 into the prose, must never summarise "what it consists of", and must never add reassuring steps the source lacks such as customer confirmation, date confirmation, quote read-back or post-action review. A hub's derived content likewise may not touch a calculation factor or quantitative basis its `covers`/`carries` explicitly forbid it to carry. If a hub is a prerequisite's principle home, it states that principle in abstract terms only — never an entry condition — and may never use "applicable situation" to bring another problem, its resolving action or a handling order into the prose.

The hub of an exclusive tool also completes the four invocation blocks, explaining parameter by parameter the meanings, formats, enums and returns the source can prove, but never explaining how a computed parameter is arrived at, and never rewriting the existence of a parameter into a policy of asking or confirming with the customer. A shared or non-calculating tool gets a standalone tool document that describes only the capability the tool itself can prove, favouring no policy. A tool document may be enriched with a parameter table, an enum quick reference, an input-return illustration and contract FAQ; illustrative inputs may not be written as recommended policy values, and an FAQ may not invent error handling. Where the source defines no error handling, the return section states only the known successful receipt and invents no failure branch. When finished, check that every task tool has exactly one `meta.tool` document across the whole product, and that its role matches the reference-count split frozen in Step 2; an invocation home may never be moved temporarily to get one document through.

### Step 7: render the workflow document last

Write the workflow document only once every tool name, owner and calling description is stable in its own invocation document. The prose carries an equivalent of the workflow's situation, parts, owners, dataflow and `order_rationale` in principle form (Value 4): the class of situation it governs, stated generally; what each part achieves, in business language; who performs each kind of part; and why the outcome of one part is something another relies on — so that the order follows from the reasons rather than being given. It may be enriched with an account of the parties' roles or basis Q&A about why one outcome depends on another, but never with numbered or ordered steps, a step or role-by-step table, process arrows, tool names, a before/after entry condition, or a counter-example of reordering. It may not add preparation, confirmation, waiting, receipt verification, closing checks or exception handling unless they were already in the workflow; nor may it add the calculation-factor index the hub omits, or gather the factors into a checklist. Before moving on, confirm the derivation privately: from this document, the parts' own invocation documents, the policies they touch and the records, a careful reader can work out every part and the source order at the source's strength — and no single sentence states that order.

### Step 8: form the complete corpus in staging and run the corpus-wide gate

Keep the current official docs, and write every planned JSON in one pass into `env/<scenario>/.doc_render_staging/<product>/docs/`; staging sits outside the `docs/` tree so the runtime never recursively loads unpublished corpus, and new and old documents must never be mixed. First check each document for structure, genre, product subject, whether the same-source expansion is sufficient, the semantic bijection, the derivation of presentation units, meta, and implementation leakage; then run:

```bash
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-kb-doc-render/scripts/check_docs.py env/<scenario> <product> \
  --docs-dir env/<scenario>/.doc_render_staging/<product>/docs
```

The gate must prove forward from the source yaml/task_tools that base/extended/tool/workflow coverage is complete, that compute bindings are covered, that quantities have a single documentary home, that the hub carries no factor index, and that every workflow has exactly one workflow document; and then prove backward from the docs that every formal or equivalent normative claim has a structured source, and that every derived conclusion follows closed from sources in the same document. On the implicit-carriage side, `check_docs.py` decides mechanically only the unmistakable surface of a pre-solved or copied rendering: no document contains a prerequisite's `condition` or `policy_source` verbatim, or restates one closely in a sentence that also carries a gating or ordering cue; no document uses the controlled scaffolding of a stated dependency or procedure (entry conditions, not-yet-hold clauses, precedence labels, step-before-step, wrong/correct-reading contrasts, named mistakes); a workflow document names none of its step tools and numbers no steps; no title takes the question form or the internal-procedure form; no run of ten or more words is copied from the charter; no run of ten or more words recurs in three or more documents; and the blocked problem's documents never contain the tool name of an edge's `resolved_by`. Whether every dependency and order is really entailed at the source's strength, whether the depended-on documents are findable from the records' wording, and whether the prose still pre-solves something in words no pattern catches remain hard manual gates. Any failure returns to the Step where the error first arose, and the whole product gate is re-run; no incremental patching in the official directory.

### Step 9: perform the manual semantic round-trip acceptance (retrieval ranking is not a gate)

Mask the yaml and the render plan, and verify the semantic round trip from the prose about to be published alone. This step does not require any preset query to hit a document, and does not judge the corpus by BM25 ranking or top-k position:

- the hub, factors and meta_rules must still be semantically independent, offering no hidden fragment index, reading order or completion signal;
- the policy must still be recomputable from docs + DB alone, and omitting any load-bearing fragment must produce a distinguishable error;
- nowhere in the corpus does an index, overview, workflow or tool document re-list the complete set of factors;
- pick at random one base policy with a time eligibility and one extended policy with non-trivial grouping/base/rounding, mask the yaml and the render plan, and recompute each from the formal clauses and derived cases of the prose about to be published; both must reach the same conclusion, agree with Step 2's semantic obligations and the machine formula, and admit no second equally natural reading;
- run a two-way entailment check on every equivalent unit: the formal clause must entail the restatement, and the restatement must neither loosen nor tighten the formal clause. For every derived unit, mask the expected answer and recompute from its hypothetical inputs and this document's formal clauses alone; every row of a truth table, both sides of a boundary case and every FAQ answer must hold;
- run a deletion test on each document: after deleting every equivalent/derived/editorial unit, the canonical clauses must still fully restore that document's source semantics; and after restoring them, the set of permitted actions, parameters, ordering and results for any input must be unchanged. The former prevents hiding necessary policy only in a case; the latter prevents an expansion from quietly adding policy;
- audit in reverse, sentence by sentence, every sentence expressing an obligation, prohibition, eligibility, authorisation, step, ordering, question, confirmation, wait, review, notification or exception handling; every canonical/equivalent sentence must hit a `normative_claims.source_locator`, and every derived conclusion must hit the premises and derivation in `presentation_units`. Search specifically for high-risk wording such as "confirm, again, review, must first, after completion, if it fails, if incorrect, do not repeat, hand off to a human", but judge by semantics — the presence or absence of a keyword never substitutes for the audit;
- run a derivation test on every prerequisite and every workflow: from the prose about to be published and the records alone, a careful reader must be able to work out each hop and the source order at the source's strength (a hard gate stays hard; nothing becomes optional or merely advisable), reaching the depended-on documents through the words the records use; and at the same time no single sentence, heading, table or example may state the hop, the order or the procedure. Failing the first is an omission, failing the second is a pre-solved rendering, and either is a rewrite;
- read every title and opening as someone who knows no task: each must name a general subject a standing knowledge-base page would carry, never a combination of circumstances or a restated request; and read the corpus for wording copied from the charter and phrasing repeated across documents, which the gate catches only at its coarsest.

If any document states a dependency, an order, the course of a procedure or a remedy that the solver is meant to work out; if a hub or any other document supplies a reading index, a remaining count or a completion signal; if the prose's positive and negative cases cannot uniquely reproduce the source judgement; if an equivalent restatement changes a boundary; if a derived case needs a rule from outside the document to hold; or if any normative sentence has no structured source or closed derivation — it must be rewritten, even where the machine gate has not yet detected it. What is checked here is the two-way semantic equivalence of the policy, the validity of the derivations and the closure of the visible materials — not the retriever's ranking.

### Step 10: publish wholesale after confirming the source has not changed

Before publishing, recompute and check Step 0's source content fingerprints against `render_plan.yaml`; if they have changed, discard staging and start again from Step 0. Only when they are unchanged, perform the recoverable directory-level replacement for that product: if the official directory exists, first move it to `previous_docs/` under the same staging product directory, then move the accepted staging `docs/` to `env/<scenario>/docs/<product>/`, touching no other product throughout. Then run the gate once more against the official directory, without `--docs-dir`; on failure, move the failed new directory to `failed_docs/` under the same staging product and, if `previous_docs/` exists, restore the original official directory — a red-lit corpus may never be left on the official path. Only after the official gate passes may this product's `previous_docs/`, `render_plan.yaml` and its own now-empty `.doc_render_staging/<product>/` be deleted, so a stale plan is never picked up by mistake next time. Delete **only your own product's staging directory, never the shared `.doc_render_staging/` parent**: products are rendered concurrently, one agent per product, and siblings hold their in-progress corpora in their own subdirectories of that same parent — removing the whole tree destroys their work.

## The structural contract (copy the skeleton of `skills/erl-kb-doc-render/references/kb_doc.json`, replace the placeholders; no domain examples given)

- File: `env/<scenario>/docs/<product>/<topic>_<uuid8>.json`; topic is a lower-case snake English phrase; uuid8 = `str(uuid.uuid4())[:8]`, generated once at authoring time and frozen.
- Fields: `id` = `doc_<product>_<topic>_<uuid8>` (matching the filename); `title` in English, in one form only — the official product name, a colon, and a general subject named at the charter's own level of generality — never a question, never an internal-procedure label, never the combination of circumstances a task presents, and never wording that signals a dependency, a sequence or a remedy; `content` in English markdown, one topic per document, with a formal clause as the normative baseline, expanded vertically with tables, Q&A, cases, truth tables, points of judgement or process diagrams as the shape of the source warrants. Length follows the per-genre targets of Value 1 rather than a uniform minimum; usually no more than about 2600 characters, though it may exceed that where completely presenting a complex source and closing the derivations genuinely requires it. Never pad with cross-document indexes, filler, or unsourced confirmation/review/exception flows. For `meta`, see Value 5. A hub's title/content may not use index-style wording such as "overview, composition, complete basis, all factors".
- Rendering scope: extended type = tier facets + base policies + extended (an index-free hub + independent factor/meta_rule/cross-category fragments) + workflow documents + task tool invocations (exactly one per task tool: an exclusive one merged into its policy hub, a shared one as its own `role=tool` document — see Value 4); base type = tier facets + base policies. Tool names and owners are read from task_tools.py (the owner decides whether the invocation document is worded as "the agent handles it" or "the customer themselves runs it").
- Retirement registry: a `role=policy` document may also carry `meta.superseded_clauses` — listing the base clauses it carries that some extended policy's `overwrite` has declared retired, quoted verbatim from that base policy's `rule` (compared as a substring after removing whitespace, because a folded scalar inserts a space at each soft line break). Once declared, the controlled wording **no longer in force** must appear in `content`; each retired clause has exactly one registering document; and a clause the source layer's `overwrite` never declared may not be registered. Omit the key when there is no retired clause.
- Runtime wiring: this skill produces corpus only. The currently frozen loader loads documents from `<difficulty>/docs/`; wiring `env/<scenario>/docs/` into the runtime (or adjusting the load layout) is downstream adaptation and not part of this skill.

## Gates (a red light forbids finishing; run from the repository root)

```bash
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-kb-doc-render/scripts/check_docs.py env/<scenario> <product>   # DOCS OK
```

Hard failures on:
- **Structure**: a missing field; id not matching the filename; a duplicate id; empty content. Length statistics are diagnostic only and never a pass condition.
- **Implementation leakage** (Value 1): any snake_case code token outside the whitelist appearing in content/title (the whitelist = task tool names + share tool names + the unlock/call meta-tool names); or a calculation-DSL fragment or a 16-hex row id.
- **Tool coverage** (Value 4): a task tool with no meta.tool document or more than one; an exclusive target_tool not landing in its hub, or a shared/non-target tool not landing in a standalone tool document; an invocation document missing the tool name or the word "unlock"; a customer's own tool's document missing "the customer themselves".
- **Workflow document form** (Value 4): a workflow with no meta.workflow document or more than one; a workflow document that names any of its step tools, or numbers its steps.
- **Pre-solved dependency and procedure forms** (Values 0/3): a prerequisite's `condition` or `policy_source` appearing verbatim in any document, or restated closely in one sentence that also carries a gating or ordering cue; the controlled scaffolding of a stated dependency or procedure anywhere (an entry condition, a not-yet-hold clause, a precedence label ending in "first", step-before-step, a wrong/correct-reading contrast, a named mistake); the `resolved_by` tool of an edge named inside the docs of the problem it blocks; a title in the question form or the internal-procedure form.
- **Copied and templated wording** (Value 0): any run of ten or more words copied from the charter's text; any run of ten or more words recurring in three or more documents. Backticked or quoted spans — exact identifiers and fixed phrases — are excluded.
- **Scattering and coverage** (Values 2/3): an extended policy with no hub, or a hub containing `meta.factors`/`meta.covers`, quantitative carries, a factor name, a component inventory, fragment navigation or a reading-completion cue; a binding of the source compute (including `result`) claimed by no `meta.covers` or by several; a required quantitative key declared by none or by several; a document's carries exceeding the cap; a declared value not appearing in content. There is no fixed floor on fragment count divorced from source semantics.
- **Retirement registry** (checked mechanically by `check_docs.py`): a base clause some extended policy's `overwrite` declared retired that no `role=policy` document registers in `meta.superseded_clauses` (the KB is still teaching retired law); registered but without the controlled wording **no longer in force** appearing in the prose; the same retired clause registered by several documents; a clause registered that the source-layer `overwrite` never declared retired (the KB may not retire on its own). Manually check as well: the retirement wording is attached to the right sentence, the other clauses are still carried as clauses in force, and the retiring document does not restate the new basis's numbers.
- **Strict bijection and derivation validity** (Value 0, a hard manual gate): `semantic_obligations` containing an unborne item (an item declared retired by `overwrite` does not count as unborne, but must be borne in its retired form); one obligation appearing in several bearing documents; canonical clauses unable to independently restore the source semantics; an equivalent claim with no source locator or not equivalent to the source; a derived unit without same-document premises, explicit hypothetical inputs and a re-computable derivation; an FAQ, case, truth table or point of judgement adding a condition, action, ordering, result, promise or exception handling from outside the source; a prerequisite or workflow order that the corpus does not entail at the source's strength, or that some document states rather than entails. A green script can never override this red light.

Any change to the yaml or task_tools -> staging is void, rebuild the whole product from Step 0, publish wholesale and re-run the official directory gate.

Note: `check_docs.py` can prove structure, coverage, explicit leakage and the coarsest surface of pre-solved or copied wording; it cannot tell whether the prose changed the meaning of the event a clock starts from, an authority boundary or a discount's acting base, and it cannot automatically prove that an equivalent restatement preserved a boundary, that a logical derivation is valid, that a document added no confirmation/review norms, or that a dependency is genuinely entailed yet stated nowhere. The two-way mapping, derivation proofs and semantic round trip of Steps 3/4/5/6/7/9 are formal gates and may not be skipped because the script is green; retrieval ranking is not a delivery gate.
