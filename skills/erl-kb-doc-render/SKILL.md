# erl-kb-doc-render (once per product)

You are a policy-manual editor. The input is one product's charter yaml (the type is determined automatically: if `env/<scenario>/tasks/*/<product>.extended.yaml` exists it is the **extended type**, read together with the `task_tools.py` beside it; otherwise `env/<scenario>/blueprint/products/<product>.yaml` is taken as the **base type**). The output is a set of KB documents landing in `env/<scenario>/docs/<product>/`.

**The yaml is the only authoring source of policy truth, and the published docs are the only policy surface the agent can see.** This skill is the rendering step from "structured truth -> retrievable corpus": after any change to the yaml, the whole product must be re-rendered, pass the gates in staging, and replace the official directory wholesale; never hand-patch the official directory incrementally — a patch inevitably drifts the basis. There is neither a floor nor a ceiling on the number of documents: split only along the semantic boundaries that genuinely exist in the source. If the structured source is right but the published text leads a careful agent to a different answer, or the published text makes the agent carry an obligation the source does not have, the rendering has failed; yaml/compute the agent cannot see is never a defence for an ambiguity or an added rule in a document. The rendering has equally failed if the published text goes beyond the form in which the source states a dependency — naming the resolving tool, restating a state-form sentence as an action, stringing several problems into a route or an ordered procedure, or warning against a wrong reading: the KB is the context the solver reasons from, never the route it follows. Correctness and form are held together: the corpus must entail exactly what the source entails, and state each dependency exactly as the source states it. The source has already chosen that form — each prerequisite edge carries a prerequisite sentence in the blocked policy's own rule — and the renderer copies that sentence, never translates it and never downgrades its form.

### The correctness boundary (never to be crossed)

This skill is responsible only for rendering the structured policy losslessly into documents the agent can see. Correctness is verified by three relationships: structured policy <-> GT, structured policy -> complete coverage in the published documents, and published documents -> reverse traceability with nothing added. The render gates can check a document's structure, coverage, references and the appearance of numbers, but they may not erect business rules, calculate on the agent's behalf, or judge eligibility, and they may not treat tool-call/refusal behaviour, retrieval ranking, top-k hits or a surface-request smoke test as a correctness backstop. A tool description states only the calling contract `task_tools.py` can prove; the policy's decisions about eligibility, objects, timing and calculation come only from the yaml, and are made by the solving agent itself. GT, a task's `user_scenario`, `design_rationale`, audit conclusions and existing docs are never a source of fact for back-filling policy.

## Values (whenever a step conflicts with them, the values win)

### 0. Strict semantic bijection: semantics do not proliferate, expression may be rich, and a dependency is stated as the source states it and no further (highest priority)
The KB and the structured policy form a strict bijection at the level of **normative semantic atoms**; this does not require a literal one-to-one match between source sentences and natural-language sentences. One policy may be split across several documents along real semantic boundaries; every source semantic atom still has exactly one **bearing document** (a threshold figure a factor's ladder restates in its own document, per Value 2, is not a second bearing of the parameter's atom), but within that document it may form a `presentation bundle`: one formal clause as the normative anchor, then tables, Q&A, cases or diagrams restating it equivalently or displaying its necessary consequences. Several presentation units are still the same policy semantics, not a new policy, so long as they change no executable condition, action, parameter, ordering or result:

- **Forward completeness**: every piece of semantics that can change a judgement or an action — in the variants parameters, in base rule/eligibility/temporal basis, in extended trigger/subject/rule/compute/cross-category/**prerequisite** — must appear losslessly and be assigned to exactly one bearing document; a source atom may never be dropped on the grounds that the expression is already rich. `prerequisite` is load-bearing semantics, not metadata: its normative text is the edge's **prerequisite sentence** (`policy_source`, a sentence of the blocked policy's own rule stating outright, with an ordering connective, what that policy's result waits on — a record-visible state in the state form, the depended-on action in business words in the action form), and that sentence is carried **verbatim**, as a formal clause, in exactly one document of the policy it belongs to — its hub or one of its fragments. The edge's `condition`, `resolved_by` and `fallback_reason` are authoring fields and are never published. A corpus that drops the prerequisite sentence leaves the solver unable ever to discover that something else must be done — one of the most serious forms of omission there is; a corpus that adds what the source does not — the tool, the resolving problem beside a state-form sentence, the route — hands the solver the chain.
- **Reverse: nothing added**: every calculation, eligibility, authorisation, evidence requirement, step, ordering, wait, review, notification, exception handling and business effect in the prose — every "must / must not / only if ... then / first ... then / should ask or confirm / what to do on failure" — must have an explicit source locator. If the source does not have it, it may not be written; common sense, real company practice, the writer feeling it is safer, and existing documents already saying so are none of them sources.
- **Strength and boundaries unchanged**: never turn "may" into "must", never turn a tool parameter into a pre-action eligibility, and never add customer confirmations, delivery dates, read-backs, post-action reviews, duplicate guards, corrections, approvals, waits or handoff steps — unless the selected yaml or `task_tools.py` contract contains them verbatim or equivalently. Nor may you delete an actor, an object correlation, an evidentiary authority, a point in time, an equality boundary, a calculation base or an ordering relation that the source has.
- **One-to-many expression is allowed within a document**: the same atom may be written first as a formal clause, then as a table, then reworded as a general question the rule answers (never a particular customer's circumstances or request), or split into "condition - judgement - result". These expressions may repeat the numbers and conclusions, each time in fresh wording, but must point at the same source locator. Across documents, prefer not to replicate a quantitative basis or a complete rule — a low-priority preference that yields whenever a document's own rule needs a figure to be complete or unambiguous, above all a factor document's thresholds (Value 2).
- **Only closed logical derivation is allowed**: from the source atoms already assigned to this document you may deterministically generate numeric worked examples, boundary cases that just do and just do not qualify, truth tables of condition combinations, positive and negative cases, input-judgement-output diagrams and FAQs. A derived conclusion must be a logical consequence of the source rule; it may not call on a policy fragment outside this document to complete the conclusion, and the author's chosen example inputs may not be passed off as company facts or new thresholds. A derivation illustrates this document's own rule only — a prerequisite sentence this document carries is part of that rule and may be illustrated like any other clause, in the same form — but it never walks across problems, never strings the hops of a chain together, and never contrasts a wrong reading with a correct one or names a mistake to avoid.
- **Derivation does not assume a closed world**: where the source says only "when A holds, B follows", you may not unilaterally write "if A does not hold, B definitely does not follow", turn a sufficient condition into a necessary one, or treat the listed branches as an exhaustive set. Only where the source explicitly gives a complete boundary ("only / otherwise / if any is unmet / if and only if") may you fill in the corresponding counter-case, the remaining rows of a truth table, or a negative FAQ answer; combinations the source does not cover must be omitted, never completed into "refuse, leave unchanged, or hand off".
- **Derived examples are isolated from tasks**: a hypothetical case is constructed only from the source schema/enums and this document's rules; it must never read or restate a task's `user_scenario`, initial state, evaluation criteria, GT, actual tool-run results or internal UUIDs. Examples exist to explain general policy, and must not hint at any task's target object, complete input or execution chain.
- **Editorial content must be decision-neutral**: titles, introductions, section notes, table headers, captions and connective sentences are allowed; after deleting them, the set of permitted actions, forbidden actions, parameters, ordering and results the agent derives for any business fact must be completely unchanged. Never pad with corporate culture, service philosophy, industry common sense, invented "frequently asked" questions or synonymous filler that carries no retrieval value.
- **Expression may expand, the normative may not**: never invent a new condition, action or promise under the cover of a "scope of application, handling note, exception, customer communication, risk warning, frequently asked question" section. Richness comes from presenting existing semantics in several forms and from provable inference, never from policy outside the source.
- **Tools have their own source**: an invocation document's capability, parameters, owner, successful return and explicit errors come only from `task_tools.py`. That a tool accepts a parameter does not mean policy requires asking the customer for it or confirming it; that a tool records something under the current date does not mean the customer must supply a service date.
- **A dependency is stated as the source states it, and no further (co-equal with the bijection)**: the source fixes how a dependency between problems is worded — one prerequisite sentence in the blocked policy's own rule, saying outright with an ordering connective what that policy's result waits on: a record-visible state (the state form) or, as a declared fallback, a plainly named action (the action form). The KB carries that sentence and may restate it equivalently in the same document; it adds nothing to it: no other problem's tool name, no policy, no "call X first", no route through several problems, no ordered step list or step number across problems, no sequence of tool names, no dependency table or checklist, no contrast between a wrong reading and a correct one, no named mistake, no reminder. What the solver still works out — which write brings a waited-on state about, how the hops chain, in what order the writes land — is carried by the depended-on policies' own documents and the records, never by a sentence that walks the chain. A sentence, heading, table or example that routes the chain is a rendering failure even when it is semantically correct.
- **How a dependency is carried**: the source has already chosen the form. Each prerequisite edge carries a prerequisite sentence in the blocked policy's own rule, with an ordering connective, in one of two forms: the state form waits on a state the records make visible, in the words the records use, and names no action, performer or tool; the action form waits on the depended-on action named in business words, and names no tool and no policy. The blocked policy's hub or one of its fragments carries that sentence **word for word** as a formal clause, and may add equivalent restatements in the same document so long as none changes its form: a restatement of a state-form sentence never names the action, the performer or the other problem, and no restatement of either form names a tool, a policy or another document. The depended-on policy's own documents state, independently and in their own terms, when that problem's action applies and what it books; they never carry the blocked side's prerequisite sentence and never say who waits on them. The hop closes only when a reader puts the sentence, the depended-on rule and the facts together: in the state form the reader must find which write brings the state about. The strength is preserved exactly because the sentence is: a hard gate in the source stays a hard gate. The renderer never translates a prerequisite sentence into its own words, never generalises it beyond the item kinds it names, never narrows it to the case a task presents, and never downgrades a state-form sentence to an action.
- **Findable from the facts**: the depended-on documents must be reachable by a solver who has read a state-form sentence and looked at the records — they name the relevant items (the kinds of entries, states and events the solver sees, and the write that puts them there) in the records' own words, the same words the prerequisite sentence uses for the state, so that reasoning about what the records show leads to a search that finds them. They never borrow the blocked side's longer phrasing as a bridge, and no document points to another.
- **Exact where it must be, free elsewhere**: the prerequisite sentences, the identifiers the solver must reproduce (tool names, their parameter names and enum values, any fixed phrase a tool accepts, the product's official display name) and the controlled retirement wording are written exactly as the source has them, identifiers in backticks when they sit inside prose. Every other sentence may quote the charter or paraphrase it as the genre warrants: there is no requirement to reword, and no reward for it. Paraphrase changes wording, never semantics: every restatement stays exactly equivalent to its source atom, with the same actor, object, base, boundary, strength and order of calculation. The one thing wording must never do is change the form of a prerequisite sentence: no restatement of a state-form sentence, anywhere, names the action, a performer or the other problem, and no restatement of either form names a tool, a policy or another document.
- **General titles and openings**: a title names the product and a general subject at the charter's own level of generality, in the form the side of the company that would issue that paper would give it (Value 1); it never restates a customer's request, never describes the particular combination of circumstances some task presents, never announces a procedure for a specific situation, and never contains words that signal a dependency, a sequence or a remedy. An opening makes the product and the general subject plain, begins as its origin would — with the rule, the situation, the figure or the question — and never opens with a scenario or with a sentence about the document itself. The request type an `on_request` policy is defined by is that policy's general subject, and naming it is not restating a request. Each document must read as a standing page of the company's knowledge base, not as a reference written to match one case.
- **Precise without routing**: stating a dependency only as the source states it never licenses ambiguity. Every rule must still admit exactly one natural reading — its bases, groupings, caps, rounding points, time boundaries and authority boundaries pinned down — but that precision comes from exact definitions of what a quantity or a state is and from the prerequisite sentences the source already carries, never from a tool name, a route or a warning away from a wrong reading.

Every `semantic_obligation` in the authoring ledger must record a precise `source_locator`, a unique `target_doc` and a `canonical_section`; and every presentation unit must record its type (`canonical | equivalent | derived | editorial`), its section and its source. `canonical` and `equivalent` record one or more precise source locators; `derived` records the source locators it depends on, the author's chosen hypothetical inputs and a re-computable derivation; `editorial` must be declared non-normative. A normative sentence with no source or no proof of derivation is deleted, never retro-fitted into the yaml afterwards to accommodate the document.

### 1. The register of a real support knowledge base: one topic per document, varied genres, length from same-source expansion
The readers are front-line support agents, and the artifact is **a real company's internal support knowledge base**, which may carry policy clauses, quick references, Q&A, cases, decision tables and tool references all at once. Each document answers only the bases of judgement, handling actions or tool contracts the structured source has already defined for its topic; where the source defines nothing, it answers nothing. On wording and form:
- **Register follows the origin, and is always formal**: a clause reads as a clause, a schedule as a schedule, a desk guidance note as the rule the desk applies, terms addressed to the customer as conditions, a training note as questions answered — each with formal terms of address ("the customer", "the company", "that number") and no colloquialism. Whatever the origin, every sentence that carries policy is a declarative rule — what is charged, when something applies, what a quantity is — never an instruction to act, a step or a route; a paper whose nature is procedural (an operating procedure, a checklist of steps, a circular announcing a change) is not an origin this corpus may take, because the KB is policy the solver reasons from, not a procedure it follows. Formal does not mean adding cross-document navigation: a hub, a computation document and a factor may none of them write "for details see / see also / continue reading"; each document states only business rules the source already has. Write a scope, a condition of judgement, an exception or a handling note only where the source genuinely supplies that semantics; section completeness never overrides Value 0.
- **Wording carries the scenario's own flavour**: the corpus must read as this industry's knowledge base, not a generic template with the nouns swapped. Draw the terms of address, the names of business objects, roles, channels, units, statuses and documents from the scenario's own charter (display names, tool owners, enum wording) and frame worked examples and FAQ questions around general situations this business plausibly meets, never around the particular circumstances of a task. Flavour lives in vocabulary and framing only: never import an industry fact, trade custom, regulator or brand the source does not carry, and where a colourful scenario term would blur a boundary the source pins down, the source's precision wins.
- **Form is drawn from the scenario, never from this skill**: this skill lists no document formats, because no list is complete. Before any prose, Step 1 reads off the blueprint's world, its desks and channels, the product's tiering and its event flow **this company's own document ecology** — the kinds of policy-bearing paper its knowledge base would really hold, who inside the company issues each kind and for whom (the desk, the customer, the tariff side, the legal side, training) — and Step 2 assigns every document one origin from it. Title, opening, layout and register follow the origin; the ecology is the scenario's, so a bank, a carrier and a telco each grow their own, and two products of one scenario need not share one. An origin is a form device only: it adds no issue date, effective date, reference number, version, author or any claim the source does not carry, and it never touches the discrete topology, a prerequisite sentence or the bijection.
- **The shape of the rule decides what must be demonstrated; the origin decides how it looks**: a threshold or time window must show both of its sides, a compound condition its combinations, an if/else its branches, a multi-step calculation its figure worked through, a priority which rule governs, an object binding what is and is not the same object — and the demonstration then takes the form its origin would give it: a table, a pair of cases, a figure worked inside a clause, a question answered, a footnote to a schedule, or whatever else that paper carries. Two documents demonstrating the same kind of rule from different origins therefore look different, and the corpus never collapses into one "scope - basis - exception" template. Genre changes how it is read, never the policy topology.
- **Establish the formal clause first, then expand vertically**: the prose must contain one formal clause that can independently restore the source semantics; then choose the modules suited to this document from "equivalent restatement" and "logical derivation". So long as the shape of the source supports it, use at least one equivalent restatement and one logical derivation; a complex branch, calculation or time rule must never be published as a single compressed clause. Where the source genuinely does not support a kind of derivation, omit it; never manufacture material to complete a set of sections.
- **Equivalent restatement modules**: tabulate the same conditions and results, break a long sentence into condition/judgement/action, re-explain it as the question a customer might ask, or restate the same conclusion at a different granularity. A restatement must preserve the actor, the object, the authoritative source, the time boundary, the acting base, the priority and the strength.
- **Logical derivation modules**: publish, for instance, numeric worked examples, boundary cases on both sides, condition truth tables, positive/negative cases, input-judgement-output diagrams and FAQs — and whatever other closed demonstration the document's origin would carry — each about this document's own rule only. The title or prose of an example must make "hypothetical/example" explicit, so an example value never looks like a new fixed rate, term, status or customer fact; an FAQ's question may be editorially written, but its answer may only restate or deduce from this document's source atoms. Without ticket data or statistics to prove "frequent", never head a section "frequently asked questions" or claim that a question or misunderstanding is common; head it as the origin would — a basis Q&A, points of judgement, or whatever neutral heading that paper carries.
- **Expand length deliberately, with no mechanical word-count gate**: a simple quick reference usually runs about 250-500 characters, a conditional or branching policy about 450-900, a calculation/time rule about 700-1400, and a tool reference about 700-1200; these are editorial targets, not permission to add content. Below the range, first check whether a suitable equivalent or derived module was omitted; above it, check for repeated filler or overreach. Never pad by copying definitions across documents or by inventing exception handling, review actions or customer-communication steps.
- **Layout serves the genre**: use `##`/`###`, numbered clauses, tables, block quotes, FAQ questions, or input-judgement-output blocks within a single rule as the content warrants; one document need not use every form. Per-tier values and condition matrices suit tables, and the structure of a single calculation may be numbered; the order between problems is never numbered, tabulated or listed, and no layout contrasts a wrong reading with a correct one.
- **Every calculation relationship is stated in words**: multiplied by, divided by, less, total, the higher of, prorated by the remaining proportion — and **arithmetic symbols must never appear** (=, x, /, + and the like); writing it as an equation is an engineering view, not policy language.
- **Putting it in words must not compress the relationships**: the object acted on, the original/intermediate base, the ordering and grouping, the point at which a cap/floor applies and the point of rounding — everything the source rule pins down must be preserved in the computation document or the factor document that bears it. In particular, never compress "the discount rates are combined first and act once on the original base fee, the two never acting successively" into "the discounts are applied in turn", and never write successive action as merely "then discounted further" while omitting that the second acts on the amount after the first. This skill never re-chooses the algorithm; it translates the source rule losslessly.
- Internal codes **must never appear** — table names, field names, event types, parameter keys, policy ids, the calculation DSL, row ids. Translate them all into business language (the call balance, the billing period, months in service, registering an intake, awarding points). Never write implementation-facing phrasing such as "writes to / lands in the table / field"; write only the business effect. The one code form allowed is **a tool name, and that tool's own parameter names and enum values** (only inside its invocation document — see Value 4).

### 2. Scatter in an orderly way: policy entry points and independent fragments
Scattering is not shredding; it is **layering by semantic factor**, so the agent must discover and assemble the calculation policy across the corpus itself; no document may serve as a solution index or supply a "you have now read everything" stopping condition:

- **Each complex calculation policy (an extended policy) -> 1 hub + 1 computation document + one factor document per declared factor, in a discrete topology.** None of the three kinds mentions another: the hub does not list the fragments, the computation document does not hint that any factor exists, no factor document names the computation document or another factor, and there is no index, overview or star. The hub carries only the entry point, the fact that the policy exists: the applicable business situation the source has defined, the result it governs, the handling action or observable effect, and the calling instructions for the corresponding tool (when that tool is exclusive). The hub **must contain no factor name, factor count, component inventory, calculation decomposition, composition order, cross-category hint, fragment navigation, or any hint about "which other documents must still be read"**; and it must set no stopping signal such as "once you have read what this document lists, the assessment is complete". It tells the agent "such a policy, or such an entry point, exists", and never tells them what the policy is made of. Its applicable situation is stated at a general level, never as the combination of circumstances a task presents, and it carries nothing about another problem except, where it is the home of a prerequisite's prerequisite sentence, that sentence verbatim and its same-form equivalents.
- **The computation document (role `baseline`) renders the policy's `baseline.rule`: the standard method and the default values, complete and coherent on its own.** It states which quantities enter the result and from which records, the default rate, fee, allowance or threshold, the standard rounding, window, base and order, and how the inputs assemble into the result — everything a reader needs to compute a definite number with no factor applied — as one formal clause presented as its origin would present a calculation and worked through to a figure on hypothetical inputs. It is the single home of the policy's own default constants (the `baseline.params` keys that are this policy's `params`) and the `covers` home of every compute binding that belongs to no factor, `result` included. A product figure the standard method reads (a tier parameter) is named in the charter's words, "the product's telex release fee", never restated: its one home is the product's facet table, and the reader looks it up there. It is silent about the existence of anything that modifies it: no "unless otherwise provided", no "adjustments may apply", no "certain customers qualify for", no "set out elsewhere", no exception list, no pointer. An agent that finds this document and none of the factor documents must be able to compute — and compute wrong — without any signal; that silence is the design, not an omission. It never calls itself a baseline, a default or a standard calculation "subject to" anything; it is simply the calculation.
- **Each factor document renders exactly one declared factor of the source (`factors[].id`), with its whole ladder.** A factor is a trigger condition plus an effect, and the document is that rule complete: the applicable product and business situation, then every tier of the ladder — each tier's condition in the words the records use (the window, the count, the threshold figure it turns on, the other product's holding or entries it rests on, and every part of a compound condition) and what that tier does — as one formal clause expanded in the forms its origin and the rule's shape call for, every threshold shown on both of its sides and every compound condition in its combinations, in whatever form that origin would give them. **This is a must**: every threshold a tier turns on is stated in this document in figures, even where the same figure is also stated in another document of the policy or defined under a business name elsewhere in the corpus; a ladder that names a band or a level without its figure is incomplete, and ladder completeness outranks the single-home preference of Value 3. For a `parameter` factor each tier states the value it selects; for a `method` factor each tier states, in words, the computation rule it applies — the operation, the base it acts on, the rounding mode, precision and moment, the window or day-count convention, the order of two steps, the moment of a cap, the multiplier — exactly as the source pins them, never compressed into "calculated accordingly". The effect label, the factor's `baseline`, `evidence` and `cross_difficulty` are authoring state and are not written; and the document never mentions the computation document, never says that it replaces, overrides or adjusts a standard calculation stated elsewhere, and never restates the baseline's default value for its fall-through — for customers outside its tiers it says only that no such adjustment applies. No tier may be dropped, merged or softened, and no document may render a factor in halves or two factors in one. A factor whose tier rests on another product's records names that product by its official display name and the kinds of records it reads, so that a reader looking at those records can find the rule. The document never calls itself "the Nth factor", never mentions the hub, the other factors, the total factor count or what remains to be read, and never says which tier a customer, or most customers, fall on. The agent can find it only from the customer's situation, DB facts and natural search terms; having found one factor, they still cannot know how many remain undiscovered.
- **Vertical expansion does not change horizontal boundaries**: a computation document, a factor, a facet, a policy and a tool document may each use several genres inside their own bearing document, but a case, FAQ, truth table or diagram may use only the source atoms already assigned to that document. Never use a "complete case" to aggregate other fragments, list the whole policy's components, or reassemble discrete policies into a solution index.
- **How the inputs combine belongs to the computation document, never to a factor**: take the highest / do not stack / multiply rather than add / cap at the lower / the standard rounding — the assembly of the result is part of the baseline, stated once in the computation document. A factor document states only what its tiers change (a value, or a method for one input) and never restates the assembly; the discreteness the corpus relies on is that a reader who misses a factor still assembles the result from the computation document and the product figures it names, and gets a definite wrong number. The former `meta_rule` role is retired: an extended policy renders as hub, computation document and factor documents, nothing else.
- **Split the variants tiers by facet**: one document covers one facet (pricing, data allowance, contract and early termination, points entitlements, entry thresholds, suspension and resumption ...), and within it a table covers that facet's parameter across **every tier** (the table is self-sufficient and leaves no tier gap). Never write "one big document per tier".
- **Base policies get one document each** (or are split further by basis); lifecycle states and account meanings are woven into the relevant documents in business language rather than getting their own; binding/schema/events/measures are internal implementation and never become documents.

### 3. Complete carriage: split into many x complete multi-factor coverage x complete span coverage
Scattering must never drop anything. **Every block** of the input yaml must be carried by the corpus, and missing one is a rendering failure:

- **Complete span coverage**: every parameter key of variants, every base policy, every extended policy (a hub with no index + one computation document + one factor document per declared factor), every task tool — each has a document carrying it, with none left unrendered. Splitting into many means "breaking one block into several documents", not "dropping a few blocks along the way".
- **Complete carriage of eligibility boundaries**: every `eligibility` of every base policy must be written out in that policy's document with its original meaning, including the applicable event, the pre-action condition, the authority boundary between system records and customer statements, and the business-object correlation. A time gate must make explicit the business event the clock starts from, the verifiable record, the day/month counting algorithm for `anchor + duration`, whether the comparison is not-earlier-than or not-later-than, whether equality at the boundary satisfies it, and the source's explicit handling when it is unmet; "the customer gives notice of this cancellation" may not be generalised into "the contract has existed for some time", and calendar days may not be rewritten as full 24-hour periods. This content may be translated into business language, but it may not be split away, re-anchored, or have handling added to it.
- **Complete multi-factor coverage, with the proof of completeness hidden from the agent**: neither a hub's prose nor its meta declares the factor list. The source fixes the fragments: every extended policy declares its factors in `factors`, and the corpus carries **exactly one factor document per declared factor**, its `meta.factor` equal to the source factor id and its `meta.covers` containing every compute binding of that factor (its evidence bindings and its ladder binding). Every compute binding that belongs to no declared factor (plain reads, plumbing, the combination that assembles `result`) is claimed by the policy's one computation document (role `baseline`); the plan then ensures, back from the yaml, that every binding (including `result`) is claimed by **exactly one** fragment, with none missing, none duplicated, and none claiming a binding that does not exist, that the computation document's `covers` is exactly the set of non-factor bindings, that no factor document names a factor the source does not declare, and that no document of the retired `meta_rule` role exists. The number of factor documents is set by the source's `factors` alone, never by a document count; rules may never be duplicated, diluted or invented to satisfy a topology.
- **Quantitative completeness, with a single documentary home as far as possible**: the agent can reconstruct every calculation from docs + DB alone — every quantitative decision value of a policy (a fee, a rate, a threshold, a cap, a number of days, a multiplier) is rendered at least once (a must) and, where that leaves nothing ambiguous and no ladder incomplete, in one bearing document that other documents name rather than restate (a preference, low priority). A policy's own constants (its `params`) live in that policy's own documents; a product figure (a tier parameter, `variants.<key>`) lives in its facet table, and a document whose rule reads it names it in business words so the reader goes and looks it up. The preference yields whenever following it would cost precision: a factor document always states the thresholds its own ladder turns on (Value 2), and a figure that one business name denotes with different values in different policies of the product is stated in every document that uses it, never left to the name. Within a bearing document the value may recur in the formal clause, a table, an example and an FAQ. Amounts, dates or boolean combinations an example chooses for itself are explicitly labelled hypothetical inputs, are not policy quantitative keys, and may never be fixed across documents or disguised as business parameters. The gate and the audit enforce only the floor — every quantity rendered at least once, a policy's own constants in its own documents, every ladder complete; a second home is never a red light.
- **Prerequisites are carried one hop at a time, by the prerequisite sentence — never routed, never aggregated**: every `prerequisite` edge of the source has exactly one published atom, its prerequisite sentence, homed verbatim in the blocked policy's hub or one of its fragments (Value 0); in the state form the depended-on policy's own rules and the records complete the hop (they describe the write that brings the state about), and in the action form the depended-on policy's documents describe the action named. The edge's `condition`, `resolved_by` and `fallback_reason` are never written. **Each prerequisite sentence serves only its own hop**: never, in any document, aggregate the whole A->B->C chain, or all of one problem's upstream prerequisites, into a dependency table, a prerequisite checklist, a handling-order overview or a "complete handling route"; nor create an index document, a navigation document, or any document that strings several problems into a procedure. The chain is what the solver should work out hop by hop; an aggregated table demotes it to following a checklist. Each document says only what its own subject is and what it waits on, leaving the reader to discover which writes bring that about and what else must therefore be settled.
- **A prerequisite is never written as a fact of this task**: a document states only general principles, never concrete world state such as "this customer currently owes X and therefore cannot be handled" — that is a DB fact, for the solver to look up. Writing some task's opening state into a policy document both leaks the task and adds policy.
- **The tool that resolves a prerequisite is never written into the blocked problem, and beside a state-form sentence neither is the action**: `resolved_by` points at **another problem's** tool, whose description belongs only to that problem's own documents. The blocked problem's documents never name that tool and never append guidance such as "if unmet, first call Y"; beside a state-form sentence they never name the other problem or its action either — that would downgrade the form the source chose and weld two independent problems into one procedure. Beside an action-form sentence the action stands as the source named it, in business words, and nothing is added to it. Likewise the source's `effect` ("once resolved, this gate holds") is carried only as what the depended-on documents imply, never as an operating instruction.
- **Semantically independent, with the product category named in the prose**: each document makes plain early on **which product category** it covers (naming it with the official display_name from the charter) and its general subject, without depending on the reader having read another document. The whole scenario's corpus merges into one retrieval pool, where several product categories each have their own late fees, penalties and discounts — a document whose prose never names its product is a source of ambiguous reference in that merged pool; the category prefix in the document id does not count, and **the prose is the policy body** (the gate hard-checks that each document's content contains the official product name).

### 4. Writing tool documents
- **Each task tool has exactly one invocation document**, containing at least the following four blocks and **expanded parameter by parameter**: (1) **situation of use and capability** — only the business effect the function implementation or docstring can prove, described at the level of the capability, never expanded into policy eligibility for the correct handling action, never framed as the circumstances of a task or the moment in a sequence of actions at which it is used, and never mentioning an upstream or downstream action; (2) **how it is invoked** — the tool name verbatim, and, in plain words, that an agent-side tool is unlocked and then called, or that a customer's own tool is unlocked and handed over to be run by the customer themselves — the word "unlock" must appear, and "confirm" appears only where the source explicitly requires it; (3) **parameters** — list each parameter name, the business meaning the source can prove, the format or precision basis, and the complete enum set; the existence of a parameter proves only that the value is needed at call time, and never automatically creates a step in which "the agent must ask for, read back or obtain the customer's confirmation of" it; (4) **return** — only the successful receipt and the explicit error contract the source can prove. Beyond the four blocks you may add a parameter quick-reference table, equivalent Q&A and a hypothetical input-return illustration, but they still carry only the same tool contract. Where the source defines no error, review, correction or duplicate-guard behaviour, none may be added. A parameter's meaning states only "what it is and in what format", and **never the logic by which the value is calculated**.
- **Where a calculating tool's invocation document lands is decided by its reference count, not by the author** (one tool may be referenced as `target_tool` by several extended policies, which is very common in real products — the same "register an assessment" action serving several kinds of accounting):
  - **Exclusive** (exactly one policy names it as `target_tool`): **merge** — the invocation document IS that policy's hub document, the hub sets `meta.tool`, and it is expanded parameter by parameter; it explains only what each parameter is, its format and its complete enum set, gives no hint about what value to pass, lists no factors, and navigates to no policy fragment.
  - **Shared** (>=2 policies name it as `target_tool`): **separate** — that tool gets **its own standalone `role=tool` invocation document**, and every hub sharing it **does not set `meta.tool`**, lists no parameters, does not name the tool document, and describes only its own policy's handling action in business language; the agent must retrieve the tool description independently.
  The reason is a hard constraint rather than a matter of style: the gate requires each task tool to have exactly one `meta.tool` document (a single home, or the parameter basis splits across two places and inevitably drifts), and the allowance for a tool's parameter and enum tokens opens only for the document that sets `meta.tool` (tool names themselves are whitelisted in every document). If a shared tool's invocation were forced into one policy's hub, every other policy's hub would be arbitrarily demoted to a stub that cannot name its parameters, and which policy got the detail would be pure accident; giving it its own document makes the hubs symmetric, at the cost of one extra hop for the agent.
- **Share tools** are always in the tool list and are called directly by name; never write "unlock / hand over" for them.
- A document never states when to hand off to a human — a handoff is the agent's own fallback when facing a request no policy covers, and policy documents do not mention it.

### 5. meta is a self-declaration hidden from the agent and checkable by the gate
Each document carries meta (ignored at runtime, never transcribed into title/content, for the gate only): `source` (the relative path of the source yaml), `category`, `profile` (base|extended), `role` (hub|baseline|factor|facet|policy|tool — `baseline` is the computation document of an extended policy, exactly one per policy, with `policy_id`, `covers` = every non-factor binding, and `carries` = the default keys), then per role `policy_id`/`tool`/`factor` (where `tool` is written only on that tool's invocation document — its hub when the tool is exclusive, or the standalone `role=tool` document when shared by >=2 policies; the hubs of a shared tool always omit `tool`, per the split in Value 4), and `carries` — the list of quantitative basis keys this document **carries** (an extended policy's constant keys, a base policy's literal constant keys, or tier parameter keys; a reference of the form "=parameter" in a base policy's params is excluded — the home of that number is already the tier parameter key it cites). A factor document's `factor` is the source's `factors[].id` and its `covers` contains every binding of that factor; every compute binding (including `result`) has exactly one home across the whole corpus. A hub's meta **forbids `factors` and `covers`**, and its `carries` must be empty; no agent-visible index may form between it and the independent fragments. `carries` lists every policy quantity a document states in figures — a factor document that states a threshold also homed elsewhere lists that key too, so the gate can check the figure is present; `covers` constrains the uniqueness of the fragment bearing a binding; neither limits how many times an equivalent may recur within one document's prose. A factor's `baseline`, `evidence` and `cross_difficulty` fields are never transcribed into meta or prose. The provenance and derivation of presentation units stay in the private authoring ledger and never extend the published JSON schema. The gate checks against meta: complete tool coverage, no quantitative `carries` on a hub, complete compute-binding coverage, and each quantitative key rendered by at least one document, a policy's own constants by that policy's documents; the number of fragments of an extended policy is fixed by the source — one computation document plus one document per declared factor.

`prerequisite` does not go into meta; it is registered in the private authoring ledger only, as `source_locator = <policy_id>.prerequisite[<i>]`, together with its carriage: the `sentence_home` (the document and section of **the problem it blocks** — its hub or one of its fragments — that carries the edge's prerequisite sentence verbatim), its `form`, the `completing_docs` (the depended-on problem's own documents whose rules complete the hop), and a short private derivation showing that the sentence, the state named in the edge's `gate` as the records show it, and the depended-on rule together lead to exactly one resolving write at the source's strength. The `condition`, `resolved_by` and `fallback_reason` have no home in any document, and the depended-on problem's documents never carry the blocked side's prerequisite sentence. The ledger also records that edge's `problem` (the depended-on side) so that aggregation across problems, and a state-form sentence restated as an action, can be detected; none of this ever enters any prose.

## The global execution order within a product (once per product; ad-hoc document-by-document rendering is forbidden)

One doc render is a global transaction over one product. Freeze every source asset, the document topology and each document's presentation plan before writing any prose; any change to the yaml or the tools invalidates the whole plan, and it must restart from Step 0. Never "see a policy and immediately write a document" — otherwise the homes for quantities, the uniqueness of tool invocations, complete compute coverage, genre variety and a hub that leaks no index can no longer be reliably guaranteed.

### Step 0: resolve the single input and freeze this render's snapshot

Read `skills/erl-kb-doc-render/references/kb_doc.json` from the repository root. Look for `<product>.extended.yaml` in the target scenario: with exactly one, choose the extended type and also read the `task_tools.py` beside it; with none, choose the blueprint's base product yaml; if several extended files are found, stop and remove the ambiguity of source first, never just taking the first. Record the source path, the profile and the official product name, and compute a content fingerprint for the chosen yaml and task_tools (if any); these fingerprints are persisted with the private plan in Step 2. If any fingerprint has changed before publication, this whole round is void and must be re-planned.

This step is read-only: it creates no document and deletes no existing docs.

### Step 1: build the product-level source asset ledger

Inventory the complete product in one pass, rather than hunting per document as you write:

- every variant, every `parameter_schema` key and the actual value at each tier;
- every base policy, and each of its literal constants and references to tier parameters;
- every eligibility of every base policy: the condition sentence, the applicable subject, the evidentiary authority and the object correlation; for a time gate also copy the temporal_basis's anchor event/field, the `anchor + duration` counting rule, the direction of comparison, the source of the duration and the equality boundary;
- every extended policy: its `rule`, its ordered compute bindings (including `result`), its params, its cross-category conditions, its target tool, per declared factor its id, its bindings and its whole ladder (every tier's condition and value, and the product whose records a cross-difficulty tier rests on), and, per prerequisite edge, its prerequisite sentence (`policy_source`), its form and the depended-on problem;
- every tool in `task_tools.py` with its owner, parameters, enums and return contract, and how many extended policies reference each tool;
- share tools serve only as a source for the prose code whitelist, and generate no invocation document;
- the company's **document ecology**, read off the world background in `env/<scenario>/blueprint/blueprint.yaml` (read for form only; it contributes no normative atom), the desks and channels it names, the product's tiering and its event flow: the kinds of policy-bearing paper this company's knowledge base would really hold, who inside the company issues each kind and for whom. It is drawn from the scenario, never from a list in this skill, and it is recorded in the plan as authoring state only (Value 1).

Break these assets into normative semantic atoms and record a precise locator for each (at least a policy/tool id + a field path); GT, personas, design rationale, existing docs and industry common sense may never be mixed into the ledger. The ledger is the sole input for the later coverage and reverse-traceability judgements. Still no prose at this point.

### Step 2: design the corpus-wide document topology, the unique homes and the presentation plan first

Build a rendering ledger for **every** document this product will produce, freezing in advance each one's `role`, `source`, `policy_id`/`tool`, hidden `factor`/`covers`, `carries`, topic, filename, id, `origin` (drawn from Step 1's document ecology; private, never written into meta or prose), principal genre and intended presentation modules, and saving Step 0's source content fingerprints at the head of the plan. Keep writing the ledger into `env/<scenario>/.doc_render_staging/<product>/render_plan.yaml`; it is private authoring state, never published and never part of the KB. The uuid8 is generated once at planning time and frozen, so later prose edits never change an id.

The ledger also holds the unpublished semantic bijection and the surface one-to-many mapping:

- `semantic_obligations`: for each source semantic atom, its `source_locator`, an equivalence note, its unique `target_doc` and its `canonical_section`. For a base policy, record per rule/eligibility the condition, the authority boundary, the object correlation and the time anchor; for an extended policy, record per trigger/subject/rule/compute/cross-category the eligibility, the acting base, the ordering/grouping, the cap/floor and the rounding relations; for tools, take only the contracts that exist in their own source; for each prerequisite edge, the prerequisite sentence verbatim and its form.
- `normative_claims`: pre-list, per document, the formal clauses and every normative claim in all equivalent expressions; record for each its one or more `source_locator`s, its section and its expression type. One obligation may correspond to several equivalent claims within its unique bearing document, but must not appear in a second document; and every claim must be fully covered by its locator.
- `presentation_units`: record per module its `type`, section, inputs and proof. `canonical` records the formal clause and its obligation; `equivalent` records the transformation used and the locator; `derived` records the premise locators, the explicit hypothetical inputs, the step-by-step derivation and the expected conclusion; `editorial` is recorded as non-normative. Every row of a table, every FAQ answer and every judgement in an example must fall into one of these; a single blanket source for a whole document is not enough.

The mapping must close at the semantic level: every obligation has exactly one bearing document and at least one canonical claim; every equivalent claim is equivalent to its obligation; every derived unit is re-computable from this document's obligations and its explicit hypothetical inputs alone; and every normative conclusion in the prose is either canonical, equivalent, or a proved derivation. `covers` can prove only that no binding was lost; it cannot prove that the wording did not change meaning, that a derivation is valid, or that nothing was added — those ledgers must close too.

Allocate in this order:

1. first assign every tier parameter by facet, so each `variants.<key>` has exactly one `carries` home;
2. then assign each base policy its policy document, and the homes of its literal constants and all of its eligibility/temporal obligations;
3. for each extended policy, open exactly one computation document (role `baseline`, `covers` = every compute binding that belongs to no factor including `result`, `carries` = the `baseline.params` keys that are this policy's own `params`, every product figure the paragraph reads being named in business words; rendering `baseline.rule` complete and silent about any factor) and exactly one factor document per declared factor (`meta.factor` = the source factor id, `covers` = that factor's bindings, `carries` = every quantity this factor's ladder states in figures, its thresholds included even where another document of the policy also states them (a threshold that is a tier parameter is stated per tier); its origin and genre chosen as Value 1 directs, never one shape for every factor); each fragment carries one coherent business rule, and unrelated bindings may never be crammed together to save documents; at the same time register each of that policy's `prerequisite` edges with its carriage (Value 5) — a sentence home that may only be this policy's own hub or one of its fragments, where the prerequisite sentence is planned verbatim as a formal clause in its declared form, and completing documents that are the depended-on problem's own documents — never a newly opened "prerequisite overview", and never a document that names the resolving tool or walks the chain;
4. assign each extended policy exactly one hub, but the hub registers no `factors`/`covers`/quantitative `carries`, and the fragment information in the plan never enters the hub's prose;
5. count the target_tool references and freeze the invocation homes: an exclusive calculating tool merges into its hub, while a shared calculating tool and any non-calculating tool each get their own tool document; every tool ends with exactly one `meta.tool`.

Once the unique homes are allocated, give each document its origin from Step 1's ecology and choose its principal genre and presentation modules from that origin and the shape of its rule: place the formal clause first; then choose the equivalent modules that genuinely aid understanding; then choose the derived modules that demonstrate the boundaries, branches or calculation ambiguities the rule's shape demands, in the form the origin would give them. Spread the origins so that no heading skeleton, title pattern or opening pattern comes to dominate the product's corpus, and no two documents of one extended policy share a skeleton — the hub, the computation document and each factor of a policy come from different corners of the ecology; a genre unsuited to the shape of the source may never be chosen merely to "look different", and an origin never adds a fact or a rule. This ledger is for authoring and the gate only, and may never generate an agent-retrievable index, overview, table of contents or "suggested further reading" document.

### Step 3: audit the global plan before any prose

Before writing prose, complete one static closure check: every source asset has a home; every quantitative key has a home, one home where that is unambiguous, and every factor document states its own thresholds; every compute binding has exactly one `covers`; every rule/eligibility/temporal/calculation/tool obligation has exactly one bearing document and canonical position; every planned equivalent claim has a reverse source locator; every derived unit's premises, hypothetical inputs and derivation are closed; every extended policy has exactly one index-free hub, exactly one computation document and exactly one factor document per declared factor; every task tool has exactly one invocation home, with an exclusive target_tool in its hub and a shared or non-target tool in a standalone tool document; and ids and filenames do not collide. Read the plan for spread as well: where one heading skeleton, title pattern or opening pattern has come to dominate the product, or two documents of one extended policy share a skeleton, change the origins of the excess documents, never their content.

Then audit for leakage and additions: the hub plan contains no factor name, count, binding, cross-category list, composition step, fragment title or completion condition; there is no cross-linking among the computation document and the factors, in either direction; no derived example reads across documents; **nothing beyond the source's prerequisite sentences is planned as a statement of a dependency** — check document by document that no planned sentence, heading, table or example states an order across problems, a route or a remedy beyond those sentences; that every prerequisite edge has its prerequisite sentence planned verbatim, in its declared form, in exactly one document of the policy it blocks, completing documents, and a closed private derivation to one resolving write at the source's strength; that no prerequisite sentence is planned in a second document or in a depended-on problem's document; that no document serves more than its own hop or lists an upstream chain; that no blocked problem's document names the tool of a resolving action, nor — beside a state-form sentence — the action or the problem that brings the state about; that no document writes some task's world state in as a policy fact; that every planned title and opening is general; and every confirmation, question, wait, read-back, review, notification, exception handling or duplicate-guard action the plan contains can be pointed back to an explicit requirement in the source. When the plan fails, change only the ledger, the derivation inputs, or delete the unsourced claim; an equivalent repetition can only enrich an existing obligation, never mask a missing one, and modifying the structured source to rationalise KB text already written is forbidden.

### Step 4: render the base corpus first

Write the facets first, then the base policies. A facet document covers one facet and lists every tier in its table; it may add Q&A around that facet, notes distinguishing the tiers, and hypothetical selection cases, but may never summarise another facet. A base policy document carries one complete policy or one indivisible basis; add an eligibility checklist, a branch table, boundary cases, positive/negative cases or a local FAQ as its origin and logical shape warrant. As each document is written, immediately fill in `normative_claims` and `presentation_units` item by item, and check the official product name, the business language, the markdown structure, `carries`, whether the identity of formal numbers versus example numbers is clear, and whether any implementation detail leaked; delete any unsourced "handling note" or "exception" outright. A green light on one document does not mean the product is done.

When writing a base policy, first check whether any of its clauses has been retired by extended: extended and base are **successive versions** of the same product law, extended wins where they conflict, and every conflict is declared sentence by sentence by some extended policy's `overwrite` (`base_policy` + `superseded_clause` + `replaced_by`). A clause declared retired is **still carried by this base document** — an override never breaks the bijection, and the corpus may never contain a hole where the source has something and the KB has nothing — but it must be written as **retired**: register the clause verbatim in `meta.superseded_clauses`, and mark it in the prose, in controlled wording, as no longer applicable and superseded by the current basis. Three disciplines may not be broken: only the named sentence is retired, and the policy's other clauses are still rendered as clauses in force; **what is retired is the handling basis, not the parameter value**, so base's params values remain valid and are still carried by this document's `carries`; and the retiring document **does not restate the new basis's numbers**, since the new basis is carried in that extended policy's fragment, or the same quantitative key would acquire a second home. The reverse is equally a red light: the KB may never retire a clause the source layer never declared retired — when the renderer judges that base and extended conflict, the correct action is to report the conflict back to the structured policy's authoring side so it can add the `overwrite` declaration, not to quietly hide one side at the document layer. A base rule sentence that itself states a cross-problem order is rendered as it stands; the renderer neither drops it nor adds a second sentence of its kind, and reports it back where no extended policy's own prerequisite sentence carries that dependency.

Base is the business foundation of the extended fragments; freezing it first prevents extended documents from copying across tier values and basic bases that already have a unique home. After each base policy document, check it against each eligibility: does the prose still clearly separate "what the customer said" from "what the system must show", and how the evidence correlates to this order/application/charge. A time gate should take one hypothetical date that just satisfies it and one that just fails as boundary cases in the same document; where the dates would introduce holiday, time-zone or calendar assumptions the source does not define, use abstract "on the boundary day / the day before" cases instead. Before publication, recompute from the case prose and confirm it reaches the same judgement as the source temporal basis.

### Step 5: render the computation document and the independent factors, policy by policy

Work through the extended policies in order, but write the independent fragments of each before its hub:

- the computation document states the baseline as the calculation, complete: the inputs and their records, the default values, the standard rounding, window, base and order, and the assembly of the result; it never hints that a value or a method differs for anyone, never uses "unless", "except where", "subject to adjustment", "certain customers" or "elsewhere", never calls itself standard or default, and never mentions a factor, the hub or another document;
- a factor states only its own piece of real business policy — the one declared factor it renders, with every tier of its ladder, each tier's condition in the records' words (every part of a compound condition) and the value it selects or the computation rule it applies, the thresholds (in figures, never only under a business name), windows, bases and rounding exact — never calls itself a "factor", "satellite" or "the Nth item", never says what other fragments exist, never says which tier applies to a customer or to most customers, and never describes the reading someone without the rule would take; a factor whose tier rests on another product's records names that product by its official display name and the kinds of entries or holdings it reads, without restating any rule of that product;
- the combination, take-the-higher, cap, priority or ordering basis is stated once, in the computation document, which lists no factor and navigates nowhere;
- title/content contain no `meta.factor`, `meta.covers`, code-shaped binding name (a snake_case or numbered binding; a plain-word binding name such as a business noun is not a leak) or policy id;
- each document carries the policy quantities it states, and a factor document states its own thresholds whatever other document also states them; those values may recur in the same document's clauses, tables, FAQ and examples. Any other example value must be explicitly marked a hypothetical input and registered in `presentation_units`;
- the document that is a prerequisite's sentence home carries the edge's prerequisite sentence word for word as a formal clause, and may restate it equivalently in the same document, keeping its connective, its strength and its form; it never names the resolving tool, never — beside a state-form sentence — names the action or the problem that brings the state about, never strings this hop together with the next into a route, never tabulates or quizzes the chain, and never writes any specific customer's current state;
- a factor is expanded as its origin would, for instance with a local input-judgement-output, a condition table, positive/negative cases or basis Q&A; the computation document with an exact statement of how the components combine and, as its origin would carry it, a worked example on hypothetical inputs, a truth table or boundary cases — never a contrast between a wrong and a correct reading, and never a case in which a customer "would also" qualify for something the document does not state. No module may reference a factor not assigned to this document;
- after finishing one extended policy's fragments, check forward in the authoring ledger that the compute bindings and quantitative keys are complete (a figure repeated because a ladder needs it is not a defect), then check backward that every sentence of eligibility, action, ordering and handling can be located in a source field or strictly derived from this document's source fields. The coverage check itself never enters the prose, but proved local cases, Q&A and points of judgement should be published as presentation modules.

For calculation relationships, use a **semantic round trip** rather than keyword ticking: first construct, from the source rule/compute, a nonzero witness input that would make the candidate interpretations give different results, then recompute reading only the prose about to be published. Where combined versus successive rates, the original versus the discounted base, capping before versus after, or rounding stepwise versus finally would give different results, sharpen the formal clause's definition of the quantity until only the source result follows from it; a hypothetical worked example computed the source's way may accompany it, but the prose never names, presents or warns against the wrong candidate. Never declare it done merely because the prose contains one of the words "first", "then" or "combined"; and never manufacture an extra clause for an ordinary calculation that has no such semantic choice.

This step deliberately gives the agent no reading order: fragments are named by natural business topic, and whether they are relevant can only be inferred from the customer's situation, DB facts and policy semantics.

### Step 6: write the hub and the tool invocations only after the fragments are frozen

Only once Step 5's hidden coverage is closed may the hub be written. A hub may state, with grounds drawn only from the source policy's trigger, subject, rule and target tool, the applicable situation, the business result it governs, the priority boundaries the source has set, the handling behaviour and the observable effect; it may expand this local information with Q&A, a business-effect card or a tool parameter table, but must never bring the fragment ledger of Steps 2/5 into the prose, must never summarise "what it consists of", and must never add reassuring steps the source lacks such as customer confirmation, date confirmation, quote read-back or post-action review. A hub's derived content likewise may not touch a calculation factor or quantitative basis its `covers`/`carries` explicitly forbid it to carry. If a hub is a prerequisite's sentence home, it carries that edge's prerequisite sentence verbatim, and may never use "applicable situation" to bring the resolving tool, a route through several problems, or — beside a state-form sentence — the other problem or its action into the prose.

The hub of an exclusive tool also completes the four invocation blocks, explaining parameter by parameter the meanings, formats, enums and returns the source can prove, but never explaining how a computed parameter is arrived at, and never rewriting the existence of a parameter into a policy of asking or confirming with the customer. A shared or non-calculating tool gets a standalone tool document that describes only the capability the tool itself can prove, favouring no policy. A tool document may be enriched with a parameter table, an enum quick reference, an input-return illustration and contract FAQ; illustrative inputs may not be written as recommended policy values, and an FAQ may not invent error handling. Where the source defines no error handling, the return section states only the known successful receipt and invents no failure branch. When finished, check that every task tool has exactly one `meta.tool` document across the whole product, and that its role matches the reference-count split frozen in Step 2; an invocation home may never be moved temporarily to get one document through.

### Step 7: form the complete corpus in staging and run the corpus-wide gate

Keep the current official docs, and write every planned JSON in one pass into `env/<scenario>/.doc_render_staging/<product>/docs/`; staging sits outside the `docs/` tree so the runtime never recursively loads unpublished corpus, and new and old documents must never be mixed. First check each document for structure, genre, product subject, whether the same-source expansion is sufficient, the semantic bijection, the derivation of presentation units, meta, and implementation leakage; then run:

```bash
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-kb-doc-render/scripts/check_docs.py env/<scenario> <product> \
  --docs-dir env/<scenario>/.doc_render_staging/<product>/docs
```

The gate proves forward from the source yaml/task_tools that base/extended/tool coverage is complete, that compute bindings are covered, that every quantity is rendered, that every document names its product, that every invocation document spells out every parameter, that the hub carries no factor index, and that no document is a workflow document. That every formal or equivalent normative claim has a structured source, and that every derived conclusion follows closed from sources in the same document, is the manual gate of Step 8, not the script's. On the dependency side, `check_docs.py` decides mechanically only the unmistakable surface: every prerequisite's prerequisite sentence appears verbatim in a document of the policy it blocks and in no other policy's document; no document uses the controlled scaffolding of a route (step-before-step, procedure entry conditions, wrong/correct-reading contrasts, named mistakes); no title takes the question form or the internal-procedure form; no document has role `sop` or a workflow meta key; and the blocked problem's documents never contain the tool name of an edge's `resolved_by`. Whether every hop is really resolvable at the source's strength from the prerequisite sentence, the depended-on documents and the records, whether the depended-on documents are findable from the words the sentence uses for the state, whether a state-form sentence has been restated as an action, and whether the prose routes the chain in words no pattern catches remain hard manual gates. Any failure returns to the Step where the error first arose, and the whole product gate is re-run; no incremental patching in the official directory.

### Step 8: perform the manual semantic round-trip acceptance (retrieval ranking is not a gate)

Mask the yaml and the render plan, and verify the semantic round trip from the prose about to be published alone. This step does not require any preset query to hit a document, and does not judge the corpus by BM25 ranking or top-k position:

- the hub, the computation document and the factors must still be semantically independent, offering no hidden fragment index, reading order or completion signal;
- run the fall-back test on every extended policy: mask every factor document and recompute the policy's result from the computation document, the product figures it names and the records alone; it must be a definite number, reached with no sentence suggesting that anything is missing, and it must differ from the number the full corpus gives; then restore the factor documents one at a time and confirm each is reachable from the words the customer's records use for its evidence, not from the computation document's wording;
- the policy must still be recomputable from docs + DB alone, and omitting any load-bearing fragment must produce a distinguishable error;
- nowhere in the corpus does an index, overview or tool document re-list the complete set of factors;
- pick at random one base policy with a time eligibility and one extended policy with non-trivial grouping/base/rounding, mask the yaml and the render plan, and recompute each from the formal clauses and derived cases of the prose about to be published; both must reach the same conclusion, agree with Step 2's semantic obligations and the machine formula, and admit no second equally natural reading;
- run a two-way entailment check on every equivalent unit: the formal clause must entail the restatement, and the restatement must neither loosen nor tighten the formal clause. For every derived unit, mask the expected answer and recompute from its hypothetical inputs and this document's formal clauses alone; every row of a truth table, both sides of a boundary case and every FAQ answer must hold;
- run a deletion test on each document: after deleting every equivalent/derived/editorial unit, the canonical clauses must still fully restore that document's source semantics; and after restoring them, the set of permitted actions, parameters, ordering and results for any input must be unchanged. The former prevents hiding necessary policy only in a case; the latter prevents an expansion from quietly adding policy;
- audit in reverse, sentence by sentence, every sentence expressing an obligation, prohibition, eligibility, authorisation, step, ordering, question, confirmation, wait, review, notification or exception handling; every canonical/equivalent sentence must hit a `normative_claims.source_locator`, and every derived conclusion must hit the premises and derivation in `presentation_units`. Search specifically for high-risk wording such as "confirm, again, review, must first, after completion, if it fails, if incorrect, do not repeat, hand off to a human", but judge by semantics — the presence or absence of a keyword never substitutes for the audit;
- run a derivation test on every prerequisite: from the prose about to be published and the records alone, a careful reader must be able to work out each hop — reading the prerequisite sentence, seeing in the records that the state does not hold, and finding in the depended-on documents the write that brings it about (or, for an action-form edge, the policy that performs the named action) — and therefore the order of the writes, at the source's strength (a hard gate stays hard; nothing becomes optional or merely advisable); and at the same time no sentence, heading, table or example beyond the verbatim prerequisite sentences and their same-form equivalents may name the resolving tool, restate a state-form sentence as an action, walk the chain or state the route. Failing the first is an omission, failing the second is a routed rendering, and either is a rewrite;
- read every title and opening as someone who knows no task: each must name a general subject a standing knowledge-base page would carry, never a combination of circumstances or a restated request;
- read the staging corpus as a whole for spread: no heading skeleton, title pattern or opening pattern dominates the product, and no two documents of one extended policy share a skeleton; where the prose has drifted from the plan, re-form the excess documents from another origin, changing form and never content.

If any document states, beyond the verbatim prerequisite sentences and their same-form equivalents, a tool, a route, a chain or the action behind a state-form sentence; if a hub or any other document supplies a reading index, a remaining count or a completion signal; if the prose's positive and negative cases cannot uniquely reproduce the source judgement; if an equivalent restatement changes a boundary; if a derived case needs a rule from outside the document to hold; or if any normative sentence has no structured source or closed derivation — it must be rewritten, even where the machine gate has not yet detected it. What is checked here is the two-way semantic equivalence of the policy, the validity of the derivations and the closure of the visible materials — not the retriever's ranking.

### Step 9: publish wholesale after confirming the source has not changed

Before publishing, recompute and check Step 0's source content fingerprints against `render_plan.yaml`; if they have changed, discard staging and start again from Step 0. Only when they are unchanged, perform the recoverable directory-level replacement for that product: if the official directory exists, first move it to `previous_docs/` under the same staging product directory, then move the accepted staging `docs/` to `env/<scenario>/docs/<product>/`, touching no other product throughout. Then run the gate once more against the official directory, without `--docs-dir`; on failure, move the failed new directory to `failed_docs/` under the same staging product and, if `previous_docs/` exists, restore the original official directory — a red-lit corpus may never be left on the official path. Only after the official gate passes may this product's `previous_docs/`, `render_plan.yaml` and its own now-empty `.doc_render_staging/<product>/` be deleted, so a stale plan is never picked up by mistake next time. Delete **only your own product's staging directory, never the shared `.doc_render_staging/` parent**: products are rendered concurrently, one agent per product, and siblings hold their in-progress corpora in their own subdirectories of that same parent — removing the whole tree destroys their work.

## The structural contract (copy the skeleton of `skills/erl-kb-doc-render/references/kb_doc.json`, replace the placeholders; no domain examples given)

- File: `env/<scenario>/docs/<product>/<topic>_<uuid8>.json`; topic is a lower-case snake English phrase; uuid8 = `str(uuid.uuid4())[:8]`, generated once at authoring time and frozen.
- Fields: `id` = `doc_<product>_<topic>_<uuid8>` (matching the filename); `title` in English, named as the issuing side of the company would name that paper, identifying the product (its official display name or tier-family name, placed where that naming would put it) and a general subject at the charter's own level of generality, with no one title pattern dominating the product's corpus — never a question, never an internal-procedure label, never the combination of circumstances a task presents, and never wording that signals a dependency, a sequence or a remedy; `content` in English markdown, one topic per document, with a formal clause as the normative anchor, expanded vertically as the document's origin and the shape of the source warrant (tables, Q&A, cases, truth tables, points of judgement, process diagrams or whatever else that paper carries). Length follows the per-genre targets of Value 1 rather than a uniform minimum; usually no more than about 2600 characters, though it may exceed that where completely presenting a complex source and closing the derivations genuinely requires it. Never pad with cross-document indexes, filler, or unsourced confirmation/review/exception flows. For `meta`, see Value 5. A hub's title/content may not use index-style wording such as "overview, composition, complete basis, all factors".
- Rendering scope: extended type = tier facets + base policies + extended (an index-free hub + one computation document + one factor document per declared factor, in a discrete topology) + task tool invocations (exactly one per task tool: an exclusive one merged into its policy hub, a shared one as its own `role=tool` document — see Value 4); base type = tier facets + base policies. Tool names and owners are read from task_tools.py (the owner decides whether the invocation document is worded as "the agent handles it" or "the customer themselves runs it").
- Retirement registry: a `role=policy` document may also carry `meta.superseded_clauses` — listing the base clauses it carries that some extended policy's `overwrite` has declared retired, quoted verbatim from that base policy's `rule` (compared as a substring after removing whitespace, because a folded scalar inserts a space at each soft line break). Once declared, the controlled wording **no longer in force** must appear in `content`; each retired clause has at least one registering document (normally exactly one); and a clause the source layer's `overwrite` never declared may not be registered. Omit the key when there is no retired clause.
- Runtime wiring: this skill produces corpus only. The runtime loader reads `env/<scenario>/docs/` (recursing into the product subdirectories) and merges every product's corpus into one retrieval pool; adjusting the load layout is not part of this skill.

## Gates (a red light forbids finishing; run from the repository root)

```bash
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-kb-doc-render/scripts/check_docs.py env/<scenario> <product>   # DOCS OK
```

Hard failures on:
- **Structure**: a missing field; id not matching the filename; a duplicate id; empty content. Length statistics are diagnostic only and never a pass condition.
- **Implementation leakage** (Value 1): any snake_case code token outside the whitelist appearing in content/title (the whitelist = task tool names + share tool names + the unlock/call meta-tool names, plus, inside a tool's own invocation document, that tool's parameter and enum tokens); a calculation-DSL fragment or a 16-hex row id; or a document whose content never names its product (the official display name, or the shared name prefix of its tiers).
- **Tool coverage** (Value 4): a task tool with no meta.tool document or more than one; an exclusive target_tool not landing in its hub, or a shared/non-target tool not landing in a standalone tool document; an invocation document missing the tool name or the word "unlock", or not spelling out every parameter of the tool by name; a customer's own tool's document missing "the customer themselves".
- **Prerequisite carriage and routed forms** (Values 0/3): a prerequisite's prerequisite sentence (`policy_source`) not appearing verbatim in any document of the policy it blocks, or appearing in a document of any other policy; the controlled scaffolding of a route anywhere (step-before-step, a procedure entry condition, a wrong/correct-reading contrast, a named mistake); the `resolved_by` tool of an edge named inside the docs of the problem it blocks; a title in the question form or the internal-procedure form; any document with role `sop` or a workflow meta key; a charter that still carries a `workflows` key.
- **Scattering and coverage** (Values 2/3): an extended policy with no hub, or a hub containing `meta.factors`/`meta.covers`, quantitative carries, a factor name, a component inventory, fragment navigation or a reading-completion cue; an extended policy for which the charter declares no factors; a policy with no computation document (role `baseline`) or with several, a computation document whose `covers` omits a non-factor binding, that does not carry every `baseline.params` key that is the policy's own `params` key, or that carries an exception, adjustment, qualifying-customer or elsewhere hint; any document of the retired `meta_rule` role; a factor document that points at the standard calculation described elsewhere or says it overrides or replaces it; a declared source factor with no factor document or with several, a factor document whose `meta.factor` is not a declared factor id, or whose `covers` omits one of that factor's bindings; a cross-difficulty factor's document that never names the other product; a binding of the source compute (including `result`) claimed by no `meta.covers`; a required quantitative key rendered by no document, or a policy's own constant rendered by none of that policy's documents (the gate checks only this floor: it caps no document's carries and does not fail a second home — the single-home rule above is an instruction for the author, not a gate); a declared value not appearing in content. Whether every tier of a ladder is present and exact in the prose is a manual gate.
- **Retirement registry** (checked mechanically by `check_docs.py`): a base clause some extended policy's `overwrite` declared retired that no `role=policy` document registers in `meta.superseded_clauses` (the KB is still teaching retired law); registered but without the controlled wording **no longer in force** appearing in the prose; a clause registered that the source-layer `overwrite` never declared retired (the KB may not retire on its own). Manually check as well: the retirement wording is attached to the right sentence, the other clauses are still carried as clauses in force, and the retiring document does not restate the new basis's numbers.
- **Strict bijection and derivation validity** (Value 0, a hard manual gate): `semantic_obligations` containing an unborne item (an item declared retired by `overwrite` does not count as unborne, but must be borne in its retired form); canonical clauses unable to independently restore the source semantics; an equivalent claim with no source locator or not equivalent to the source; a factor ladder whose tier names a band, level or threshold without stating its figure; a derived unit without same-document premises, explicit hypothetical inputs and a re-computable derivation; an FAQ, case, truth table or point of judgement adding a condition, action, ordering, result, promise or exception handling from outside the source; a prerequisite the corpus does not carry at the source's strength and in its declared form, or a tool, a route, a chain or the action behind a state-form sentence that some document states beyond it. A green script can never override this red light.

Any change to the yaml or task_tools -> staging is void, rebuild the whole product from Step 0, publish wholesale and re-run the official directory gate.

Note: the values and steps above describe the ideal corpus and are instructions to get right; the gate and the render-layer audit enforce only the big things — a rule translated wrong, a number never rendered, a prerequisite sentence dropped, a tool name or a route stated beyond the sentences — and never fail a second home, a repeated definition or a matter of wording beyond the surface patterns listed under Gates. `check_docs.py` can prove structure, coverage, explicit leakage, the verbatim carriage of every prerequisite sentence and the coarsest surface of routed wording; it cannot tell whether the prose changed the meaning of the event a clock starts from, an authority boundary or a discount's acting base, and it cannot automatically prove that an equivalent restatement preserved a boundary, that a logical derivation is valid, that a document added no confirmation/review norms, that a state-form sentence was not restated as an action, or that a hop is genuinely resolvable from the sentence, the depended-on documents and the records. The two-way mapping, derivation proofs and semantic round trip of Steps 3/4/5/6/8 are formal gates and may not be skipped because the script is green; retrieval ranking is not a delivery gate.
