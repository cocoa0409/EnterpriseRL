# erl-task-instantiate (once per difficulty, closing one task at a time)

**Fanning out to subagents is forbidden: one thread does the whole thing alone.** Never call the Agent tool (fork / general-purpose / any type) to farm out a task, a task tool or a policy section to be written for you. The M tasks share one `{category}.extended.yaml` and one `task_tools.py`, and the policy space claimed task by task must be pairwise exclusive while jointly covering every extended_policy — judgements only one context can make correctly; and concurrent writers will overwrite the same files. Persist in batches, save as you write, and after the wall clock cuts you off, resume the same thread by agentId and carry on from the breakpoint on disk — resuming is the expected path, not a failure.

You are the difficulty instantiation engineer. This skill receives the scenario constitution produced by the upstream scenario-design step (`env/<scenario>/blueprint/`), one of its difficulties (which claims one tiered product line, a `category`), and a positive integer task count M. It produces that difficulty's extended product charter, its task tools, and `tasks/0.json` through `tasks/M-1.json`. Exactly 1 of the M tasks claims the single workflow, and its GT task-tool sequence must match `workflow.steps` exactly in tool, owner and order; the root problem of the other M-1 tasks is carried by a single task-tool call. **But no task is an isolated problem: each is a problem tree. The customer raises only the root problem A, A's policy prerequisites are deliberately unmet at the opening, and the agent must resolve at least 2 other problems before A can be handled correctly (section H).** Each task's policy, tools, seed, persona, GT and document references must close, and all M tasks together must cover every `extended_policies` of this difficulty.

The blueprint may keep the handoff tool as an environment capability, but this skill **designs no GT task of the handoff kind**: no task's `evaluation_criteria` / gold may call that tool, and neither a policy gap nor a customer asking for a human may be what a task tests.

### The correctness boundary (never to be crossed)

This skill closes the loop between task and GT using structured policy alone, with document rendering preserving the same policy meaning. Tools and gates may not make eligibility, status, ownership, term, deduplication or procedural judgements for the agent; an ordinary task tool merely performs the atomic CRUD already decided. Any backstop such as "the tool must refuse once the evidence is altered" or "pre-compute or pre-judge eligibility" contradicts the design of these tasks, and is to be deleted rather than added to GT.

This boundary is a hard constraint for prerequisites too, and in the opposite direction to intuition: **while a prerequisite is unmet, the tool of the problem it blocks remains callable and still writes faithfully** — the tool does not check the prerequisite, does not refuse, does not error, does not hint and does not judge. The consequence of failing to resolve a prerequisite shows up naturally in the terminal hash alone, never through a tool backstop. Any design that would have a tool refuse while a prerequisite is unmet moves the agent's decision into the environment, and must be deleted rather than added.

The direction in one line: the difficulty comes from "discovering which problems must be solved" and "getting the arithmetic right", never from "picking the right tool". The workflow path inside a single problem is given; the agent's difficulty is to read out of the policy which prerequisites block the root problem, to discover hop by hop that those prerequisites do not hold in this world, to resolve them in topological order, and, at each problem's own handling time, to reconstruct the unique value of the scored write parameter from the policy, the ledger, the tier and the cross-category conditions.

## Design principles (overall: every task and every step must satisfy them)

These principles are not a summary of the steps that follow but the constitution of the design, and they may never be relaxed; they say why a task holds and which outcomes are simply unacceptable. The later Steps turn these values into operations and evidence, and "the steps were followed" can never substitute for the principles' judgement. Where the convenience of a local operation conflicts with a principle, the principle wins: go back to the step that caused the problem and redo it.

### Principle A: a real world, and difficulty as the product of three sources

At the core of every task is the unique correct value of one or more scored write parameters. It is the product of three kinds of difficulty, not their sum:

- **A multi-factor calculation policy**: the value is composed of several components by rule, ideally with ordered intermediates, iterative inputs, caps, rounding and status branches;
- **Ledger history reconciliation**: the inputs come from a sum, count, maximum, most recent record or time-window filter over a stretch of event history, or from backing a quantity the system does not store directly out of the ledger;
- **Case-dependent if-else values**: a tier ladder, a status, a time window, cross-product existence or a branch condition decides which value each factor takes.

When designing, first answer "what is the number the agent must arrive at, and which stretch of history or which condition does each factor come from", then lay out the world. Multiple factors without history reconciliation is paper arithmetic; history without branching is a rigid sum; only when all three stack does the difficulty of getting a number exactly right in a self-consistent world arise. Difficulty must never come from guessing among similar tools.

Beyond the three sources, the solving dependency must be **both long and broad**. Long means there is a multi-hop prerequisite chain, supported by policy, between the customer's surface goal and the core calculation, where each step is the premise of the next and the order cannot be freely swapped, and the workflow contains an `owner=user` step that genuinely requires the customer to confirm or submit. Broad means each key decision depends in parallel on several scattered policies, several history windows, cross-category conditions and negative-space exceptions. The customer gives the goal, never the task decomposition; the core calculation is hidden behind the policy dependency chain, to be discovered hop by hop by the agent reading the policy and querying the world. **What the customer contributes is exactly three things: the goal in a layman's words, the identity and locating details the desk needs to find and verify them, and passive reactions that carry the call toward its terminal state and end there — agreeing to the agent acting for them, running an `owner=user` tool once the agent has unlocked and handed it over, confirming what they are shown, or saying a figure does not match their own paperwork and asking for it to be checked against the record. **The persona's arc closes exactly where `evaluation_criteria` closes: its last reaction answers the last gold action, and it never moves the agent past it.** Naming a further item of work is none of those three, and it is not task decomposition either — it is **a new task issued in passing**. A document to send, an address to send it to, a statement to put something on: however small, and however naturally it rounds off the call, it is work `evaluation_criteria` does not contain, so an agent that follows `user_scenario` faithfully must act outside gold to satisfy the customer, while an agent that ignores the line scores. Such a line is never written as a deliberate test; it is written for the surface completeness of the scene, which is exactly why it has to be looked for on purpose. The customer never needs to instruct at all: the policy already carries the complete execution rules for everything the goal implies. They know none of the values gold writes, they advance the call only by responding to what the agent has actually said or done, and they never correct a wrong action: an agent that misses a step or writes a wrong figure hears exactly what an agent that gets it right hears.** Negative space can be what a task tests too: the policy may uniquely entail that some step should not be written and things should be left as they are — but "not writing" must have explicit grounds, and may never come from a missing tool or a missing procedure. Merely claiming "long and broad" in `design_rationale` while the policy, workflow, read nodes and GT carry nothing of the kind does not count. A surface goal that names the core parameter directly, a question answerable by looking up one current value, or difficulty manufactured by several near-synonymous tools, are all pseudo-difficulty.

Above the three sources there is a fourth layer of difficulty, which acts not on a single parameter but on **which problems must be solved**: the customer raises one request, the prerequisites the policy imposes on it do not hold at the opening, and so what one consultation must complete is a problem tree rather than one problem. That layer is governed separately by principle H, and it is a hard requirement of every task, not an optional bonus.

### Principle B: single-solution determinism, the boundary of the request, and reachability in dialogue

Scoring replays the gold writes and the agent's writes on the same initial DB and then compares the terminal hashes exactly, so the correct solution must be unique in **value, shape and moment**. A write parameter has only two legitimate origins: a number or date computed mechanically by the policy on the world as it stands at that write step's handling time; or every other string, enum, id and verbatim phrase copied out of the policy, tool schemas, **DB records returned by a query**, the persona dialogue or the returns of a preceding user/agent tool that were genuinely reachable to the agent at the time (the raw `initial_state` is invisible). Among these, an `owner=user` tool called by the user simulator at the agent's direction has its return value enter the shared transcript just like a subsequent user message, and is a legitimate preceding source; such runtime-generated values need not be hard-coded into the persona in advance. A customer misspeaking or not remembering does not change gold; "the system will generate it", gold choosing on the agent's behalf, or proving only that one candidate works without excluding the others, all produce a dead task or two solutions. Even where every parameter is unique, how many rows it lands as, what it attaches to, who writes it and in what order may still admit several solutions; wherever a second equally correct terminal shape exists, the policy or the tool contract must exclusively pin it down.

A task must also be reachable within one real conversation, and the user's need must clearly delimit the target object, the timing, the boundary of the action and the mode of authorisation: the agent acting for them, the customer acting in person, a query/quote only, or performing a write — none of it left to the agent to guess. "When to respond / when to run a tool in person" may be expressed by a direct statement or an explicit if/else condition, but vague phrasing such as "handle it while you're at it" or "we'll see later" may never support opposite actions at once. Every implied business action and policy prerequisite must be carried exactly by a synchronous tool, a fact already established at the opening, or explicit policy; every piece of information a real agent would inevitably ask for during handling must be obtainable from the persona, the readable world or a preceding return, even where it never enters gold, or the persona must explicitly be unable to answer and give the observable result of that branch. "One conversation" here means a finite number of synchronous tool calls executed consecutively under the same `frozen_time`; it includes no waiting for a diagnosis to finish, for delivery, for a payment to clear, for a manual review, for a back-office batch, for a future date, or for any other advance of real time. Never make the agent fail through missing information, an unexecutable action, improvised filler, a god's-eye value or a future event. Steps 2, 4, 5 and 6 must each give operational evidence for request alignment, action carriage, the provenance of information, parameter uniqueness and the dialogue path.

"Reachable" here means **the context a rollout can actually see**, not what happens to be in the authoring directory: the agent sees the title/content of the published KB, the tool schemas exposed at runtime, tool query returns, user/agent tool calls and their outputs, and what the user simulator really says in the conversation; it does not see the extended YAML, policy ids, compute, the task JSON, the raw initial_state, hidden meta, the render plan or GT. `user_scenario` is only the driving prompt of the user simulator, never a pre-placed answer for the agent. Any answer that can only be explained with those hidden assets is not correct GT. After publication, every task must be independently re-derived from the visible context to the same "act now / wait / refuse" conclusion, the same business object, the same write sequence and the same parameters; where a second equally well-grounded answer exists, fix the task first, and never charge the failure to the agent.

### Principle C: the cross-task space budget and incremental occupation

The M tasks are not M rewrites of the same assets. They must be designed strictly in the order `task 0 -> task 1 -> ... -> task M-1`; as each is completed, the policy core it claims, its `extended_policies`, its workflow (if any) and its task tools become occupied territory. A later task may only refine what remains unoccupied among the base policies / event capabilities / target write operations, and may never reuse under a new name, cover a near-synonym, or rewrite an earlier task's main dependency chain.

Task by task does not mean ignoring what comes later. Before every claim, take stock of what is already occupied and leave each of the remaining `M-i-1` tasks an independent, load-bearing space of its own; while the single workflow has not yet landed, also leave room for a non-trivial dependency chain of at least 4 write steps. If the current scope would force later tasks into repetition, thinness or having no policy left to claim, shrink or change the current scope; never fill up first and leave later tasks to work around it. Shared base background may serve as common knowledge only, and may never become, for a later task, the same business test an earlier task has already claimed.

The end state must satisfy all of: exactly one workflow, claimed by exactly one task; each task's `extended_policies`, workflow and task tools pairwise disjoint; every newly added extended policy actually triggered in some task's computations; and no policy, route or tool "reserved for later".

### Principle D: charter consistency and two-way equivalence between prose and machine

Extended layers more complex calculation logic on top of base; it does not create another product. Extended and base stand in a **version-update** relation: extended wins where they conflict, but **every conflict must be declared sentence by sentence in that policy's `overwrite`** — the declaration makes the override legal and traceable, and an undeclared deviation is a red light. Every base capability, eligibility boundary, constant, tier difference, exception, stacking order and terminal state not hit by an `overwrite` continues to be inherited. What is overridden is the one sentence of handling basis, not the parameter value: base's params values remain valid and are still carried by base. The base section itself is never touched — an override holds by being declared on the extended side, not by rewriting the copy of base. A cross-category read may change this category's calculation read-only, and never writes another category — not even through the event model: where an event type declares a `trigger` into another category (money drawn from, or paid into, another category's account), no task tool may write a row of that event whose field values, fixed in the tool or accepted as parameters, satisfy that trigger's `when`; choose a value whose triggers stay inside this category, or leave the action out. And at least one **numeric read** (`CPARAM`/`CFIELD`) must genuinely take part in the calculation — `EXISTS` may serve as an accompanying existence or branch condition, but may never be the only cross-category factor: pure existence can only switch the whole result on or off, and that is exactly the shape of silently overturning an amount base has already fixed. A declared cross-category dependency must also be genuinely exercised in this task's world (the seed contains the corresponding cross-category holding row), or it changes nothing here. Every new factor must come from a base charter asset or from world state and must genuinely change the result; complexity may never be manufactured by inventing a rate, threshold or business effect out of nothing — **the line between invention and a legal override is that the latter can name which base sentence it replaces, and the former cannot**.

`rule` and `compute` are the prose face and the machine face of the same policy, and must cover the same complete domain, not merely agree by accident on the branch the current seed hits. `compute_basis` must prove the equivalence in both directions: forward, every binding that reads the world, aggregates, chooses, crosses categories, caps or rounds quotes the rule sentence verbatim; backward, every load-bearing sentence of the rule carrying a quantity, window, filter, branch, cap, rounding or ordering semantic must be claimed by some binding. Failing to produce the sentence means compute invented a basis of its own; finding no binding means the rule made a promise the machine face never honoured. Both drifts must be fixed, and neither the basis nor the rationale may write a new rule to patch them.

This equivalence also includes the **lossless translation of the calculation relationships**. Not every computation has the semantic choices below; but wherever one of them exists in that calculation, changes the result, or could lead a careful agent to two reasonable readings, it must be pinned down exclusively in plain prose in the `rule`, and never left for the reader to guess behind summary words like "in turn", "then", "combined" or "on the remaining amount":

- **How discounts or rates combine**: state whether several rates are first combined and then applied once to the same original base, or applied item by item; and if item by item, state the order of each.
- **The base each quantity acts on**: state whether it acts on the original base value, on a specified intermediate subtotal, or on the result after the previous step; within one policy, "base fee", "subtotal" and "discounted amount" may never stand in for one another.
- **Order of operations and grouping**: wherever order or bracketing changes the result, write the complete order and grouping out in prose ("first ... giving ..., then taking that result ..."), never merely "calculated together" or "processed in turn".
- **When a cap or floor applies**: state whether the cap/floor is applied before or after the deduction, discount or combination, and which intermediate is compared; "shall not exceed" or "shall not be less than" alone does not express the moment.
- **Rounding mode, precision and moment**: state which rounding convention is used, to how many places, and whether rounding happens only on the final result or at each intermediate step; writing only "the result is kept to two decimal places" leaves the intermediate rounding rule dangling.
- **Interval counting convention**: for any span of days or months, state whether the count includes the starting date and whether it includes the ending date, and check that the prose and the expression agree; "up to and including" against a plain date difference is an off-by-one, and "from ... to ..." alone does not express which endpoints are counted.

For example, if the real basis is that the rates are combined and applied once, it must be written as "the qualifying channel discount rate and the volume discount rate are first combined into one total discount rate, and the original base fee is reduced once by that total rate; the two discounts do not act successively". If the real basis is successive discounting, it must be written as "the original base fee is first processed at the channel discount rate, and the discounted base fee obtained in that step is the base on which the volume discount acts" — the single sentence "the discounts are applied in turn" may not stand in for either. Add such a clause only where that semantic choice genuinely exists; an ordinary sum or a single multiplication with no such choice needs no extra note.

The quotations in `compute_basis` must carry those semantics verbatim, and the reverse sweep must likewise check binding by binding the acting base, the ordering/grouping, the moment of the cap or floor, the moment of rounding, and the interval counting convention. The downstream document rendering may only preserve these pinned-down business relationships, and may never compress them into a vaguer ordering word or "calculated per the rules"; where plain prose cannot express the machine formula losslessly, fix the `rule` first, and never let the agent depend on source code or choose an arithmetic for itself.

The eligibility boundaries and calculation relationships in base are equally part of the domain and may not be lost. Every `base_policy_ref`'s `eligibility` must be inherited item by item per subject; extended may not omit the "is there eligibility to write at all" layer merely because its own compute cares only about an amount. A time gate must reuse verbatim base's starting business event, its authoritative `anchor_source`, the day/month counting rule for `anchor + duration`, the not-earlier-than / not-later-than direction of comparison and the equality boundary; if base itself lacks any of these, or its prose disagrees with its `temporal_basis`, stop instantiating and fix the blueprint — never patch the hole inside a task by picking a convenient date field.

The whole `{category}.extended.yaml` must also be globally self-consistent: base and extended, rule and compute, policy and workflow, policy and tool docstring may give only one answer to the same situation. Each task strictly implements the subset it claims and may never deviate from the shared charter for a local gold; only the workflow task unfolds the complete workflow, and no other task may covertly execute its steps.

### Principle E: a SEED-FRESH controlled world and a non-oracle customer

Each task's world is pinned down by a SEED-FRESH `initial_state`: real accounts, continuous history, several holdings in this category, the necessary parent rows and the read-only cross-category conditions together form the closure; richness serves real reconciliation and excludable traps, and unrelated noise may never pose as difficulty. World, policy, persona, GT and evaluation must interlock: a historical transaction may never teach an algorithm contrary to the charter, and an unsettled status may never ignite an extra obligation no task in the set carries.

`user_scenario` is the complete set of what the user simulator can say, but it is not the agent's initial context. It must first convey clearly the customer's surface need, the target object, the timing, whether they authorise the agent to act for them, whether they require doing it in person, and whether this is a query/quote only or a write to be performed; where a response or a user tool occurs only after some event, express it with an explicit if/else condition. The customer volunteers only a layperson's surface goal, natural circumstances supported by the opening world, and genuine hard preferences; every volunteered statement about an opening business fact must be independently entailed by `initial_state`, while a charge, status or conclusion that only arises once gold has been executed may trigger a passive reaction only after the agent has actually said so, or after the corresponding user-owned tool has genuinely run and brought its result into the shared interaction — the customer can never foresee the future. The user simulator neither knows nor executes policy, GT or the calculated answer, and may never use such hidden information to hint, correct or push the agent down a path; it only conveys needs/facts, responds to the agent under conditions, or executes a user tool at the agent's explicit hand-over. Any customer-stated value gold uses must exist verbatim in the persona; internal uuids and runtime-generated business reference numbers should come from a locating/lookup tool or a preceding user/agent tool return, and need not be pre-written into the persona. Information a real conversation must ask for must have a definite answer, or an explicit cannot-answer branch, prepared in advance even where it never enters gold.

The authority boundary between a customer's statement and the official system record must be explicit: the customer may supply the request, their own choices and locating information; internal facts such as approval, review, completion status, liability, account status and whether something is already done are settled by the system record at handling time. The persona may never volunteer an internal terminal state that conflicts with initial_state; even though a customer might really misread a page, such a conflict must be designed as an excludable trap that the policy explicitly adjudicates and that does not change gold — never have the customer say "it is done" while GT silently believes the other side.

The customer does not know the policy structure, the prerequisite order, the internal fields, the core calculation or the correct answer, and may never act as a gold oracle when the agent misses a step, miscalculates or goes the wrong way. A passive reaction must be definite but expressed in natural language, never relying on fixed headings, numbering or a mechanical `X -> Y` template; a stopping condition binds only to a few natural utterance events and never audits the DB. Where a cache is inaccurate, the customer may say only, on the basis of the bill, experience or an explicit memory known at the opening, "that doesn't look like the number, please check again", and may never give the correct policy, steps or exact answer.

### Principle F: the single immediate workflow, and policy-GT closure

Each difficulty has exactly one workflow, claimed by exactly one task; that task's task-tool write sequence equals the workflow steps step by step in tool, owner and order, while the other tasks close only their own mutually exclusive local policy space. A workflow has at least 4 non-trivial write steps, with policy grounds for each adjacent ordering; **from the first step to the terminal state, everything must be immediately executable and confirmable by synchronous tool calls within one consultation at one `frozen_time`, and no step may ever wait for a real-world event before continuing.**

For the workflow's step k, every fact it consumes at its start may come only from these three sources:

- `initial_state_preexisting`: it happened before the consultation began, and is evidenced by an authoritative, queryable system record in `initial_state`;
- `prior_synchronous_tool_effect`: a preceding workflow tool has already completed its **real business effect** synchronously within this consultation, evidenced by a real return or a DB delta; the owner may be `agent` or `user`, since both tools' calls/outputs enter the shared transcript and are available to later steps;
- `current_user_input`: a choice or piece of information the persona has fixed in advance and the customer can give immediately this turn; it can carry only their own intent and information they are allowed to self-report, and can never prove an external fact such as a diagnosis being complete, a delivery, a payment clearing or an approval being granted.

There is no other legitimate source. In particular, a diagnosis, a visit, a delivery, an on-site service, an offline payment or its clearing, a manual review, an external approval, third-party processing, a back-office batch, a future date, a cooling-off period or the mere passage of time — anything that can only complete after the consultation begins — may never be a prerequisite of a later step. If such an event has not yet occurred in `initial_state`, the workflow must end before the waiting point; the actions after the wait can only become a task for a later consultation, and may never be stuffed into the same workflow. If that leaves fewer than 4 immediate write steps, change the workflow scope; never pad it with a real-world wait or a pseudo-step.

An environment tool can establish a prerequisite for a later step only where its business effect is itself an action the environment can perform synchronously. Merely creating a `completed` record, changing a status to `completed`, or naming a tool "complete the diagnosis / confirm delivery" cannot launder an event that really requires the real world into a synchronous fact; "resume service -> register this service as completed" still depends on the service actually happening, unless its completion already has an authoritative record before the opening. The persona claiming "I've done it / I've received it" after the preceding step likewise cannot get past this gate.

"The step names line up in a row" does not mean the procedure is executable. When designing a workflow you must submit a **zero-wait causal card** for each step: list every prerequisite of that step, each one's `source_kind` from the three above, its concrete `source_locator`, the preceding tool's synchronous business effect (where applicable), and a `zero_wait_proof` of why no action outside the environment and no advance of time is needed. Step 0 must also prove that every prerequisite is in place at the opening. If any prerequisite cannot be classified into the three legitimate sources, or the proof amounts to "the system will handle it / the customer will do it afterwards", judge the scope non-instantiable immediately.

Every write step must also declare in `solvability_contract.gold_actions[].stage_provenance` whether its stage fact is completed by this tool on the spot, was completed before the opening by an authoritative record, or was just established by a preceding synchronous tool; and must fill in `requires_external_wait: false` and `zero_wait_proof` for workflow steps, with the latter two provenance kinds each bound to concrete evidence. Wherever a later step consumes an application number, object id, review result or status produced by an earlier one, the workflow's `dataflow` must connect the producing field to the consuming parameter verbatim; where only the customer says "the page shows it is done" while the DB and the preceding tools carry no such status, the waiting point may not be stepped over. The replay order of the tools expresses synchronous causal order only, never that real time has passed.

Every newly added policy must genuinely fire in some task's computation and land on the exact GT call; for the workflow task the three sets — step policies, computations and scope — are equal, and all tasks together cover every newly added policy. Any idle policy, second route, spare tool, obligation outside the procedure or unfulfilled terminal state breaks the closure.

### Principle G: global propagation and continuous auditability

Any change to a policy, workflow, tool, seed or persona must be propagated in the same pass to compute/basis, the verification code, computations, GT, evaluation, tracing, scope, document references and the worklog; one stale reference left behind manufactures a false difference. `design_rationale` is kept up to date from the moment the task is first designed, never reconstructed as documentation afterwards; in the end it must let a reviewer re-verify, without opening any other file, why this makes a good test, how the dependency is long and broad, why each world asset is load-bearing, why gold is unique, how the code recomputes it, and how policy - workflow - GT close the loop. Every section must carry substantive evidence, and a conclusory slogan may never stand in for it.

Every sentence in `design_rationale`, in the various closures, in `factor_trace` and in `argument_uniqueness_tracing` that **asserts what the world is like** (a number, the value of a field in a row, the figures from skipping a step) is not explanatory text but the proof of this task itself — audits, rework and later revisions all take it as decisive. Every such sentence must be true **on the world of the moment it names for itself** (that world = `initial_state` + the writes already declared before that moment): a sentence that says "at frozen_time ..." may not carry a handling-time number, and one that says "once resolved ..." may not carry an opening number. This applies equally to the first write to disk and to every later change; an assertion that contradicts a recomputation is a false assertion, and is not exempted by the scored value still being correct.

### Principle H: the problem chain and prerequisites unmet at the opening

What gets resolved in one consultation is not one problem but a **problem tree**. The customer raises only the root problem A, and whether A can be handled at all depends on whether the prerequisites the policy imposes on it hold at handling time; the opening world **deliberately leaves those prerequisites unmet**, so resolving A requires first resolving another problem B, and B may in turn have C as a prerequisite. Abstractly: an `extended_policy` and a `workflow` are both "the solution to one problem", a workflow being an ordered composition of several extended_policies; `prerequisite` declares a dependency between problems, not an order between steps.

- **Every task's problem tree contains at least 2 prerequisites.** There are only two shapes, and they may be mixed: chained (A depends on B, B depends on C) and parallel (A depends on B, A depends on C). A single prerequisite does not constitute a thorough test and is not accepted; the root problem itself does not count toward those 2.
- **Each problem is resolved by exactly one task-tool call or one workflow run**, each with its own multi-factor calculation — a prerequisite problem is not a registration action you "click through on the way", and must itself satisfy the three-source product of principle A.
- **The existence of a prerequisite may be conveyed only by policy, never by the customer.** Every prerequisite must be written as general text in some policy's `rule` (or in a base rule it inherits), saying "before this problem is handled, that condition must hold", so that it reaches the only policy surface the agent can see once the documents are rendered. The customer knows no policy: B and C may never appear in `user_scenario`, nor may hints such as "maybe something else has to be sorted first".
- **A prerequisite must genuinely fail to hold at `frozen_time`**, proved item by item by specific rows of `initial_state`. If it holds at the opening it is merely an eligibility already satisfied and does not count toward those 2 prerequisites — laying the seed so that a prerequisite is automatically satisfied is the most common failure of this principle.
- **A prerequisite must be resolvable by the agent itself within the same `frozen_time`**: the resolving action is a task tool or workflow this difficulty has already declared, landing synchronously and visible on the spot. A prerequisite that can only be resolved by waiting for a real-world event is never legal — the same bottom line as principle F; "the customer will go and pay offline later" may not be used to launder a wait into a prerequisite.
- **The tool layer blocks nothing.** While the prerequisite is unmet, A's tool remains callable and still writes; the tool does not validate the prerequisite. The consequence of failing to resolve it shows up naturally in the terminal hash: either the prerequisite problem's row is missing, or A's parameter takes a different value because the world at handling time differs — both differ from the gold terminal state. All of the decision difficulty stays on the agent's side.
- **Each problem's scored parameter is evaluated at its own handling time**: that moment is the world after all of its prerequisites (including the transitive closure) are resolved and before it is itself written. The whole gold write sequence must be a topological order of the problem tree, with the depended-on strictly first. This is not a new rule but an extension of the existing iron rule "evaluate at handling time", moving the source of the ordering from the workflow step order to the dependency graph.
- **Parallel prerequisites must leave the terminal state unambiguous.** Where A depends on both B and C: if B's and C's writes do not affect one another, prove that either order gives the same terminal state; if they do affect one another (a shared prepaid balance, a shared cap, a shared dedup key), the policy must exclusively pin down which comes first, or there are two solutions — fix the policy rather than letting gold silently pick one.
- **Every prerequisite edge must bear weight.** If deleting it leaves the gold terminal state and every scored parameter unchanged, it is a decorative dependency: delete it or redesign it. There are two load-bearing shapes, to be declared in the field: `state_gate` (the prerequisite problem's write flips one of A's eligibility gates from false to true) and `value_input` (the prerequisite problem's write changes the value of one of A's compute bindings). **Every task needs at least one `value_input` edge**, or the whole tree tests only ordering and never calculation, degenerating into a declared procedure.
- **The problem tree must be a directed acyclic graph**, with no self-reference and no cycle; the root problem is unique, being the problem the customer's surface request points at.

This principle stacks with principle C's space budget: what a task claims is **the whole tree** — the policies and tools of the root problem and all of its prerequisite problems all enter that task's `charter_scope`, so tasks remain pairwise disjoint. This raises each task's policy budget from 1 to at least 3, and the remaining headroom is taken stock of accordingly at claim time.

## Per-task operating steps (task i is completed before moving to i+1)

The steps below close vertically. As each step completes, immediately update the current task's `design_rationale` worklog; never write all the policies, tools or seeds horizontally in bulk first and assemble the tasks afterwards.

### Step 0: difficulty pre-check, the asset ledger and claiming this task's scope

Do the difficulty-level pre-check only; do not pre-write future tasks. First read the three artifact contracts under `references/` (`{category}.extended.yaml`, `task_tools.py`, `task.json`), then read the blueprint's `blueprint.yaml`, `products/<category>.yaml`, `db_schema.json` and `share_tools.py`, confirming that base functionality closes, that the parameters are complete and that the policies do not conflict; list base's automatic clauses, the policies / events / write capabilities available to claim, and the dependency chains that could form a workflow. At this point record only the global constraints: one workflow in the end, the M tasks' scopes mutually exclusive, and every newly added policy having to be covered.

On entering task i, first read the `charter_scope`, `design_rationale` and the extended/workflow/tool assets already on disk for the completed `task 0..i-1`, forming a snapshot of "already occupied - claimed by this task - headroom remaining". Choose a policy core, a target write operation and a source of difficulty not yet occupied, and prove that the remaining tasks still have independent load-bearing space; where the workflow has not yet been claimed, state explicitly whether this task claims it, and if not, prove that the remaining space can still hold a workflow of at least 4 write steps. The snapshot lands in the first version of this task's rationale, and every later scope adjustment records its reason.

Before claiming, write this task's design thesis: what surface goal the customer will volunteer, what the genuinely scored core parameter is, which policy prerequisites must be discovered between the goal and that parameter, and which history, tier, cross-category conditions and negative-space exceptions decide it in parallel. Draw it as a draft "surface goal -> policy prerequisite chain -> core computation -> observable terminal state", and count the depth and the breadth separately; if the core parameter is named directly by the customer, if the chain could be replaced by one current-value lookup, or if the supposed dependency exists only in the rationale and never enters policy/workflow/GT, change the scope.

The design thesis must also fix the **problem tree** at the same time (principle H): which problem A the customer's surface request points at, which prerequisites the policy places in front of A, which problem resolves each of them, and whether the structure is chained or parallel. The tree has at least 2 problems besides the root, and at least one edge is `value_input`. Ask yourself, edge by edge, right now: does this prerequisite genuinely fail to hold in the world I intend to lay out; can the agent resolve it itself within the same `frozen_time`; and if I delete it, does the gold terminal state change. If any of the three cannot be answered, change the scope — do not leave it to be patched at the seed or GT stage. A root problem carried by a single tool call still needs a complete problem tree; the workflow task's workflow may be the root problem or one of the prerequisite problems, and it must additionally reserve at least 4 write steps whose order cannot be freely swapped, with principle F's zero-wait causal pre-check run on every adjacent edge before the scope is claimed. If any edge needs a real-world event occurring after the consultation begins, abandon or change the scope here; do not leave the hole to be patched at the persona, seed or GT stage.

The blueprint product is already a complete product charter of capabilities, parameters and base policies, not a draft awaiting instantiate's patches. If a basic capability this task needs is not in base, fix the blueprint and re-run the upstream gates; never quietly manufacture a product capability locally in this task.

After claiming the scope and before writing extended, build two general authoring check cards for this task and write them into `design_rationale`:

- **The base-invariant card**: for each candidate subject, list every base policy and `eligibility` that matches, transcribing item by item the condition, the evidentiary authority and the pre-action object correlation; for a time gate, additionally list the starting business event, the anchor source, the clock, the source of the duration, the day/month counting rule, the not-earlier-than / not-later-than direction and the equality boundary. Work through, with concrete seed facts, "why this task does or does not satisfy it at frozen_time". If base hides a prerequisite only in prose, if one duration has two plausible starting points, or if an action binding cannot land on a future tool parameter, go straight back to the blueprint.
- **The business-object card**: state the narrowest business object the customer's request points at (which order/charge/application/voucher under which holding), the unique correlation key, the rows that enter each aggregate, the rows on the same holding that must be excluded, and which existing reversals/compensation/adjustments settled what. A case reference, a customer id or a holding id may serve as an aggregation boundary only where the policy explicitly defines it as the whole processing set; otherwise a narrower source-object correlation must exist.

Each task also builds a third card, the **problem-tree card**: draw the root problem and every prerequisite problem (each node written as a policy id or workflow id), recording per edge its `kind` (`state_gate` / `value_input`), the location of the gate, the sentence of the condition that must hold before handling, the rule sentence that declares this prerequisite, the tool or workflow that resolves it, and the seed evidence for "why it does not hold at the opening" (down to `initial_state.<table>.<row_id>.<field>` and its value). The card also carries the topological order, the argument that parallel edges leave the terminal state unambiguous, and the per-edge deletion sensitivity (which scored quantity or terminal field changes once this edge is deleted).

A workflow task builds a fourth card, the **zero-wait causal card**: list, per step, every prerequisite with its `initial_state_preexisting | prior_synchronous_tool_effect | current_user_input` source and locator; wherever a preceding tool is cited, write out verbatim the real business effect that tool completed synchronously. If no legitimate source can be found, or the source is really "wait for a real-world event and then add a status row", abandon that workflow scope immediately. Every prerequisite edge of the problem tree must pass the same test: the action resolving the prerequisite must be completable synchronously at the same moment, and may never be "the customer will sort it out offline later".

These cards come before the formulas and the GT; they prove "which action may be taken, whose case is being handled, and whether it can all be done immediately in one consultation", and only then does the extended compute answer "what the parameter is when doing it". Never compute a number first and then work backwards to an eligibility, an object scope or a pseudo-synchronous procedure that accommodates it.

### Step 1: write this task's policy and the single workflow to disk

This task handles only the policy core it claims. Each newly added `extended_policy` layers calculation logic on top of base: every base eligibility boundary, constant, tier difference, exception, stacking order and terminal state not declared in `overwrite` is retained; to deviate, the base sentence being replaced must be named in `overwrite`. Each policy completes the following fields and their cross-references on the spot:

- a non-empty `base_policy_refs` that lists every base policy whose subject intersects this policy's subject; never pick only the few the formula reads directly while omitting the eligibility, status, term or dedup policies on the same business write. Where compute reads other base bases as well, their real input lineage may additionally be referenced, but an extra reference does not mean it creates an eligibility gate on this write;
- `overwrite`: this policy's override declarations relative to base, required, written `[]` explicitly where there is none. Each entry names one `base_policy` (which must also be in `base_policy_refs`), a `superseded_clause` quoted verbatim from that base policy's `rule`, and a `replaced_by` quoted verbatim from this policy's own `rule`. The granularity is the clause: only the named sentence is retired, and the same base policy's other clauses stay in force. The test is **to evaluate the quantity this policy computes and base's provision for the same quantity, each once, in the same world**: different means it must be declared here, identical means write `[]`. Calling base's output a "wrong value" or "the algorithm to avoid" anywhere in the rule, in `compute_basis`, in `design_rationale` or in any of your own notes, without declaring it here, IS an undeclared override;
- a general, independently executable `rule`, stating each factor's source, history window, filters, status branches, stacking order, acting base, grouping of operations, the moment of the cap/floor, the rounding mode and the rounding moment (writing only the dimensions that genuinely exist in that calculation); the rule contains no authoring-viewpoint phrasing such as "in this task / this problem / usually zero";
- `trigger` as either `state_due` or `on_request`, making clear whether it is a debt the state of the world already owes or a quote that arises only when the customer asks;
- `computes`, `target_tool`, `params`, `inputs`, `cross_category`; each policy's `cross_category` must be non-empty, every category it declares must be genuinely referenced by an `EXISTS` / `CPARAM` / `CFIELD` in compute, and must agree item by item with `inputs.cross_category`; a cross-category read may serve only read-only as an existence or branch condition, and the result is written back only to this category;
- `compute`: ordered intermediate bindings whose last item `result` is the scored parameter, with expressions using only the restricted primitives, and every name resolvable back to the schema / a parameter / an event field;
- `compute_basis`: every binding that reads the world, aggregates, chooses, crosses categories, caps or rounds quotes the rule sentence verbatim on the spot; where no sentence can be quoted, go back and add it to the rule, and never let the basis invent a basis of its own;
- `prerequisite`: the **other problems** that must be resolved before this problem can be handled, required, written `[]` explicitly where there is none. It declares a dependency between problems, not the step order inside one problem (that lives in the workflow's `steps` and `order_rationale`). Each entry contains: `problem` (the id of the depended-on problem, taken from another `extended_policies[].id` or `workflows[].id` of this difficulty; never pointing at a base policy — base is a source of eligibility, not a problem that can be resolved — never self-referential, and the whole graph must be acyclic), `kind` (`state_gate` or `value_input`), `gate` (for `state_gate`, `<policy_id>.<eligibility_id>` or `<table>.<field>`; for `value_input`, the affected binding name in this policy's compute), `condition` (the sentence that must hold before this problem is handled, verbatim), `policy_source` (a sentence quoted verbatim from this policy's `rule` or from the `rule` of one of its `base_policy_refs`, proving that the prerequisite **is written into general text** and will be visible to the agent once the documents are rendered — if no sentence can be quoted, the prerequisite exists only in the author's head, so add it to the rule or delete the edge), `resolved_by` (the action that resolves it, i.e. the depended-on problem's `target_tool` or workflow id), and `effect` (which gate of this problem becomes satisfied once resolved, or which compute binding changes to what value).

`prerequisite` is a general declaration at the policy layer and carries no shadow of a specific task: it says only "that must be settled before this can be handled", never "in this task it is not settled at the opening". The latter is a task-level fact and belongs in the task JSON's `prerequisite_closure`. The same policy may find the prerequisite satisfied in one world and unsatisfied in another, and the policy text holds in both.

When classifying, ask: "with no customer calling at all, should this money already be on the books at month-end reconciliation?" Should means `state_due`; should not means `on_request`. No answer means the trigger semantics are still vague — fix the base/extended policy before seeding the world. A `state_due` lit by the seed must be carried by the current task; an `on_request` fires only where the customer genuinely asks and enters this task's path, and the formula may never rest at "computable in theory".

For each `base_policy_ref`, run an **eligibility inheritance sweep** once more: take all of its `eligibility` items whose subject intersects this atomic write, and, apart from those declared replaced sentence by sentence in `overwrite`, none of them may be rewritten, weakened or skipped by extended's rule, compute, workflow or tool behaviour; those still inherited must enter `solvability_contract.gold_actions[].base_preconditions` item by item. No separate correspondence is needed between a gate and `overwrite`: the blueprint already requires each gate's `condition` to appear verbatim in its `rule`, so a `superseded_clause` that quotes that sentence retires that gate at the same time; one that does not leaves the gate in force. To retire only the amount basis while keeping the gate, simply do not quote the gate's sentence. The lineage here is "every base policy governing that subject", not a list of formula references the author picked. Where there is no structured eligibility but base's prose plainly contains an approval, completion, status, time, liability, channel or dedup prerequisite, it may not be waved through as "policy_scope" — go back and fix the blueprint first. A time condition decides eligibility first and the amount second; where eligibility does not hold, the correct terminal state of the task is to wait or refuse and that write must not be scheduled, so never construct a GT that forces the tool call.

Every key computation has at least 6 real factors, with at least one condition taking a zero/false branch in this task's world; the history and cross-category factors must genuinely change the result and may not be decorative. The cross-category one must satisfy two conditions at once: it reads a **value** (`CPARAM`/`CFIELD`) rather than mere existence, and that dependency is genuinely exercised in this task's seed — reading a parameter that is the same at every tier, or declaring it without seeding the corresponding holding row, does not count as genuinely changing the result. The rule's complete domain and compute's branch structure align item by item, never writing only the branch the current seed hits.

`compute` permits only machine-parsable restricted expressions: this holding's fields, `P`/`K` parameters, `SUM`/`COUNT`/`MAX`/`LATEST` (optionally with a time window and field filters), the cross-category `EXISTS`/`CPARAM`/`CFIELD`, `MONTHS`/`DAYS`, threshold ternaries, `MN`/`MX`/`RND`/`ABS` and arithmetic comparisons. Every intermediate binding's name must resolve back to a real schema, parameter, tier or event field; one wrong name is a red light. The formula must cover the complete policy domain, not merely the branch the current seed hits; hand-picking a number and stuffing it into gold without an executable formula is a design defect, not difficulty.

The `rule` is general text addressed to all customers, and may never say "not triggered in this task / usually zero" from the author's viewpoint. Negative space is written as a conditional clause (those meeting X are granted/charged Y, those not meeting it are out of scope). `rule` and `compute` must have equal domains: in particular, for enum branches such as handling method, loss type and channel, every branch the rule promises must enter compute, and compute may not invent a branch the rule never states. Wherever the calculation involves a semantic choice about combining versus itemised rates, the acting base, the order or grouping of operations, the moment of the cap/floor, or the rounding mode/precision/moment, it must be pinned down item by item in plain prose in the rule; "in turn", "then", "combined" and "on the remaining amount" cannot carry that information alone. `compute_basis` quotes the rule sentence verbatim for every binding that reads the world or makes a choice; pure algebraic plumbing is exempt. After quoting, cross-check the sensitive words: current period / this month / the last N months correspond to a time window, unsettled/waived to a status filter, "whichever applies by method" to a conditional branch, a cap to `MN`, rounding to `RND`/`int`; a filter present in the expression but absent from the quotation must likewise be added to the rule or deleted.

"Pinned down item by item" here is done as needed, not as a requirement that every calculation carry extra notes: add the clause only where that choice genuinely exists, or where omitting it would let a careful reader reach two reasonable results. Where rates are combined, state whether they are combined first and applied once to the same original base or applied item by item; for each quantity, state whether it acts on the original base value, a specified intermediate subtotal, or the previous step's result; where order or bracketing changes the result, write the full order and grouping as "first ... giving ..., then taking that result ..."; for a cap or floor, state whether it applies before or after the deduction/discount/combination; for rounding, state the mode, the precision, and whether it happens only on the final result or at each intermediate step. Never let summary words such as "in turn", "then", "combined" or "on the remaining amount" leave the agent to guess. If the reality is that the rates are combined and applied once, write explicitly "the rates are first combined into one total rate, and the original base fee is reduced once by that total rate; the two discounts do not act successively"; if the reality is successive discounting, write explicitly "the discounted amount from the previous step is the base on which the next discount acts". Where plain prose cannot carry the machine formula losslessly, fix the `rule` first, and never require the agent to read source code or choose an algorithm for itself.

Once the forward references are complete, sweep back: read the rule through and point every load-bearing sentence carrying a quantity, time window, status filter, enum choice, stacking order, cap or rounding semantic at the compute binding that implements it; a sentence nobody claims must either get a binding or lose the promise. Then check back from each binding to the rule: a window, filter or branch present in the expression but absent from the quotation must be added to the rule or removed from the expression. Do the same two-way sweep for the acting base, the ordering/grouping, the moment of the cap/floor and the rounding mode/precision/moment listed above. `compute_basis` records the mapping only and may never legislate for either side; changing the rule means changing the basis and checking compute, and changing compute means checking back against the rule.

Turn Step 0's dependency draft into an executable policy network: the core computation may not be named directly by the surface goal; the agent must first read the prerequisites out of the policy, then gather the history, tier, cross-category conditions and exceptions before it can evaluate. A workflow task realises the depth as write steps whose order cannot be swapped, with an adjacent `order_rationale`; every task realises the breadth as policies and world factors that are genuinely read and genuinely change the result. If removing a supposed dependency changes no judgement, parameter, legal ordering or terminal state, it is not load-bearing: delete it or redesign it.

If task i claims the single workflow, design it in this step and create no second route: exactly one entry in `workflows`, with at least 4 task-tool write steps and no share read tools; each step has `tool`, `owner`, `writes`, `role` and a list-form `computed_by`, and must also declare with `subject` precisely the one base event that atomic write realises, and list with `base_policy_refs` every base policy governing that event. Those last two mean that even an intake or customer-confirmation step with `computed_by: []` cannot bypass base eligibility. Include at least one `owner=user` step that the real business semantics require the customer to confirm or submit and that the customer can complete synchronously in the current turn; never disguise an action the customer should perform as an agent write. A policy referenced by the workflow must have its `target_tool` equal to that step.tool with its subject covering step.subject, and the union of the policies equals the workflow task's scope. `scenario` states only the general applicable situation and the terminal state once done, restating no steps and drawing no arrow chain; write an `order_rationale` for every adjacent pair, giving the policy grounds and which prerequisite or terminal state would fail if they were swapped. A fact a later step needs must already hold in an authoritative record at the opening, be established on the spot by the real business effect of a preceding synchronous tool, or be input the customer can supply immediately. Where a real-world event has not yet occurred, end before the waiting point; environment CRUD, a status label, a persona line and a future receipt may none of them fabricate its completion. Write the per-step zero-wait causal card at the same time; a vague claim of "completed on the spot" in `order_rationale` is not enough.

Also write `workflow.dataflow` for every real cross-step dependency: the producing and consuming step indices, the `object_binding`/`state_effect` kind, the producing table and field, and the parameter binding in the consuming action. Where passing an object also carries the next step's eligibility correlation, mark the corresponding base policy / eligibility / gate field on the consumer binding; where a status change becomes the next step's eligibility (for example the earlier step changing an application to approved/completed), `state_effect.establishes_gates` must pin the changed field item by item to every relevant base eligibility of the consumer, and that consumer's `base_preconditions` must reference the real row through `prior_step`. Where there is no cross-step data dependency it may be empty, but as soon as `order_rationale` claims "the object the earlier step produced/approved/completed is used by the later step", there must be a dataflow; asserting in prose that the order matters is not enough.

Record every policy or workflow revision in the worklog immediately, and propagate it to the affected tools, seeds, persona, verification code, GT, evaluation, tracing and document references in the same pass.

### Step 2: write this task's accompanying atomic tools to disk

Add only the task tools this task genuinely needs, and never build spare tools for future tasks. A tool is named `<verb>_<object>_<uuid8>`, frozen once. For every task tool defined, call `uuid.uuid4().hex[:8]` once for that tool alone to generate its own brand-new `uuid8`; never reuse one uuid across tools, never copy an existing tool name and hand-edit the tail, and never batch-derive them from a common prefix, a sequence number or adjacent values. Once generated, the uuid8 is frozen with that tool and used identically in every reference from the policy, workflow, GT, evaluation, scope, documents and tracing. The tool is hidden: its name is discovered by the agent only through this difficulty's extended policy `target_tool` or a workflow step, and is reached through the paired `unlock_*` -> `call_*` meta-tools.

A tool **never validates a prerequisite**. For the problem a prerequisite blocks, its tool remains callable while that prerequisite is unresolved, still writes faithfully, and still returns a normal receipt — it does not check, refuse, error or hint. Wanting a tool to refuse while a prerequisite is unmet moves the agent's core decision into the environment: delete that design outright. Likewise, the tool that resolves a prerequisite does not refuse because "the upstream is not done yet". Every prerequisite judgement is made by the agent, from the policy and the world.

Each tool makes exactly one atomic state change, and never aggregates history inside itself or computes a derived value for the agent. A value synthesised from history, the tier or another category must be gathered and worked out layer by layer by the agent, then passed in as a fixed-format parameter. A tool uses `ctx.make_id(prefix, *all arguments)`, `round(float(x), 2)` for money, `ctx.now()` for time only, and `ctx.create/update/delete` for writes only; the owner is `agent` or `user` according to the business meaning.

Atomic CRUD is not a licence to step over a real-world waiting point. Never add a tool such as "complete the diagnosis", "confirm delivery", "payment cleared" or "approval granted" merely to make a workflow continuous, where its implementation simply writes a completion marker while the corresponding business still requires action or waiting outside the environment in the real world. Only where a tool's own business effect is an action the current system can complete synchronously may its DB delta become evidence for a later step's prerequisite.

Before implementing each tool, write a "capability contract card" in this task's worklog: `owner/rw`, the user, the target business object, the atomic action one call completes, the exact account/status/business-record change (and the event it writes, none of whose triggers may land in another category), what the customer or agent can observe on success, and the shape of the success receipt with every returned field. For a lookup tool, replace the atomic action with the search scope, the available filters, the business fields returned on a hit and the meaning of a miss. If any item on the card cannot be stated, the tool's boundary is not yet defined: fix the tool design first, and never lean on the function name, the implementation code or a later KB document to supply the meaning.

The docstring is the runtime schema the agent reads, and must losslessly carry everything from the capability contract card that the runtime needs to know, as three parts: "capability description + `Args:` + `Returns:`"; a single operation name, or the function name translated into words, is not enough. The capability description must make clear who performs what atomic action on which business object, exactly which account/status/business-record change it produces on success, and what result the user can observe; a lookup tool must make clear the search scope, the filtering, the content returned and the meaning of no result. `Returns:` must state the concrete shape of the success receipt, the business meaning and type/format of every returned field, and which field or status indicates the call succeeded; every field of the dictionary actually returned must be covered item by item. The docstring contains no implementation terms such as table, row, primary key or schema, states no workflow position, upstream/downstream or policy prerequisite, and writes no policy formula for a numeric/date parameter.

What is constrained here is **informational completeness**, not a copy template or a business example: the number of tools, the business actions, the parameter combinations, the field names, the enum sets, the receipt shape and the style of writing are all decided by the current scenario. Sufficient does not mean piling on words; it means an agent that sees only the runtime schema can tell, without guessing, what the tool does, how to construct each argument and what it gets on success. Never reuse a fixed sentence pattern or add information unrelated to the current tool merely to pass the gate.

`Args:` must write each parameter of the function signature except `ctx` exactly once, with none omitted, none merged into one description, and none added that does not exist. Each description must open with one and only one class contract; `references/task_tools.py` gives an abstract contract list only, and provides no business example to copy:

- `Id class —`: state which kind of business object/record the reference number belongs to, and state explicitly where the agent obtains it before the call; writing only "ID" is not acceptable.
- `Fixed-format class —`: state the business meaning and fully disclose the representation contract jointly fixed by `db_schema.json` and the tool's actual format handling, including the applicable unit, format, precision, rounding mode and moment, time zone or empty-value convention; a dimension already specified may not be omitted, and where a necessary dimension is defined in neither place, fix the specification first rather than assuming it in the docstring; never write the policy formula for the value.
- `Enum class —`: list the closed set item by item in literals matching the schema exactly in case, and state for **every value** the unique business situation in which it applies. Never use "e.g." or "such as", never omit a value, never list bare values, and never merge several values into one vague situation.
- `Verbatim class —`: state which customer utterance it is taken from unchanged, and require it to be copied character for character, preserving the original wording, digits and punctuation, with no translation, normalisation, summarisation, addition or deletion; the last characters of the whole parameter description must be exactly ` (verbatim).`.

There is no fifth "free text" class, and classes may not be combined into a hybrid. If a parameter cannot be placed in exactly one class, redesign the signature; a fixed constant belongs inside the tool implementation and should not be passed in by the agent. The type annotation, the DB field type, the precision handling in the implementation, the docstring class and the GT argument must agree in all five places. A tool's business name, parameter set, enum literals, business objects and receipt fields must all be derived independently from the current blueprint, policy, workflow and DB schema; never borrow a preset example from a reference, a neighbouring difficulty or an existing env.

A tool performs no policy eligibility judgement, and computes no amount, discount, history aggregate or derived parameter for the agent. An ordinary task tool accepts only the parameters declared in the schema and directly performs one atomic CRUD as declared; it may not check for itself whether the target row exists, its ownership, status, approval, review, completion, liability, term, deduplication, a preceding receipt or any other business prerequisite, and may not return a policy-based `Error` because such a condition is unmet. Those facts are judged by the agent before the call, from the visible KB, query results and the conversation context; `check_solvability` only verifies the structural correspondence between eligibility evidence and the GT write steps, and runs no tamper-then-refuse test against a tool. The underlying `ctx` checks on table names, field names and permissions are frozen runtime infrastructure, not a task tool's business logic.

A tool's parameters must carry the narrowest correlation key from Step 0's business-object card. A derived write such as a reversal, compensation or adjustment must write the original order/charge/application id, or the composite business key the policy explicitly defines, into the new record; it may not carry only customer_id, holding_id or a case reference covering several line items, unless base explicitly provides that the object handled is that complete set. Register the tool on the spot into this task's draft `charter_scope` and into the workflow step (if any), and check that every implied business action is carried by a tool, an established fact or explicit policy.

After every addition or change to `task_tools.py`, immediately run the difficulty-level tool contract gate below; it does not depend on the current task being finished, and checks every task tool already in the file. Without `TASK TOOL CONTRACT OK`, do not proceed to Step 5 to write the verification code or GT:

### Step 3: seed this task's initial_state, laying the path and planting excludable traps at once

Seed a real account SEED-FRESH, not a test fixture that is "just enough to compute": the customer, several holdings in this category, bills/usage/payments/charges/points/late-fee ledger rows across several consecutive billing periods, the cross-category holdings the calculation needs, and the necessary parent rows. The counts, amounts and date distribution should look like a real person's account over several years; a rich history creates both real reconciliation difficulty and a credible reason for a branch not firing naturally or a cache being out of date. Every seed row id is an opaque uuid4 from `str(uuid.uuid4())`, every non-primary `*_id` points at a parent row already seeded, no foreign key dangles, and no noise unrelated to this task is seeded.

Check every row three ways as it lands: **against the tier** — flags, statuses and amounts must be derivable from the holding's tier parameters and the policy; **against the schema** — the declared invariants must hold, the one exception being a stale cache deliberately created in this step and declared in the rationale; **against itself** — the chronology, status and amounts of one row and of one holding are self-consistent, an exit is never earlier than an entry, and a settled row's amount matches what it settles. Settled history may enrich the distraction; an unsettled status has only three legitimate identities: the object some step of this task handles, a genuinely load-bearing factor of some computation with no "settle first" prerequisite, or something not yet owed after frozen_time. The amount relations in historical transactions also teach the agent a basis, and must not contradict the charter's formula, or an agent reconciling carefully will be led astray by the seed itself. Before seeding, list base's automatic clauses (daily accrual, locking at a threshold, renewal/expiry on maturity and so on) and check row by row that they will not ignite an extra obligation the current task set cannot carry.

While seeding, **actively falsify every prerequisite of the problem tree** (principle H): following the problem-tree card, lay down for each edge the concrete rows that make that prerequisite fail at `frozen_time` — an arrears balance above zero, a status not yet transitioned, a required record not yet existing — and record per edge which field of which row takes what value to make it false. Those rows are not distracting noise; they are the load-bearing structure of this task's difficulty: delete any one of them and the corresponding prerequisite is automatically satisfied, that edge lapses, and the problem tree collapses into a single problem. Conversely, confirm that these "false" states do not ignite a third-party obligation the current task set cannot carry (the same standard as the automatic-clause check above). The side that should hold once a prerequisite is resolved must also be verified while seeding: once the resolving action lands, the gate really does flip true, or the corresponding compute binding really does change value.

Then run two handling-time audits. First, for each gold candidate write, list every base eligibility and judge with `frozen_time` + the pre-write DB that they must all be true — **note that this judgement for the root problem is made on the world after all of its prerequisites are resolved**, not on the opening world; on the opening world it ought to be false, and that is precisely the design of this task; approval/completion/status are judged from the official row, and the persona may not make them true. Second, check the correlations row by row against the business-object card: every historical row entering a sum or a deduction can be proved to belong to the target order/charge/application, existing compensation and reversals offset only the source objects they are explicitly correlated with, and other business rows on the same holding must be excludable by a visible key. Where an object relation rests only on the author's conviction, add a field, add a parent row, or change the task.

If a holding has a corresponding event-statistic cache field, set the cache and the ledger to differ, and design a natural customer re-check reaction, only where this task deliberately chooses the history difficulty of "recompute from the ledger rather than read the cache"; otherwise there is no need to manufacture a discrepancy. Whether or not a cache is used, cross-category holdings, historical fragments and branches must all have policy grounds at the point of actual choice, and may never be adjudicated by extra tool logic.

### Step 4: write this task's persona — convey the need clearly and respond passively

`user_scenario` is everything the user simulator knows, and must be written as coherent, natural language that a real customer would say and react with. It is not a policy summary, a GT explanation or an agent operating script; its first job is to convey the customer's need clearly. It must cover six kinds of information: a real persona; the situation and surface request in a layperson's voice; the definite target object and timing; the authorisation boundary between the agent acting for them / the customer acting in person / a query only or performing a write; information given only when asked; and passive reactions to what the agent says, to a user tool call, or to the outcome. Those six are a completeness checklist, not six fixed paragraphs, headings, numbers or sentence patterns; the finished text must contain no template labels such as "Role:", "Situation:", "Goal in one line:", "Only if asked:" or "Passive reply script:", and must not mechanically stack "if the agent does X -> you say Y". Weave conditional behaviour naturally into the character's narration — "only when the agent asks about ... does she ...", "once she hears it has been ..., he will ...", "when the agent hands her the tool, she will ..." — in the voice of that specific person, while keeping the trigger conditions and responses explicit, definite and reproducible.

Different tasks' personas should vary in tone, order of information, pace, vocabulary and conversational detail with the character and the situation, never differing only in name, product and numbers. Natural language does not mean vague or improvised: what the user wants done, now or later, who performs it, whether a write is authorised, and which outcomes are responded to only after which condition, must all be uniquely determinable from the scenario; the same conversational event may lead only to a pre-written response, and every customer fact gold uses must be locatable verbatim. At least 2 self-stated credentials are needed for verification (`customer_id` is a locator and does not count toward the match), and every customer attribute gold will use must appear verbatim; internal uuids are returned by locating/lookup tools and are not written into the persona.

Sort the persona's content by temporal source into three kinds before writing it as prose: **volunteered at the opening**, **stated only when the agent asks**, and **reacted to only after the agent states some event in the conversation**. The customer volunteers only the surface goal, the natural situation and hard preferences; every statement among them about an existing business fact must point verbatim at a row already present in initial_state, and a charge, status, document or conclusion that only gold writes may never be written as a fact known before the call.

**The problem tree is a silent constraint on the persona (principle H): `user_scenario` raises only the root problem and never mentions any prerequisite.** The name of a prerequisite problem, the thing it resolves, or its associated charge or status may not appear, and neither may hints such as "do I need to sort something out first?" or "I think there's still an unpaid one" that steer the agent toward it — the customer knows no policy, and the existence of a prerequisite may only be read out of the documents by the agent. Everything the customer contributes about a prerequisite is a **passive if/else**: only after the agent explains that something must be done first does the customer cooperate in the pre-written way (agreeing to have the agent act, supplying the information that action needs, or running the tool in person after the agent unlocks and hands it over). Those branches are written as natural personal speech rather than a conditional template, and the trigger must be an utterance event the agent produces, never a DB state. If the agent never discovers a prerequisite, the customer may not remind, may not press, and may not act unusually — the consequence of failing to resolve it is borne by the scoring alone. Identity, reference numbers, cross-category holdings and specific fields are given only when asked; a balance or amount may be misremembered, admitted as forgotten, or referred to the agent to look up, but a misspoken number may never become a source for gold. An `owner=user` tool's return is a shared runtime fact: the user simulator may continue the conversation from the real return value, or repeat it when asked, only after the agent has first unlocked and handed it over and it has actually been called; the value may never be leaked or guessed before the triggering event, and a fixed reference number may never be treated as knowledge the customer has in advance. There is no fourth kind, "after the agent completes the previous step, the customer waits for a real-world event to finish and comes back to report": if a real-world event has not completed at the opening, the persona may not talk it into completion during this consultation, and may not trigger a later workflow step on that basis.

Label existing facts sentence by sentence with their authority kind: `caller_statement` carries only the customer's intent, their own choices, or facts policy permits them to self-report; `system_record` carries approval, review, completion, liability, status, amounts and whether something is already done. Where the latter kind is to be mentioned naturally by the customer, it may only be expressed as "I saw / I thought", and initial_state must contain a corresponding source for that observation; it may never form, together with the official record, two equally credible truths. Unless this task uses "the customer misread it" as an explicitly excludable branch, keep the persona exactly consistent with the DB, and do not manufacture an authority conflict for the sake of a trap.

Then walk the whole call through again from the perspective of a real agent. Every piece of information that would inevitably be asked during handling — including target values, the object to register, where a payment goes, confirmation preferences and so on that never enter a GT write parameter — must have a definite passive answer in the persona consistent with the world, or the persona must explicitly be unable to answer, with the observable result of that branch (put on hold, rescheduled, or handled according to the existing record) written out. Never leave the simulator to improvise. For a stale cache, write only the reaction the customer can make from the opening bill, their experience or an already declared memory, such as "that doesn't match what I'm seeing, please check it against the bill"; the customer may never give the correct formula, steps or exact answer, nor pull the agent back to gold on any wrong path. Then read the finished persona once more against Principle A's three contributions — the goal, the identity and locating details, and passive reactions — and strike any sentence that does none of the three; a courtesy at the sign-off that names a document, an address or a statement is the usual one. Check the ending in particular: the persona's last reaction must answer the last action of `evaluation_criteria` and stop, asking for nothing beyond it.

The persona advances on observable events in the conversation, but is not a gold oracle. Stopping conditions bind only to a few natural agent utterance events such as "accepted / registered / assessed / invoiced", never audit the DB, and never enumerate all the gold steps; when the agent claims the surface result has occurred, the customer may close on the pre-set reaction even if the agent actually missed a step or wrote the wrong thing, with the scoring still judging it wrong. Every passive trigger must be definite enough that the same event leads only to the same response, while staying in character and untemplated.

### Step 5: write and run the verification code first, then form the GT

Before filling in gold by hand, write independently runnable code from the extended policy / workflow already on disk and this task's initial_state. The code reproduces each computation's ordered bindings and simulates the real tool writes step by step; a later write step's formula must be evaluated on the handling-time world of `initial_state + the preceding gold writes`, never computing every value at once against the opening ledger.

**The problem tree fixes where the ordering of "handling time" comes from (principle H): every problem's scored parameter is evaluated on the world after all of its prerequisites (including the transitive closure) are resolved and before it is itself written.** So the verification code must topologically sort the problem tree first and evaluate problem by problem in that order; a depended-on problem's write effect must already be in the simulated world before its dependant is evaluated. The root problem is especially easy to get wrong: its parameter **may not** be computed on the opening world — the opening world is precisely the world in which its prerequisites are unmet, and a value computed there is wrong. Where the topological order leaves parallel prerequisites free to be ordered either way, run both orders and prove that the terminal state and every scored parameter are identical; different means the policy has not pinned the order down, so go back to Step 1 and fix the policy rather than silently choosing one in the verification code.

The code must also run a **prerequisite sensitivity** pass: for each prerequisite edge, skip that prerequisite problem's write, replay everything else unchanged, and record which scored parameter or terminal field changes. Every edge must show at least one change, or it does not bear weight (principle H): go back to Step 0 and redesign. Write that comparison output into the worklog. If a handling-time result differs from the expected value you had in mind, change the expected value, the seed or the formula and re-run; never move the moment of evaluation back to the opening to accommodate a hand-filled answer. The commands run, the key outputs, the failures and the revision records all go into the current task's worklog.

Then, still before filling in GT, write `solvability_contract` per `references/task.json`. It is not a declaration that "I think it is solvable" but an evidence ledger for every task-tool write step: how the customer's request selects this class of action, which business stage that action is at, which base policies govern this step's exact subject and by what authoritative fact each of their eligibility items is satisfied before the write, how the evidence correlates to this action's parameters, where every necessary customer input appears verbatim, how the time condition holds at frozen_time, and why each primary holding/event seed row is consistent with the policy. Facts established by a preceding workflow step are referenced with `prior_step`; an external stage already completed before the opening must cite the official initial_state row and may not cite the persona alone. Every `gold_actions` entry of a workflow must also carry `requires_external_wait: false` and a concrete `zero_wait_proof`, consistent with the rationale's per-step zero-wait causal card; where a preceding tool merely registered a completion status without synchronously performing that real-world business, registering it as `established_by_prior_tool` is forbidden. A step's base prerequisites may not be omitted merely because its `computed_by` is `[]`; where an adjacent branch exists, whether it is excluded is confirmed by the structured policy and the semantic blind audit, and no mechanical counterfactual field is added.

Do not replay eligibility negative cases against a task tool, and do not require a tool to refuse when evidence is deleted, a status is tampered with, an object relation is broken or a duplicate record appears. `solvability_contract` registers only the pre-write visible evidence and its correspondence to GT; gold's forward CRUD replay is confirmed by `replay_tasks` against the terminal state, and the business judgement remains entirely the agent's to make from the policy.

Only once the code is stable and its output reasonable may `computations.expected` and the GT write arguments be filled in. Every number/date must equal the machine recomputation; every string, enum, id or verbatim phrase must be copyable from the context reachable before that action. A write parameter has only two legitimate origins: a number/date computed by the policy compute, and every other string copied verbatim from the charter, a tool schema, initial_state, user_scenario, frozen_time or the shared return of a preceding user/agent action; "the system will generate it" is not a source, and where a new number is needed, derive it inside the tool, pre-seed it, or have a preceding tool return it. A locator may come from a preceding locating tool or the real return of a preceding user-owned tool, while a customer-stated identity or reference number must come from the persona and may never quietly use a god's-eye value from the DB the customer never said.

Write `argument_uniqueness_tracing` on every `call_*`, carrying an entry for **every argument it passes, calculated or not**, in the two shapes `references/task.json` gives. A **calculated** parameter restates the value and then draws out every factor of its compute with that factor's value and source — the tier parameter, the stretch of history that aggregates to it, the cross-category condition that matched — so that any agent who calculates correctly converges on the same number. A **non-calculated** parameter (enum / fixed-format / verbatim / id) gives three parts: the value; the basis, quoting verbatim the charter rule, docstring, workflow field, seed row or customer line that pins it down and naming the source; and the uniqueness, why any correct agent converges on it given everything reachable at that step's handling time, saying for an enum why each remaining candidate does not hold. If no sentence can be quoted, the parameter is under-specified, and tracing may never write the rule in for it; a rule requiring the agent's judgement should go back to the rule/docstring to add an exclusive basis for the value, and a constant requiring no judgement should have its parameter removed or be folded into the tool. A customer misspeaking or being vague only lowers information quality; it can never replace policy and world truth, and can never change expected.

Check "value" and "shape" together: if an equally correct agent might use a different number of booked rows, a different attribution, a different attachment, a different owner or a different order and reach a different terminal state, go back and pin it down exclusively in the policy/docstring/workflow first, then re-verify; gold may never silently choose one. Before each write action, state where its parameters were computed or copied from and why they are reachable at that moment; after it, state how it changes the handling-time world of the later computations.

Finally, run a draft recomputation isolated from the hidden assets: setting compute/expected/GT aside for the moment, write out "whether it can be handled now, the object handled, the layers of the formula and the order of the actions" from the policy prose that will be published, the tool schemas, the facts the user scenario will state, and the DB rows the share tools can return. Where the draft disagrees with the gold about to be filled in, treat it first as a specification problem; the author knowing the machine formula may never override another reasonable reading of the visible text.

### Step 6: write evaluation_criteria from the verified GT, and close this task's scope

`evaluation_criteria` first writes the common share read nodes — locate, verify, read the ledger, read the tier, look up the cross-category holding — and then this task's real business path. **The write nodes as a whole are arranged in the problem tree's topological order: the depended-on problem's writes first, the dependent problem's writes after** (principle H); where a problem is carried by a workflow, its internal step order still follows workflow.steps, and the two levels do not conflict — the order between problems is set by `prerequisite`, and the order within a problem by the workflow. The read nodes must prove the agent has a path to discover every prerequisite: before a prerequisite is resolved, there must be a read node that finds the world fact making it currently unsatisfied (the balance, the status, the missing record), with the `necessity` stating which policy sentence makes it a gate on handling the root problem. Without such a read node, only the task author knows this task's prerequisites. Every task tool is reached through the paired meta-tools: an agent write is `unlock_agent_discoverable_tool` followed immediately by `call_agent_discoverable_tool`, and a customer's own write is `unlock_user_discoverable_tool` followed immediately by `call_user_discoverable_tool`; every action has an `objective` / `necessity`, and every `call_*` write carries tracing. An unlock has no DB effect and happens at most once per tool per task, while a call may happen as many times as needed.

A workflow task's write nodes must land step by step in the tool, owner and order of workflow.steps; a non-workflow task writes only its own atomic path. For each calculation policy, build the correspondence `policy -> workflow step/target_tool -> computation.step/arg -> GT call -> terminal state`; in the end a workflow task must satisfy the exact set equality `set(workflow.steps[*].computed_by) = set(task.computations[*].policy_id) = set(task.charter_scope.extended_policies)`. `charter_scope.extended_policies` must equal this task's set of computation policies, `required_documents` matches scope verbatim in the same order, and `task_tools` equals the task tools GT actually calls, in order of first call. No extra write tool or policy may hang outside the workflow. Confirm that every action, prerequisite and terminal state is carried, and that a wrong path is never corrected by the persona.

Where the policy uniquely entails that some candidate action does not apply in this task, the evaluation must prove why it is not written, using the necessary read nodes and `necessity`, rather than adding a no-op tool or having the persona remind the agent; and confirm at the same time that the remaining real writes still change the DB non-vacuously. Negative space must be a conclusion a correct agent excludes through the policy and the world, never a silent omission in gold.

Finally, walk the real conversation turn by turn from the persona's first sentence: first confirm whether the user's need clearly covers the object, timing, authorisation and query/quote-versus-write boundary this task's GT requires; then confirm which readable policy reveals each policy prerequisite, which synchronous tool / opening fact / explicit exemption carries each business action, where each must-ask piece of information comes from in the persona, the world or a preceding user/agent tool return, how each owner=user action is actually called by the user simulator at the agent's direction with its real output passed through the shared interaction, whether the information the next step needs is already reachable, and how the terminal state is observed in the current conversation. For a workflow, check the zero-wait causal card again for each adjacent step, and confirm that the whole evaluation runs under one `frozen_time`, never interpreting tool-call order as the passage of real time. If any point can only rest on the simulator improvising, on waiting for a real-world event or a future receipt, on a system outside the environment, or on assuming "the system will handle it", judge the scope invalid and go back to Steps 0-4 to re-choose or truncate the workflow; never write the gap into the evaluation as though it were done.

This walkthrough also confirms that the persona does not substitute for system authority, that the pre-write eligibility evidence agrees with the GT object, and that similar orders/charges on the same holding can be distinguished from the target object by a business correlation key. If any of these cannot be explained from the structured policy, the seed and the GT correspondence, the task has not closed; do not patch the hole with a tool backstop or extra refusal logic.

### Step 7: keep design_rationale up to date and close this task's loop

Create `design_rationale` at Step 0 and update it as each decision is made, rather than reconstructing it from the results at the end. In the end it is both a worklog and a self-contained test dossier; a reviewer should be able to re-verify why the task holds without opening any other file. Every section below must carry substantive evidence — a sentence, an id, a row, a formula, a command or an output — and may never say merely "satisfied / checked":

- why it makes a good test: which real support capability it forces out, and why the difficulty comes from reading policy, querying the world and calculating, rather than from missing information or guessing tools;
- why this task chose a scope not previously occupied; what the completed tasks occupied, and what is reserved for those still to come;
- the base / extended policies claimed, the `base_policy_refs`, the trigger type, the calculation factors and their load-bearing role; stating item by item which base boundaries are inherited and which existing mechanisms are refined into new factors, proving that no second product law was created;
- the base-invariant card: per eligibility gate, the condition, the authoritative evidence and the object correlation; for a time gate, the starting event/field, the source of the duration, the counting rule, the direction of comparison, the equality boundary, and positive and negative cases at frozen_time;
- why each class of initial_state row exists, and which value would change if it were removed; the automatic-clause and cache design checks (used only to confirm the history difficulty, never treating the cache or tool behaviour as a rule of policy correctness);
- the business-object card: the target object, the correlation key, the rows included and excluded, which source object each existing reversal/compensation/adjustment settles, and the source-object correlation the tool writes back;
- the natural-language design of the persona: the character's voice and how it is organised, the surface request, the information given only when asked, the definite passive reactions and natural traps, and how it avoids falling into a fixed template;
- the problem-tree card: the root problem, every prerequisite problem, per edge its `kind`/gate/condition sentence/`policy_source` sentence/resolving action, the seed-row evidence for "why it does not hold at the opening" on each edge, the topological order, the argument that parallel edges leave the terminal state unambiguous, and the actual figures from the per-edge deletion sensitivity run; plus proof that `user_scenario` nowhere mentions any prerequisite problem, and that the tool layer validates no prerequisite at all;
- the prerequisite chain the agent must discover, why it cannot be reordered, and the difficulty in depth (long) and breadth (parallel policies/history/cross-category);
- the workflow's per-step zero-wait causal card: every prerequisite, its `source_kind`, its `source_locator`, the preceding synchronous tool's real business effect and the `zero_wait_proof`; with explicit proof that no status registration, persona line or tool-call order has been taken for a real-world event having occurred;
- the places most easily got wrong through carelessness, and how a careful agent excludes the error using the policy and the world;
- the standard solution: which rows to read, which policies to assemble, how each layer of factors is computed, and which writes land under which SOP;
- the full text of the verification code or a runnable entry point, the commands actually executed and their output, with the output exactly equal to computations and gold;
- the policy/workflow/GT closure, the correspondence between the ordering and the terminal-state clauses, the list of base automatic clauses, and this task's scope closure;
- the pre-write eligibility evidence in `solvability_contract` against GT; and a record of one draft recomputation from the visible context without hidden compute/GT, and its agreement with gold.

When a problem is found, record "symptom -> root cause -> change -> the result of re-running" in the worklog, and propagate every dependency per principle G. Only once all of task i's fields, assets, verification and per-task gates are closed may task i+1 begin; the final task then adds the difficulty-level union, exclusivity and headroom closing records.

## Artifact contracts (structural requirements)

### Artifact 1: `env/<scenario>/tasks/<difficulty>/<category>.extended.yaml`

- the base section contains verbatim every key of `products/<category>.yaml`: `category`, `display_name`, `binding`, `lifecycle`, `accounts`, `parameter_schema`, `variants`, `measures`, `events`, `policies`;
- `extended_policies` contains incrementally only the policies some task has already claimed, each with every field from Step 1; each `(target_tool, computes)` is claimed by at most one policy; every cross-category declaration must agree with the `EXISTS` / `CPARAM` / `CFIELD` in compute and with `inputs.cross_category`; every policy and every workflow must fill in `prerequisite` (`[]` where there is none), whose `problem` points only at another policy id or workflow id in this file with the whole graph acyclic, and whose `policy_source` is verbatim locatable in the cited `rule`;
- `workflows` has exactly one entry in the final file. A workflow contains `id`, a general `scenario`, at least 4 task-tool write `steps`, a `dataflow` of cross-step object/state dependencies, and an `order_rationale` for each adjacent pair; each step's `subject` is the one base event it realises, `base_policy_refs` lists every base policy for that event, there is no top-level `computed_by` on the workflow, and an extended policy must hang on the exact step; the scenario restates no concrete step and writes only the applicable situation and the terminal state once done; a workflow likewise must fill in `prerequisite`, with the same semantics as on the policy side — it declares "which other problems must be resolved before this SOP can be run", not the step order inside the SOP. Every dataflow may pass only a fact established at the opening or the real effect of a preceding synchronous tool, and may never cross a real-world waiting point.

### Artifact 2: `env/<scenario>/tasks/<difficulty>/task_tools.py`

The file contains only the hidden atomic tools some task actually uses. Each task tool name's `uuid8` must be generated independently and afresh for that tool, never shared with another task tool and never derived from the same numbering sequence. Every definition must be named by an extended policy's `target_tool` or by a workflow step; idle, spare or purely distracting tools are not allowed. The handoff tool may exist among the blueprint's share tools, but may never appear in any task's gold.

### Artifact 3: `env/<scenario>/tasks/<difficulty>/tasks/0.json` ... `tasks/M-1.json`

Under `tasks/` there must exist exactly the contiguous `0.json` through `M-1.json`, and nothing else. Each task contains `frozen_time`, a `design_rationale` in the form of a running worklog, a `user_scenario` organised as natural language covering the five kinds of necessary information, a SEED-FRESH `initial_state`, a per-write-step evidence ledger `solvability_contract`, `evaluation_criteria`, `computations`, `charter_scope` and `required_documents`.

- `initial_state` is this task's DB closure, containing the customer, the holdings in this category, the event ledger the calculation must reconcile, the read-only cross-category conditions and every parent row; row ids must be uuid4, and no foreign key may dangle;
- `solvability_contract` registers in full, per `references/task.json`, the request alignment, each task-tool write step's business stage / request binding / base prerequisites / eligibility evidence, temporal feasibility, the necessary customer inputs and the consistency of every primary holding/event seed row; each write step of a workflow additionally registers `requires_external_wait: false` and a concrete `zero_wait_proof`; every temporal item deep-copies base's `temporal_basis` and gives positive and negative frozen-time boundary cases; it cites pre-write visible evidence and may never prove itself with the rationale or with expected;
- `evaluation_criteria`'s read nodes name the share tools directly, and its write nodes follow Step 6's meta-tool contract;
- each computation contains at least `policy_id`, `target_holding_id`, `step`, `arg`, `expected` and a per-factor `factor_trace`; the gate recomputes at handling time that `result == expected == the gold parameter`;
- `prerequisite_closure` registers the **factual face** of this task's problem tree: `root_problem` (the policy id or workflow id the customer's surface request points at), `edges` (per edge `from` / `to` / `kind` / `condition` / `policy_source` / `resolved_by`, plus `unresolved_at_frozen_time`: the criterion by which the prerequisite is false at the opening, the concrete `initial_state` row path and field value, and why it is false), `resolution_order` (a topological order, depended-on first, agreeing with the order of the write nodes in `evaluation_criteria`), `order_uniqueness` (for parallel edges that may be reordered, proof that both orders give the same terminal state; where they may not, the policy sentence that pins the order down), `edge_load_bearing` (per edge, the scored parameter or terminal field that changes once that prerequisite is skipped, taken from the Step 5 sensitivity run) and `no_user_hint` (proof that `user_scenario` mentions no prerequisite problem). There are at least 2 edges, and at least one `kind` is `value_input`;
- `charter_scope`'s `extended_policies`, `workflows` and `task_tools` register only this task's exclusive assets, and **their span is the whole problem tree** (the policies and tools of the root problem and all of its prerequisite problems); a workflow task's three sets close per Step 6, and every other task's workflows must be `[]`;
- `required_documents` is a list of policy ids, equal item by item, verbatim and in order to this task's `charter_scope.extended_policies`, which the later KB render carries in the documents' `meta.policy_id`.

## Gates (run per task first, then at difficulty level once all are done)

Every gate script supports an optional `--task N` (and also accepts `--task tasks/N.json`). After completing task i, run the following per task before moving on to task i+1; `--task` mode checks the selected task's own closure while also verifying that the assets of the completed prefix do not overlap; a difficulty still being designed incrementally is allowed to have 0 or 1 workflow for the moment, and the contiguity of future files, full M coverage, the unique workflow and the suite means are left to the final run. `replay_tasks.py --task N` writes back only that task's closure fields.

```bash
DIFF=env/<scenario>/tasks/<difficulty>
N=0  # the task just closed

# (1) this task's extended policy / workflow / tool / scope closure
#     including the policy side of prerequisite: fields complete, problem pointing at another
#     solvable problem in this file, no self-reference, no cycle, policy_source verbatim locatable
#     in the rule, resolved_by equal to the depended-on problem's own action, a value_input gate
#     being a real compute binding of this policy, and general text carrying no authoring-viewpoint
#     phrasing; plus this task's problem tree: >=2 prerequisites, >=1 value_input, and the whole
#     tree inside charter_scope
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_extended.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (1b) this task's runtime tool schema: capability, the four Args classes, format/enum/verbatim and Returns
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_task_tools.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (2) this task's determinism, no Error, non-vacuous writes, initial_state closure and FKs
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/replay_tasks.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (2b) this task's request/eligibility/authoritative evidence/object correlation/stage provenance
#      against the GT write steps; including the factual side of prerequisite_closure: each edge's
#      unresolved_at_frozen_time evidence must be an initial_state row path (the prerequisite really
#      does not hold at the opening, rather than the author asserting it), resolution_order is a
#      topological order ending at root_problem, edge_load_bearing is complete per edge,
#      order_uniqueness / no_user_hint / tools_do_not_block are non-empty, and user_scenario names
#      no prerequisite problem
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_solvability.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (3) mechanically recompute every computation of this task at handling time; this task's factor,
#     history and branch hard thresholds; including two mechanical prerequisite judgements: the gold
#     write order is a topological order of the problem tree (every write of a depended-on problem
#     precedes the writes of the problem depending on it); and every value_input edge genuinely bears
#     weight — skipping the depended-on problem's write and recomputing must change at least one of
#     the dependant's scored values, or that edge is a decorative dependency.
#     The load-bearing nature of a state_gate edge cannot be decided mechanically here and is left to
#     the policy audit
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_compute.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (4) this task's persona: identity grounding, no answer disclosure, and passive disclosure
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_grounding.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (4b) only after the published KB has been rendered: verbatim provenance of non-computed
#      parameters in the reachable context
# .venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_traceability.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (5) optional: where this task deliberately designed a cache trap, view the diagnostics comparing
#     the history sum with the displayed value (not a correctness gate)
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_cache_diff.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"

# (6) this task's trigger, claim and scope (a structural check only; it does not execute unclaimed
#     policy formulas)
.venv/bin/python skills/erl-task-instantiate/scripts/check_cross_fire.py "$DIFF" --task "$N"
```

The responsibility boundaries of the per-task gates:

- `check_extended --task N` still checks base compatibility, policy structure, the full governing-policy lineage, the structural legality of the `overwrite` declarations (the named base policy exists and is in the lineage, and both quotations are verbatim locatable), that compute parses, that a cross-category read writes only this category, and that each workflow step's exact subject/base refs/dataflow align with the current task's computation/GT/tool; it does not require an unfinished difficulty to have exactly one workflow, full M coverage or contiguous future files, but the prefix files of the selected task may not be missing;
- `check_task_tools --task N` treats a task tool's docstring as the agent-visible runtime schema: it checks that this task's claimed tools have a capability description that is not vague, that the signature's parameters are each covered exactly once in `Args:` and belong to exactly one of id/fixed-format/enum/verbatim, that the class agrees with the DB schema and the type annotation, that a fixed-format's unit/format/precision are complete, that an enum covers the schema's closed set with an applicable situation for every value, that a verbatim explicitly requires character-for-character copying and ends with the prescribed suffix, and that `Returns:` covers the success receipt; it does not substitute for policy, SOP or business eligibility judgement;
- `replay_tasks --task N` checks that a real tool replay gives the same hash twice, with no Error, that gold changes the DB, that the closure matches the seed, and that ids are uuid4 with no dangling foreign key;
- `check_solvability --task N` checks that locating and verification precede the business action, that the request uniquely selects the action, that every base eligibility has pre-action authoritative evidence correlated to the action's object, that stage facts have an official source, that the necessary customer inputs are verbatim reachable, and that the eligibility evidence corresponds structurally to the GT actions; a workflow step must additionally declare that no real-world wait is needed and give a zero-wait proof. The script can only check declarations and reference structure; it cannot judge whether the author disguised "registering a completion status" as a real business completion, which must be judged by the manual zero-wait semantic gate; it calls no tool to run eligibility negative cases, duplicate-record injection or "soft refusal" tests, and it cannot infer by regex whether a prose policy omits a prerequisite or picked the wrong starting business event;
- `check_compute --task N` checks each of this task's formulas and its handling time, the load-bearing history/cross-category factors, the key factors and the untriggered branch; each task needs at least one key computation with >= 6 factors. The suite means, the history maximum and the branch maximum are checked only in the final run: across the difficulty, the means of factors/branches/steps are at least 5/4/4, at least one task has history_rows >= 8, and at least one task has branches >= 5.
- `check_grounding` under `--task N` handles only the current task, making it easy to fix the persona on the spot; `check_traceability` reads the officially published KB and must be run after rendering is complete (it is not run during instantiation, when there are no docs), and it checks only the provenance of parameters — it does not substitute for the post-publication visible-context blind audit. `check_cache_diff` is only an optional diagnostic for history difficulty and is never a red-light condition; `check_cross_fire` performs only structural checks of scope, trigger and claim, with cross-task scope verified together in the final run without `--task`, and it neither executes unclaimed policy formulas nor reads a nonzero result as an obligation this task must discharge.

When a per-task gate goes red on any of the following, go back to the corresponding step and fix it; never work around it with a registration or a disclaimer:

- `check_extended`: a base key missing or altered; the lineage of base policies governing this write incomplete; `overwrite` missing or structurally illegal (the named base policy does not exist, is not in `base_policy_refs`, the `superseded_clause` is not a verbatim substring of that base policy's `rule`, the `replaced_by` is not a verbatim substring of this policy's `rule`, or the same (base_policy, superseded_clause) is declared twice); the policy structure or compute unparsable; a cross-category read not genuinely entering the formula; a target tool that does not exist or writes another category; a workflow step not declaring its exact subject / all base refs / dataflow. Misalignment among the current task's scope, required_documents, computation, GT call and tool/owner also fails. The final mode additionally requires contiguous tasks, exactly one workflow, exactly one workflow task, full policy coverage and pairwise disjoint scopes.
- `check_task_tools`: a capability description without the target object, the atomic business effect, the observable result or the success semantics; a missing `Args:` / `Returns:`; a parameter omitted, added, given a mixed class, or given a class inconsistent with the schema; a fixed-format missing its unit/format/precision, an enum missing a value or listing bare values, a verbatim not explicitly requiring character-for-character copying or not ending with ` (verbatim).`; or an actually returned field not described in the success receipt — all fail. Go back to Step 2 to complete the capability contract card and the docstring; never move the explanation into GT, the KB or the worklog and carry on.
- `replay_tasks`: a tool raising an exception or returning `Error:`, the two hashes differing, gold not changing the DB, the closure disagreeing with the seed, a seed id that is not uuid4, or a dangling foreign key — all fail.
- `check_solvability`: a missing `solvability_contract`, the customer's request unable to select this action uniquely, a base eligibility omitted, approval/completion/status supported only by the persona, evidence and the action's parameters not being the same business object, a time condition not bound to frozen_time, or a workflow not declaring `requires_external_wait: false` / `zero_wait_proof`, or a stage resting on a future event — all fail. It does not fail because a task tool accepts a business negative case, and it makes no regex-based inference of an implicit prerequisite in prose rules; an omitted prerequisite or a pseudo-synchronous real-world event is to be fixed in the blueprint's structured eligibility and by the manual semantic audit.
- `check_compute`: a missing formula-forced computation, a handling-time recomputation not equal to expected or to the gold parameter, history/cross-category factors that do not bear weight, fewer than 6 key factors, or no untriggered branch — all fail. Only the suite-level means, the history maximum and the branch maximum are deferred to the final mode.
- `check_grounding`: a customer identity attribute gold uses not appearing verbatim in the persona, or the computed answer disclosed directly by the persona. A multi-write task lacking clear, natural passive reactions is only a hint, but should still be fixed by hand, and never turned into a fixed conditional script to satisfy a count.
- `check_traceability` (after publication): any non-numeric, non-date parameter with no verbatim provenance in the context reachable before that action means a "system-generated" dead task; supply a real query return, a preceding user/agent tool output, or a simulator dialogue source — do not change the gate. An `owner=user` tool's real return value is a shared runtime source, and should not be hard-coded into `user_scenario` in advance to satisfy provenance. Only an internal id genuinely generated by the system may be removed from the parameters and generated by the atomic CRUD; a policy amount, status or decision may never be moved into a tool. It does not read the raw YAML/initial_state, and cannot substitute for an independent blind audit.
- `check_cache_diff`: optionally reports the cache statistics against the ledger total, and whether the persona has a natural re-check reaction; it changes no definition of policy <-> GT correctness and fails on no result.
- `check_cross_fire`: a missing or illegal trigger, the same `(target_tool, computes)` claimed by several policies, or a scope inconsistent with the actual tools — all fail; it executes no unclaimed policy formula and never reads a nonzero result as an obligation this task must discharge.

The hard requirements of the final gates include: the base section is verbatim compatible, and every eligibility gate is either inherited item by item or named as replaced in `overwrite` (an undeclared deviation is a red light); every task tool's capability, four-way parameter classes, format/precision, complete enum set, verbatim text and success receipt are complete and unambiguous in the agent-visible schema; every extended policy has a load-bearing cross-category **numeric** factor genuinely exercised in that task's world; the tasks are contiguous; there is exactly one workflow with at least 4 write steps, and exactly one task's GT follows its tool/owner/order/dataflow precisely; every prerequisite of every workflow step comes from a fact established at the opening, the real effect of a preceding synchronous tool, or customer input obtainable immediately, with the whole chain depending on no post-consultation real-world event or passage of time; each task closes the loop policy -> base eligibility -> computation -> GT call -> terminal state -> required_documents; all M tasks cover every extended policy with pairwise disjoint scopes; each task has at least one key computation with >= 6 factors, the suite means of factors/branches/steps are at least 5/4/4, at least one task has history_rows >= 8 and at least one has branches >= 5; all gold replays deterministically, changes the DB non-vacuously, raises no Error, and has a legal closure and FKs; the request, the authoritative status, the time and the business object all correspond at handling time to that write's structured policy; the calculation recomputes exactly at handling time; the persona neither discloses the answer nor overrides system authority; every action carries a non-empty `objective` and `necessity` and every argument of every `call_*` has an `argument_uniqueness_tracing` entry; and there is no handoff GT and no unhandled policy obligation. Where a cache is chosen as a difficulty trap, it need only stay consistent with its structured policy and the dialogue design, and constitutes no third set of correctness rules.

Any red light forbids finishing. To fix one, go back to the operating step that caused it, update the worklog and every downstream asset in the same pass, then re-run from the current task's per-task gates; once all the tasks are done, re-run from the first final gate. Finally, review from the solving agent's perspective: does arriving at the value really require gathering the history, judging the branch correctly, looking up the cross-category condition and booking in order; can the traps be excluded by policy rather than producing two solutions; do the later tasks still have independent load-bearing space; and can the workflow be completed and confirmed within one conversation.

After each task i is completed and its per-task scripts pass, the following manual values gates must also be completed immediately, with the results written into that task's worklog; without passing them, do not move on to task i+1. Once every task is done, review the difficulty-level combined effect once more alongside the final scripts. These checks can never be replaced by a green script:

- **Length, breadth and the hidden test**: actually walk from the surface goal to the core computation, naming the policy prerequisite at each hop; list the parallel policies, history windows, cross-category conditions and exceptions, and prove that removing any load-bearing dependency changes a value, an order or a terminal state; a workflow must additionally prove that at least one `owner=user` step is genuinely required by the in-person semantics and can be completed on the spot;
- **Expression of the need and the simulator's boundary**: the user must clearly express the target object, the timing, the mode of authorisation and the query/quote-versus-write boundary; explicit if/else may govern a conditional response, but the user simulator may never read policy/GT, push the procedure along on the agent's behalf, correct a calculation or leak the answer; a user tool may only be executed or responded to from its real return after the agent has explicitly handed it over;
- **The chronology of the opening world**: every fact the persona volunteers points verbatim at initial_state, confirming it does not foresee an event only gold produces; every passive fact states the natural conversational event that triggers it;
- **Base eligibility and the time anchor**: align base eligibility per subject; check the time condition verbatim against the starting business event/field, the clock, the source of the duration, the `anchor + duration` counting rule, the direction of comparison and the equality boundary, and construct one case that just satisfies it and one that just fails. Never skip the semantic judgement of "why this date is the correct starting point" or "have calendar days been swapped for full 24-hour periods" on the grounds that the script only sees `DAYS(...)`;
- **Authoritative facts and the business object**: approval, review, completion, liability, status and deduplication are all supported by pre-write system evidence correlated to the action's parameters; prove row by row that each aggregated/offset record belongs to the target order, charge, application or voucher, and that similar rows on the same holding have explicit grounds for exclusion;
- **Reachability in a real conversation, and zero-wait causality**: list turn by turn what carries each action and where every must-ask piece of information comes from, including questions that never enter gold; for each workflow step, list every prerequisite with its `initial_state_preexisting | prior_synchronous_tool_effect | current_user_input` source, proving the whole chain ends under one `frozen_time`. A diagnosis, a visit, a delivery, a payment clearing, a manual/external review, a back-office batch, a future date or any advance of real time may never become a prerequisite of a later step if it has not completed at the opening; a status registration, a tool's name and a persona line may never pose as the event having completed;
- **Two-way equivalence of rule/compute**: check the basis's forward quotation binding by binding, then sweep the rule in reverse for its promises about quantities, windows, filters, branches, ordering, caps and rounding, confirming that compute implements all of them;
- **A draft recomputation from the visible context**: mask the extended YAML, compute, expected, GT, the rationale and the hidden meta, and re-judge whether it can be handled, the object, the write sequence and the parameters, using only the KB prose that will be published, the tool schemas, the DB reachable by query and the persona's actual lines. Finding two solutions here, or a difference from gold, means fixing the task; a green script can never substitute for this;
- **A self-contained dossier, and propagation**: re-verify the difficulty, the load-bearing role of the world, the unique GT, the actual code output and the closure from the rationale alone; pick a revision point at random and confirm that every downstream reference has been updated.
