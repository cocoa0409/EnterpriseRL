# erl-task-policy-audit (task <-> structured policy)

Audit one difficulty's policy and task bundle: **find every problem it has, report them one by one, with re-checkable evidence.** And there it stops — you edit no artifact, and you arrange no one to fix anything. Every command is run from the enterprise_rl repository root.

## Input: four artifacts and how they relate

| Artifact | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Scenario constitution | `blueprint.yaml`, `db_schema.json`, `share_tools.py` and this category's `products/<category>.yaml` under `blueprint/` — the world's table structure, the shared tool contracts, and this category's base policies |
| Extended charter | `tasks/<difficulty>/<product>.extended.yaml`: a verbatim copy of the base section + `extended_policies`; each policy additionally carries `prerequisite`, declaring the other problems that must be resolved before this one can be handled, each edge stated by a prerequisite sentence of that policy's own rule |
| Task tool contracts | `task_tools.py` in the same directory: the signatures, owners, docstrings and runnable effects of the hidden task tools |
| M tasks | `tasks/<difficulty>/tasks/0.json ... M-1.json`, each containing `frozen_time`, `user_scenario`, `initial_state`, `evaluation_criteria`, `computations`, `solvability_contract`, `prerequisite_closure`, `charter_scope` and `required_documents` |

The relationships among the four are the entire object of this audit:

- **The extended charter is the only structured policy surface.** The base section and the extended section together are all the policy currently in force for this category; the base copy must be verbatim identical to the scenario constitution, and every deviation of extended from base is declared sentence by sentence in that policy's `overwrite`.
- **Each task is one instantiation of the policy**: `initial_state` gives a world, `frozen_time` gives the single clock, and `user_scenario` gives what the customer brings and what can be elicited from them; together the three constitute the whole situation of one transaction.
- **`evaluation_criteria` + `computations` are the scored solution**: the write-tool sequence, the owners, the order, each parameter's value, and the terminal state.
- So what must be decided is: **whether the policy is self-consistent**, and **whether, in that situation, the policy can entail only this one solution**.

## Core constraints: four relationships

| Relationship | What must be proved | Recorded when it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy self-consistency | Every conflict between base and extended is declared sentence by sentence in `overwrite`, and the merged semantics after the declaration are unique; `rule -> compute_basis -> compute` is lossless in both directions; eligibility, time, triggers, objects, cross-category reads and terminal states carry no conflict, omission or invented procedure; every prerequisite edge is stated by a prerequisite sentence of the blocked policy's own rule — in the state form unless a fallback is declared with its reason — and no rule states a cross-problem order that grounds no declared edge | `SOURCE-POLICY RED` |
| Policy <-> task unique solution | Under this task's `user_scenario`, world and scored solution, the user's need, the authorisation boundary and the timing of the transaction are clearly expressed, and the structured policy uniquely entails act-now/wait/refuse, the object, the parameters, the owner, the order and the terminal state; the agent need only act within the scope of the user's need; there is neither no solution nor several | `SOURCE-TASK RED` |
| The problem tree holds | The customer raises only the root problem, and the prerequisites the policy imposes on it are genuinely unmet at `frozen_time`; the tree has one to three prerequisite problems, at least max(n − 1, 1) `value_input` edges (n being its number of prerequisite problems), and no cycle; the existence of each prerequisite is conveyed by a prerequisite sentence of the blocked policy's own rule rather than by the customer; at least one problem of the tree is resolved by an `owner=user` tool the customer runs at the agent's hand-over; every edge bears weight; the resolving action requires no waiting; the tool layer validates no prerequisite; each scored parameter is evaluated at the moment "after all of its own prerequisites are resolved and before this problem itself is written" | `SOURCE-CHAIN RED` |
| Factors bear weight | Every computation declares four to six factors — at least 2 labelled `parameter` (the tier selects a value), at least 1 labelled `method` (the tier selects how the quantity is computed, in a way that line of business genuinely varies) — and every task's computations together carry at least 1 multi-source factor (every tier other than the fall-through a conjunction or a disjunction of two or more conditions over distinct records) and at least 1 deciding its tier from the customer's holdings or ledger in another difficulty; each factor's trigger condition is a ladder of at least three pairwise-disjoint tiers, the fall-through counted, stated in full in the policy's own `rule`, and every tier's value or method drawn from the charter; the ladder is real: no two tiers, the fall-through included, select the same value or method, every condition a tier states — each part of a compound condition taken on its own — is one that line of business actually weighs for that quantity and not one that holds of every customer who reaches this write, and every tier's value or method, the fall-through's above all, is what that business actually gives customers in that situation; each label is honest (a `method` tier changes the algorithm, a `parameter` tier a number); the policy's `baseline` is a complete standard method with default values, every sentence of it in the `rule`, from which a reader computes a definite number with no factor applied, and every factor's fall-through tier is that baseline; on this task's world each factor's `baseline` is exactly what the baseline states for that input, substituting it changes the scored value, and substituting every factor's baseline at once gives a definite fall-back number that differs from gold; the tier taken rests on seeded records, never on a field that names the outcome; the near-miss record is present and is excluded by the rule alone; and the other product's holding and ledger rows a cross-difficulty factor reads are actually in `initial_state` | `SOURCE-FACTOR RED` |

**A RED arises only from these four relationships, and its consequence must be demonstrated on the spot.** Anything that cannot be demonstrated is recorded as an observation: observations still go into the report, but they are not grounds for rework.

| Relationship | What a red light must demonstrate |
| --- | --- |
| Policy self-consistency | Quote the two passages of the artifacts verbatim and show that they reach different conclusions in the same situation |
| The problem tree holds | Point at the row in `initial_state` that makes the prerequisite already satisfied at the opening; or skip the depended-on problem's write, recompute, and show the dependent's scored value unchanged (the edge does not bear weight) |
| Factors bear weight | Substitute the factor's `baseline` bindings, recompute on the handling-time world, and show the scored value unchanged; or show that `baseline` is not what the policy's `baseline.rule` states for that input (a straw man, or the correct value under another name); or compute from `baseline.rule` alone and show it gives no definite number, or the gold number; or point at the field in `initial_state` that names the tier outcome and show the tier rests on it; or show that the other product's rows the factor reads are absent from the seed; or show two tiers of one ladder selecting the same value or method; or name a condition, or one part of a compound condition, that holds of every customer who reaches this write or that no business in that line weighs for that quantity; or name a tier's value or method, the fall-through's included, that no such business would publish as its treatment of customers in that situation |
| Policy <-> task unique solution | Write out the second reading, and the value it produces |

This aligns the threshold for a red light with the product requirements; it **does not restrict what shapes may be found**: any shape at all is a red light so long as it can demonstrate the consequence the table above demands, and it need not have appeared on any list beforehand. Conversely, a stylistic blemish, a disagreement about granularity, or a "this could have been clearer" that demonstrates no consequence is not a red light.

Take them in order: policy self-consistency first, then the problem tree, then the factors, then the unique solution. When the policy itself is contradictory, "can it uniquely entail a solution" has no determinate meaning; when the problem tree does not hold (for example, a prerequisite already satisfied at the opening), or a factor does not bear weight (its baseline gives the scored number anyway), the task is not testing what it claims to test, so even a valid unique solution belongs to a different, easier task.

The verdict rests on those four artifacts alone. **Do not erect a third set of business rules**: whether a tool accepts a business negative case, a nonzero result from some unclaimed policy, a cache diagnostic, or the auditor's personal preference — none of these changes whether the four relationships hold.

The need-versus-dialogue boundary is a necessary part of the unique solution: `user_scenario` is responsible for making clear what the customer wants, on which object, when it is to be done, whether they authorise the agent to act for them or require doing it themselves, and which outcomes they respond to only once a condition is met — and that is all; it carries no policy, no scored solution, no calculated answer, and no steering of the agent toward one correct path. The user simulator's role is to convey those needs and facts, to respond passively to the agent under explicit if/else conditions, and to execute an `owner=user` tool when the agent hands it over and asks explicitly; it may not supply policy judgements for the agent, correct calculations, or actively push a hidden solution forward.

## Output: one charter-global file, plus a per-task verdict with evidence

The artifacts land in `env/<scenario>/audits/policy_task/<difficulty>/`: one `_difficulty.md` holding the conclusions that belong to the shared charter, and one `<task_id>.md` per task. Each task file must independently answer, **for its own task**, whether the four relationships hold, **citing `_difficulty.md` by path for the charter-global conclusions instead of re-deriving them**, and for each failure give **symptom -> root cause -> evidence -> demonstrated consequence -> affected artifact and location**. The evidence must be re-checkable quotations and paths: the policy's rule sentence, the `compute` binding, the DB row path, the tool contract, the persona's lines.

**Report problems; do not design the fix.** When the policy contradicts itself, write the contradiction down as it is; never suggest "reword it and the problem goes away" — that leaves the defect in place under a new name. Likewise, never attribute a problem to the solving agent: several solutions, no solution, a real-world wait, a hidden-field dependency, a mismatch between policy and task — these are defects of the artifacts; "the tool call succeeded" does not mean the policy is correct, and a rollout agent's arithmetic slip is not a defect.

The audit unit is one difficulty: the M tasks share one extended charter and one set of tool contracts, and must jointly satisfy the difficulty-level policy conflict scan and scope exclusivity, so all M must be audited in one pass by one auditor.

**What that unit implies for the output: a conclusion about the shared charter is reached once and written once.** The extended charter, the tool module and the `prerequisite` graph are single objects, so checking them once per task yields M copies of one finding rather than M findings. Keep the finding and its consequence apart and neither is repeated nor lost: **the charter-global check is performed once, in `_difficulty.md`, while the demonstration of what that defect costs a particular task stays in that task's file** — a consequence is only ever demonstrable against one world and one scored solution, which is exactly why it cannot be hoisted, and a charter-global fact carries no world, which is exactly why it must not be repeated. A task file that restates a `_difficulty.md` conclusion instead of citing it is a defect of this audit, not thoroughness.

## Boundaries and isolation

- The write domain is only `env/<scenario>/audits/policy_task/<difficulty>/`, and nothing else: an audit does not change `blueprint/`, `tasks/` or the runtime `erlbench/` — a problem found goes into the report, and the audited artifact is not touched.
- The read domain is this scenario's blueprint, this difficulty's instantiation artifacts (under the masking division of the next section) and `erlbench/`; **no other `env/` scenario may be read**, and neither may a sibling difficulty's tasks directory.
- The auditor must be independent of the agent that wrote these artifacts; it accepts no answers supplied on its behalf, and makes no cross-difficulty merge judgements for anyone else.
- Each task is audited and written to disk separately; "another task in the same difficulty passed" never substitutes for auditing this one. The division is by what the conclusion depends on, never by convenience: **the three per-task relationships — the problem tree, the factors' weight and the unique solution — are never shared, cited or inherited between tasks**, because each rests on that task's own world and scored solution; only the charter-global halves of policy self-consistency and of the factors' structure live in `_difficulty.md`, and a task file cites them.
- Any re-audit must preserve the old revision, freezing the new derivation before appending the comparison; an old blind answer must never be rewritten to accommodate new GT.

## The source-layer view and the freezing rule

The auditor may read:

- the blueprint's product for this category, its base policies, the DB schema and the shared tool schemas;
- this difficulty's `<product>.extended.yaml` (the base section + `extended_policies`) and the tool contracts in `task_tools.py`;
- that task's `frozen_time`, `user_scenario` and `initial_state`;
- the structured policy's `rule`, `compute_basis`, `compute`, `overwrite`, `prerequisite`, and the runnable tool effects.

Until the independent derivation for that task is complete and on disk, the following must be masked or left unread:

- the whole of `evaluation_criteria`, including read/write tools, owner, order, arguments, objective, necessity and tracing;
- the whole of `computations`, including expected, factor_trace, factor_sensitivity, baseline_result, step/arg, and every gold parameter and GT sequence;
- `charter_scope`, `required_documents`, `design_rationale`, `solvability_contract`, `prerequisite_closure` and the closure fields;
- existing audit conclusions, historical revisions and any hidden meta.

The raw `initial_state` may be used as an audit oracle for the ground truth of the world, because it is precisely the policy-task closure that is being verified; but any fact used **to derive the answer** must additionally have a reachable source — a runtime share query path, a persona line, or the output of a user/agent tool that has already occurred in order and entered the shared transcript. A field visible only in the raw seed, which the agent can neither query nor elicit, remains unreachable, and does not become permissible merely because the auditor can see `initial_state`. The derivation must rest on the scenario, the DB and the executable policy that task itself declares, and must never use expected/rationale to prove itself.

**Ordering is a hard constraint: the independent derivation must be frozen to disk first, and only then may the masked GT fields be read and the comparison appended.** Reverse that order once and the blind audit of that task is void. Any early contact with GT — including reading a scored value out of the brief, out of a recursive grep, or out of a side file — must be honestly recorded in the file as `contamination_disclosure`, with that task's blind audit treated as void; concealing contamination is worse than the contamination itself, because it lets an untrustworthy blind answer pose as evidence. Recursive grep is a measured leak path: think through the path scope before running any search, and never let `tasks/*.json` fall into the match domain.

**Every task's independent derivation must be frozen to disk before any task's GT is revealed.** Auditing straight through one task — freeze, reveal, compare — and only then starting the next would let the first task's revealed conventions, formulas and parameter shapes into the later task's supposedly blind derivation, and the leak is invisible afterwards because both files look correctly ordered on their own. Freeze all M first, then reveal. `_difficulty.md` shares one file across all M tasks and is therefore the sharpest form of this path: **while any task's derivation is still unfrozen, nothing derived from a revealed GT may be written into it** — its charter-global section is built from the charter, the schema and the tool contracts alone, all of which are readable before any reveal, so this costs nothing.

## The audit procedure

### Step 0: confirm the entry conditions

The following must be supplied along with the input, and the auditor only checks and cites them rather than running the gates itself: that this difficulty's instantiation-stage gates are all green, that the masked views have been constructed per the division above (together with their hashes), and that the gate snapshot (the commands and their actual output lines) came with the brief. If any is missing, report "incomplete input" as a problem; do not run them yourself and do not lower the standard.

**A machine green light is only the qualification to enter a manual audit, not a proof of correctness.** Scripts can prove structure, references and parsability; they cannot prove that a structured policy is self-consistent across its real domain, nor that it has only one answer in this world.

### Step 1: the charter-global scan, written once, then per-task policy self-consistency

First run one conflict scan over this difficulty's complete structured policy: do all base/extended policies reach compatible conclusions on overlapping subjects, triggers, eligibility, time anchors, action parameters, terminal states and cross-category read boundaries; are the claimed scopes of the M tasks mutually exclusive and jointly complete. Write the scan into `_difficulty.md`; every affected task's audit file then **cites it by path**, and a conflict may not be ignored merely because the conflicting policy happens to belong to another task. Citing is the whole point of writing it once: an instruction to "cite" with no file to cite degenerates into re-deriving.

Then work through the seven checks below. **Where each goes is decided by what its conclusion depends on, and nothing else.**

- **Once, into `_difficulty.md`: checks 2, 3, 6 and 7.** Each interrogates a single shared object — the `rule`/`compute` correspondence across the charter's policies, the `(target_tool, computes)` claims and the uuid8s of the one tool module, the prerequisite sentences of the charter's rules, and the acyclicity and quotability of the one `prerequisite` graph. None of the four reads a task's world, so running them per task produces identical conclusions M times over. Partitioning them by which policies a task claims is no better: a coverage check split in half reports half its gaps in one file and half in another, and neither file can see the whole.
- **Check 1 splits, and the split is the general rule.** Its sentence-by-sentence override-declaration sweep runs **once** across all of the charter's extended policies in `_difficulty.md`, because base and extended are rendered into the documents together, so an undeclared override anywhere is a defect of the charter. But **the demonstration that it hands a particular task a second defensible answer belongs in that task's file** — a consequence exists only against one world and one scored solution. Finding is O(1); demonstrating is O(M).
- **Per task: checks 4 and 5.** Handling time and real-world waits are judged on that task's own world, so neither is shared and neither repeats.
- **Factors split the same way as check 1.** Their structure is charter-global and goes **once** into `_difficulty.md` under `factor_ladders`: per policy, that `factors` declares four to six factors — at least 2 `parameter`, 1 `method` — and per task that the factors of its policies include at least 1 multi-source and 1 `cross_difficulty`; that each factor's bindings are disjoint and its `decides` is an input rather than `result`, that its ladder has at least three pairwise-disjoint tiers each drawn from the charter, that no two of its tiers select the same value or method, that every condition and every outcome of every tier is one that line of business actually holds, and that the `rule` states the whole ladder in prose — every tier, its condition and its value or method — with no tier that lives only in `compute`. Whether each factor **bears weight** is a fact about one world and goes into that task's file under `factor_weight`.

The seven are set out in full below whichever file their conclusion lands in; build each task's `source_derivation_frozen` section from the per-task ones plus its citations. Check at least the following:

1. **base/extended closure and override declarations**: this guards against **two clauses in the KB fighting over the same quantity** — base and extended are rendered into the documents together, and if the value an agent computes by following the base clause differs from the scored value, the task has acquired a second defensible answer. **Declaring an override makes the override legal; it does not make several solutions legal.**

   The base section is verbatim compatible and the base copy may not be rewritten; an override holds only by being declared on the extended side. Every conflict must name, in that policy's `overwrite`, the base sentence being replaced — not naming it IS **an undeclared override**, recorded as `SOURCE-POLICY RED`; where it is named, the merged semantics after replacement must still be unique. Every base provision not hit by an `overwrite` (eligibility, authoritative evidence, time anchors, status/liability/dedup/channel boundaries) is inherited in full, and may not be weakened by extended, by a tool, or by the persona.

   Check at the granularity of **the sentences of the cited base policy**: take each entry in `base_policy_refs` and judge, sentence by sentence, whether extended follows it or handles the quantity it governs differently; the criterion is "the quantity that ultimately gets scored", not "whether a new parameter name appeared". **Reaching a conclusion on each sentence is what counts as having checked it** — an exhaustive enumeration of value combinations is not required, and "some value combination was not tried" may not be recorded as a red light. `overwrite: []` is checked by the same sentence-by-sentence conclusions, and is not held to a stricter standard for being an empty list.

   Three disguised forms need specific attention: **the anonymous intermediate factor** (the override hides in an intermediate of the calculation chain, with no new parameter name), **the 0/1 multiplier** (an existence condition multiplied onto an amount or quantity base has already fixed, zeroing it out), and **the dropped branch** (base declares a branch that extended's selector never produces). Another strong signal: the author calling base's output a "wrong value" or "the algorithm to avoid" in the rule, in `compute_basis` or in any of their own notes shows they already know they deviated from base — check whether that deviation has been declared.

2. **rule/compute equivalence in both directions**: every quantity, history window, filter, tier, branch, cross-category read, acting base, grouping of operations, cap/floor, rounding mode, rounding point and interval counting convention has a verbatim quotation in `compute_basis` and is implemented by a restricted expression; sweep back sentence by sentence to confirm that compute invents no basis of its own and that the rule makes no empty promise. Every declared factor's ladder is part of this equivalence: each tier the `factors[].ladder` names must be a sentence of the `rule` and a branch of the `decides` binding, and each branch of that binding must be a tier the rule states; a tier present in one face and absent from the other is `SOURCE-POLICY RED`.

3. **unique action binding and tool UUIDs**: each `(target_tool, computes)` is claimed by at most one policy; the policy's subject, base refs, target tool, tool docstring and terminal state admit only one business reading; no confirmation, review, wait or procedure outside the policy is added; and no docstring states an action's position in a sequence, names another tool, or carries an ordering word such as before, after or first. Check each task tool's `<verb>_<object>_<uuid8>` individually: every uuid8 must be independently newly generated for that tool and frozen with it, never reused across tools, never copied and given a different tail, and never appearing to be batch-derived from a common prefix, a sequence number or adjacent values.

4. **handling time**: every formula is evaluated on the world of "`initial_state` + the preceding synchronous writes" at its corresponding write step; a later write is never computed early, and persona, expected or a tool fallback never substitutes for an eligibility judgement.

5. **no real-world waits**: every fact a gold write consumes is an authoritative record already in `initial_state`, the real DB effect of a preceding gold write of the same task (cited through `prior_step`), or a choice the customer states immediately, and `stage_provenance` declares which. A diagnosis/visit, a delivery/call-out, a payment clearing, a manual/external approval, third-party processing, a back-office batch, a future date, a cooling-off period and the mere passage of time may never become a prerequisite of a later write if they have not completed at the opening. Creating a `completed` row, changing a status, renaming a tool or a persona line cannot fabricate a real-world event; a problem that would need one cannot be part of the tree.

6. **prerequisite sentences**: for every prerequisite edge of every policy, `policy_source` is a verbatim sentence of that policy's **own** `rule` and passes the four-point test — it joins that policy's own result to what it waits on with an explicit ordering connective (before, after, only after, only once, until, not until, first, prior to, or an equivalent ordering or timing connective); what it waits on is, in the declared `form`, a record-visible state named in the words the tables and finders use with no action, performer or tool (state form), or the depended-on action in plain business language (action form); it names no tool, no policy id, no snake_case token and no row id; and it is written at exactly the strength and scope the edge's `condition` carries (closed sets named by kind, open sets stated open). The state form has priority: an action-form edge must carry a `fallback_reason`, and the auditor tests it — where a record-visible state the depended-on write leaves would have singled out that write, the fallback is unjustified and is recorded as an observation naming that state. For a state-form edge, exactly one write of this difficulty brings the waited-on state about; where two could, the reader cannot resolve the hop, and it is `SOURCE-POLICY RED` with the two candidate writes. Prerequisite sentence + the records + the depended-on policy's own rule lead to the resolving action in exactly one reading — write that derivation out; an edge whose sentence admits a second reading, or is broader or narrower than the condition, is `SOURCE-POLICY RED`. The rule carries nothing else about that dependency: no second wording, no tool name, no other problem's id; the depended-on policy's rule does not restate the sentence. A cross-problem order stated in a rule that grounds no declared edge is an undeclared dependency, recorded as `SOURCE-POLICY RED` with the two orders and the terminal state each produces; a dependency declared in the `prerequisite` field, a docstring or `design_rationale` but stated by no sentence of the blocked rule is not a dependency the agent can discover. A **base** rule sentence that states a cross-problem order may not ground an edge: where a task's tree rests on it and the extended rule carries no prerequisite sentence of its own, it is `SOURCE-POLICY RED`; otherwise it is recorded as an observation.

7. **policy self-consistency of the `prerequisite` declarations**: every policy has filled in `prerequisite` (`[]` when there is none); every `problem` points at another policy id in this file, never at a base policy and never at itself, and the whole graph is acyclic; each edge's `policy_source` is verbatim locatable in that policy's own `rule` — if it cannot be quoted, that prerequisite is not written into the policy, the agent can never discover it, and it is recorded as `SOURCE-POLICY RED`; `form` is `state` or `action` and `fallback_reason` is present exactly when it is `action`; `condition` names the handling-time state check 6 derives and has not been quietly strengthened or weakened by the `prerequisite` field; the action `resolved_by` is the depended-on policy's `target_tool` and genuinely makes the gate described in `effect` hold or changes the binding's value; and `kind` matches `gate` (`state_gate` points at a real eligibility gate, `value_input` at a binding name that genuinely exists in this policy's compute). `prerequisite` is part of the general text, so authoring-viewpoint phrasing such as "in this task / at the opening / the current world" written into it is likewise a red light.

Conflicts, pseudo-synchronous real-world events, drifting formula bases, undeclared cross-problem orders in a rule, or undiscoverable prerequisites found in this section are recorded as **`SOURCE-POLICY RED`** whenever their consequence can be demonstrated per the "Core constraints" section, and as observations when it cannot. It is recorded where its conclusion lives — a charter-global finding in `_difficulty.md`, the demonstration of what it costs a given task in that task's file — and must carry "symptom -> root cause -> evidence -> demonstrated consequence -> affected artifact and location", and must never be written up as the agent getting the task wrong.

### Step 2: per-task independent derivation; freeze, then reveal GT and compare

Once policy self-consistency passes, still within the same frozen section, the auditor uses only the structured policy, `frozen_time`, `user_scenario`, the raw DB and the available tool schemas/synchronous effects to derive the complete solution independently:

1. Whether the user clearly expressed the target object, the timing, the boundary of the action and the mode of authorisation (the agent acting for them, the customer acting in person, a query/quote only, or performing a write); the agent's convenience may never be used to infer an authorisation the user did not express; **and separately confirm that `user_scenario` raises only the root problem and nowhere mentions or hints at any prerequisite** — if the customer names a prerequisite outright, or steers the agent toward one with "do I need to sort something out first?", the persona has short-circuited the task's problem-tree difficulty, recorded as `SOURCE-CHAIN RED`; the same holds for any other item of work the customer names — a document to send, an address to send it to, a statement to put something on — which is a new task the customer issues that `evaluation_criteria` does not contain, so an agent following `user_scenario` faithfully would have to act outside gold, and is recorded the same way; and confirm the persona closes where `evaluation_criteria` closes — a reaction that moves the agent past its last gold action is recorded the same way;
2. Whether, at `frozen_time`, the answer is act now, wait for a future condition, or refuse; every base eligibility, authoritative record, time boundary and no-wait justification;
3. The narrowest business object, the correlation keys, the history rows included and excluded, and the source objects of reversals/compensation/adjustments;
4. **Walk the problem tree yourself, starting from the root problem**: read the root problem's rule and list every prerequisite sentence it carries — what its result waits on; for each, use the raw DB to decide whether that state holds at `frozen_time`; for each that does not, find the policy whose own rule describes the write that brings it about — in the state form the sentence does not say, and you find it as the reader must, from the other policies' rules; in the action form the sentence names the action and you find the policy that performs it — and repeat the same questioning for that problem, until every condition holds. Write down the tree you derived (nodes, edges, `kind`), the topological order, and the DB row evidence for "why this is false at the opening" on each edge. The criterion here is not "how many edges the author declared" but **whether reading the policy and the world alone yields the same tree**;
5. Each scored parameter's complete formula, input rows, intermediates, branches, acting base, ordering, cap/floor, rounding and its value at handling time — **each problem's moment is after all of its own prerequisites are resolved and before it is itself written**; if the root problem would evaluate to a different number on the opening world, write both numbers down and explain why the latter is the right one; and, for every rule of that policy that decides one of its inputs by tiers, which tier this customer is on, which raw DB rows put them there (naming the other product's rows where the tier comes from another difficulty), what the baseline states for that input instead, the scored value under it, and the fall-back number the baseline alone gives for the whole computation;
6. The write-tool sequence executable on the spot, the owners, the forward and backward dependencies, the customer input and the terminal state.

Before deriving, close the context: every policy input and eligibility fact must be obtainable through a runtime share tool query, or supplied by the persona or a preceding user tool output; every value the customer states and every piece of information a real agent must ask for must already have a definite answer in the persona; and the query parameters themselves must come from earlier reachable information, while the actual output of a preceding user/agent tool becomes reachable information once the call completes. Separately check whether the user's need clearly covers the action, owner, timing and terminal state GT requires: if the user only asks for a quote while GT charges, if the user says they will submit it themselves afterwards while GT requires submitting it now, or if any other authorisation/boundary cannot be uniquely derived from the scenario, record `SOURCE-TASK RED`. The persona and the DB conflicting on the same authoritative fact, a necessary field hidden only in the seed, or the simulator having to improvise all make GT unsolvable or multiply solvable.

After the independent derivation, apply a separate set of judgements to the problem tree; any one that fails is recorded as **`SOURCE-CHAIN RED`**:

- **A prerequisite that is not in fact unmet at the opening**: some declared edge whose `condition`, judged against the raw DB at `frozen_time`, is already true. That edge is not a prerequisite at all, merely an eligibility already satisfied; and if removing it leaves the tree with no prerequisite problem at all, or with fewer `value_input` edges than max(n − 1, 1) for its remaining n, the task falls below the depth it claims. This is the most common failure of this relationship, and the one most easily masked by the wording of `design_rationale` — the criterion is the DB, never the author's account.
- **An edge that does not bear weight**: skip the write of one prerequisite problem, replay everything else unchanged, and no scored parameter or terminal field changes. It is a decorative dependency.
- **Fewer than max(n − 1, 1) `value_input` edges**, n being the number of prerequisite problems (one for a tree of one or two, two for a tree of three): with none, every edge is a `state_gate` and the tree tests ordering and never calculation; with fewer than the floor, the tree's breadth is bought with status flips rather than with values entering a calculation.
- **Two solutions on parallel edges**: one problem has several out-edges whose writes affect one another (a shared balance, a shared cap, a shared dedup key), and the policy does not exclusively pin down the order — two orders give two equally defensible terminal states.
- **A prerequisite that cannot be resolved without waiting**: the resolving action needs a real-world event that can only occur after the consultation begins (an offline payment clearing, a manual approval, a back-office batch, the date advancing). Here the correct solution can only be to wait, so the problem tree does not hold.
- **The tool layer judging the prerequisite**: a task tool refuses the call, errors, or gives a hint while the prerequisite is unresolved. That moves the agent's core decision into the environment, so the task no longer tests what it claims to. Confirm by reading each function body in `task_tools.py`.
- **A prerequisite undiscoverable from the policy surface**: an edge whose existence is written only in the `prerequisite` field or in `design_rationale`, with `policy_source` quoting no sentence of the blocked policy's own `rule`, or quoting one that names no state and no action that policy's result waits on — the agent could read every policy and still never know to do that, so the task is unsolvable for it.
- **No customer's own write in the tree**: no problem of the tree has an `owner=user` `target_tool`, or the persona never hands one over passively at the agent's direction — every task must test the hand-over of at least one write to the customer, and a tree whose every write is the agent's does not.
- **The topological order disagreeing with the GT write order**: `resolution_order`, the direction of the `prerequisite` edges, and the problem-level write order in `evaluation_criteria` contradict one another.

Then apply a further set of judgements to the factors of each computation, on the same handling-time world; any one that fails is recorded as **`SOURCE-FACTOR RED`**:

- **A factor that does not bear weight**: substitute its `baseline` bindings, replay everything else unchanged, recompute, and the scored value is unchanged. This customer sits on the factor's fall-through tier, so an agent that never finds the factor's document scores anyway; the factor is decoration, however many tiers it has.
- **A dishonest baseline**: the factor's `baseline` is not what the policy's `baseline.rule` states for that input — a straw man no reader would choose (a zero where the standard method gives a figure), or the correct value under another name; or a factor's fall-through tier is not the baseline. Compute the input from `baseline.rule` alone and compare; where that reading gives the scored value, the factor is decoration whatever `baseline` says.
- **An incoherent or unsurprising baseline**: `baseline.rule` is not self-sufficient (a reader cannot compute a definite number from it and the records without a factor), some sentence of it is not in the `rule`, its default keys are not the ones `baseline.params` names, or the number it gives on this world equals gold — an agent who reads only the computation document must arrive at a definite, wrong answer.
- **An unreal ladder**: two tiers of one ladder select the same value or the same method, so that one tier has been written as two; a condition of a tier, or one part of a compound condition, is one that line of business does not weigh when settling that quantity, or holds of every customer who reaches this write and so restates the policy's scope rather than dividing its customers; or a tier's value or method, the fall-through's above all, is a treatment that business would not publish for customers in that situation — a default no such business applies to its ordinary customer is the straw-man baseline in another form, and a ladder is judged by what that industry actually does, never by the shape the gates accept.
- **A tier read off a stamp**: the tier the ladder selects for this customer is determined by a field on the record that names the outcome, rather than by the history aggregate, date span or cross-category rows the rule describes; or such a stamp exists in the seed and agrees with the tier, so that reading it and reading the rule cannot be told apart by the score.
- **Missing or unseeded cross-difficulty evidence**: no factor of the task's computations decides its tier from another difficulty's records; or the other product's holding for this customer, or the ledger rows an `XSUM`/`XCOUNT`/`XMAX`/`XLATEST` reads, are absent from `initial_state`, so the read returns absent and the factor tests nothing about that product; or those seeded rows contradict that product's own charter.
- **No near-miss**: no record in the world is one that a careless reader would count toward a tier and the rule excludes (outside the window, through an uncounted channel, opened after the qualifying date); every factor's evidence is then either plainly present or plainly absent, and the ladder is applied by inspection rather than by reading the rule. Judge this per computation, not per factor: one genuine near-miss per computation suffices.
- **A mislabelled effect**: a factor labelled `method` whose tiers only substitute a different number into the same formula (a rate, a fee, a threshold), or a factor labelled `parameter` whose tiers change how the quantity is computed; or a `method` tier that selects a computation rule the charter nowhere states.
- **Fewer than 4 or more than 6 factors, a ladder of fewer than three tiers or with tiers that overlap as stated, fewer than 2 `parameter` or 1 `method`, or a task with no multi-source factor**, counting only rules that pass the tests above — a compute binding is not a factor, and a rule the tests strike out is not a factor either.

The tree must be recomputed problem by problem in topological order, each later write reading only the state that holds at that time; do not skip the eligibility and object derivation of any write merely because it is one atomic call. If the independent derivation yields two equally defensible answers, no feasible answer, a need for a hidden field or a future event, something incompatible with the user's surface request or authorisation boundary, or if the user simulator can only push the agent down a path by drawing on policy/GT, record **`SOURCE-TASK RED`**.

The frozen section must list the policy rule sentences, DB row paths, tool contracts and persona-line evidence actually used, and may cite no masked field. **Only after the freeze is on disk** may the full `evaluation_criteria`, `computations` and the remaining GT fields be revealed and compared item by item in `source_gt_comparison`: the user's need/authorisation/timing, the necessary read set, now/wait/refuse, eligibility, the object, each parameter, tool/owner/order, and the terminal state. The closest alternative candidate must be excluded by explicit policy/world evidence — "GT chose this one" is not exclusion evidence.

An independent derivation that differs from GT is still **`SOURCE-TASK RED`**, not a shortcoming of the model.

### Step 3: settle the problems one by one

The auditor produces only verdicts and evidence, **edits no artifact**, and arranges no fix. Two disciplines may not be crossed:

- **Never attribute to the solving agent.** Several solutions, no solution, a real-world wait, a hidden-field dependency, a mismatch between policy and task — all are task defects; "the tool call succeeded" does not mean the policy is correct, and a rollout agent's arithmetic slip is not a red light against the task.
- **Report problems only; do not design the fix.** When the structured policy contradicts itself, write the contradiction down as it is; never propose "reword it and it goes away" — that leaves the defect in place under a new name.

**A red light turns on whether an assertion is true, not on who can read it.** An audit verifies whether the proof the artifact submits holds, not whether re-solving the task reaches the same answer — reaching the same conclusion does not mean the proof holds. So every sentence in `design_rationale`, in the various closures, in `factor_trace` and in `argument_uniqueness_tracing` that **asserts what the world is like** (a number, the value of a field in a row, the figures from skipping a step) is part of the proof: verify it word for word, recompute it on the world of the moment it names for itself, and where it contradicts the recomputation, red-light it under the relationship it supports. Every sentence that **expresses what the author thinks** (wording, granularity, an assessment of the difficulty or the design) changes no relationship and is recorded as an observation. That the auditor has already worked out the right answer is what makes it competent to rule, not a reason for exemption.

Every problem states: the verdict, the symptom, the root cause, the re-checkable evidence, the demonstrated consequence (per the table in "Core constraints"), the affected artifact and location (policy / compute / seed row / persona / tool / which parts of the scored solution), and its relation to the previous revision.

### Step 4: closing out the difficulty

Once all M tasks have a frozen `source_derivation_frozen` and a `source_gt_comparison`, and all four relationships PASS, close out the difficulty, writing the checklist below into `_difficulty.md` under its own heading. It is the one difficulty-level conclusion reached **after** the reveals, so it sits below the charter-global section rather than inside it — that section is pre-reveal by construction and must stay that way:

- the per-task audit files correspond one-to-one with the tasks and `_difficulty.md` sits alongside them, old revisions are traceable, and every comparison section carries a PASS;
- this difficulty's extended policies are fully covered, the M tasks' scopes are pairwise disjoint (a scope spans the whole problem tree, the root problem and all of its prerequisite problems), task tools and GT are closed, and each task tool uses an independently generated, frozen uuid8;
- every task's problem tree has one to three prerequisite problems, at least max(n − 1, 1) `value_input` edges, at least one problem resolved by an `owner=user` tool, and no cycle; and the `prerequisite` edges of the whole difficulty together are still a directed acyclic graph, with no cycle created by cross-task references;
- every computation of every task declares a self-sufficient baseline and four to six factors (2 `parameter` and 1 `method`; per task at least 1 multi-source and 1 cross-difficulty), every ladder of at least three tiers stated in full in its rule, its tiers distinct in outcome and its conditions and outcomes ones that line of business actually holds, and every factor proved to bear weight on its task's world against an honest baseline, with the baseline alone giving a definite number different from gold;
- this difficulty's instantiation-stage gates are re-run once on the final form.

The closing output takes the form of **the per-task verdicts on all four relationships + the difficulty's closing checklist in `_difficulty.md`**. The cross-difficulty merge surface and the scenario-level check do not belong to this skill — do not run them, and do not rewrite your own verdicts on the strength of their results.

Only when "policy self-consistency PASS + problem tree holds PASS + factors bear weight PASS + unique solution PASS" hold together does this difficulty pass the source-layer audit.

Across rounds, append under `## revision_N`, leaving the text of older revisions untouched.

A re-audit re-derives a charter-global section only when its shared inputs can have moved it. Compare the `<product>.extended.yaml` and `task_tools.py` hashes against the round that produced the section: where both are unchanged, carry the section forward by reference and record the two hashes as the grounds for doing so; where either has changed, re-derive it in full. Each task's per-task sections are judged the same way against its own task file. This is a rule about re-deriving, not about re-reading: a rework can move a task's proof fields while the charter stands still, and those are per-task.

## Definition of done

This skill is complete not when "some number of reports have been written", but when every task of this difficulty can be answered with evidence on record: the structured policy is self-consistent and every prerequisite is stated by a prerequisite sentence in the blocked policy's own rule, in the state form unless a fallback is justified; the customer raises only the root problem while the prerequisites the policy imposes are genuinely unmet at the opening, the problem tree can be derived independently from the policy and the world, at least one of its problems is the customer's own write, and every edge bears weight; every computation rests on a self-sufficient baseline and four to six declared factors — over the task's computations at least one multi-source and at least one reading another difficulty's seeded records — every ladder's tiers distinct in outcome and its conditions and outcomes ones that line of business actually holds, and every factor bears weight against an honest baseline; and it uniquely entails GT under that scenario/DB. Any of the four that fails is reported honestly, is not blamed on the rollout agent, and is not papered over by the audit report adding business steps or calculations of its own.
